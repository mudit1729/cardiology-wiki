# OCT Procedure

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) uses near-infrared light interferometry to generate ultra-high-resolution cross-sectional images of coronary arteries. With 10-15 micron axial resolution (10x better than IVUS), OCT excels at detailed assessment of plaque morphology, stent apposition, and tissue characterization.

## Indications

- Stent optimization: apposition, expansion, edge dissection, tissue coverage
- Assessment of stent failure: thrombosis, restenosis, neoatherosclerosis
- Plaque characterization: thin-cap fibroatheroma (TCFA), erosion, rupture, calcified nodule
- ACS mechanism identification (plaque rupture vs. erosion vs. calcified nodule)
- Calcium characterization for modification strategy planning
- Guidance for bioresorbable scaffold implantation

## Contraindications

- Ostial right coronary or ostial left main lesions (inability to clear blood adequately)
- Severe renal insufficiency (contrast flush required: 15-30 mL per pullback)
- Large vessel diameter >5 mm (limited penetration depth of OCT)
- Hemodynamic instability (transient ischemia during contrast flush)
- Severe aorto-ostial stenosis precluding adequate flushing

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Select OCT system: Abbott OPTIS (Dragonfly catheter) or Terumo OFDI (Lunawave)
- Ensure 6Fr guide catheter with adequate engagement for contrast flush
- Prepare contrast or dextran flush medium
- Plan pullback length (standard 54 mm or 75 mm pullback available)
- Assess renal function (cumulative contrast load consideration)

## Key Anatomy

- OCT resolution: axial 10-15 microns, lateral 20-40 microns
- Penetration depth: 1-2 mm (limited compared to IVUS 4-8 mm)
- Three-layer visualization with exquisite intimal detail
- Lipid plaque: signal-poor with diffuse borders
- Fibrous plaque: signal-rich, homogeneous
- Calcium: signal-poor with sharp, well-demarcated borders (can measure thickness)
- Red thrombus: high-backscattering, protruding; white thrombus: low-backscattering

## Equipment

- OCT console: Abbott OPTIS Integrated System or Terumo Lunawave
- OCT catheter: Dragonfly OPTIS (Abbott) or FastView (Terumo); 2.7Fr crossing profile
- Contrast flush: iodinated contrast (undiluted or 50:50 with saline) or dextran
- Power injector or manual injection (recommended 4 mL/sec for 3-4 seconds)
- Compatible with 6Fr guide catheter (standard)

## Technique

1. Advance OCT catheter over guidewire beyond the distal edge of target lesion
2. Position catheter tip in distal reference segment
3. Connect flush line to guide catheter (power injector preferred for consistent flush)
4. Calibrate OCT system; initiate pullback
5. Deliver contrast flush (14 mL at 4 mL/sec through guide) simultaneously with pullback trigger
6. Automatic pullback at 20-40 mm/sec captures 54-75 mm segment
7. Review pullback: assess lumen dimensions, plaque morphology, calcium, thrombus
8. Post-PCI OCT: evaluate stent expansion, apposition, edge dissection

**OCT Criteria for Stent Optimization:**
- Minimum stent area >= 80% of reference lumen area (or >= 4.5 mm2 in proximal LAD)
- No malapposition: stent struts within 200 microns of vessel wall
- No significant edge dissection: flap >200 microns with >60 degree arc
- No tissue protrusion at risk for thrombosis
- No uncovered stent struts at critical points

## Complications

- Transient ischemia during contrast flush (seconds; well-tolerated)
- Ventricular arrhythmia during RCA imaging (rare; due to transient ischemia)
- Additional contrast load (15-30 mL per pullback)
- Air embolism (rare; careful purging essential)
- Vessel dissection (extremely rare)

## India-Specific Considerations

- OCT availability more limited than IVUS in India; concentrated in academic and high-volume centers
- Cost per OCT catheter: INR 30,000-50,000 (higher than IVUS)
- Additional contrast volume is a concern in diabetic nephropathy patients (prevalent in India)
- OCT excels for stent failure assessment; valuable for understanding in-stent restenosis and thrombosis
- Growing Indian research contributions in OCT-based plaque characterization
- Training via hands-on workshops at NIC, CSI, and manufacturer-sponsored programs
