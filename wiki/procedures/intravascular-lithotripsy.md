# Intravascular Lithotripsy

Intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) uses pulsatile sonic pressure waves to fracture both superficial and deep coronary calcium, enabling vessel compliance recovery for optimal stent expansion. The Shockwave C2 system delivers localized, circumferential energy through a balloon-based catheter platform.

## Indications

- Severely calcified coronary lesions requiring modification before stenting
- Lesions with both superficial and deep calcium (unique advantage over atherectomy)
- Nodular calcified lesions
- Calcified lesions in tortuous vessels (where rotablation is challenging)
- Calcified in-stent restenosis (select cases)
- Large vessel calcification (up to 4.0 mm IVL catheter available)

## Contraindications

- Inability to cross lesion with IVL catheter (requires pre-dilation to enable crossing)
- Active coronary perforation or dissection
- Vessel diameter outside catheter sizing range (<2.5 mm or >4.0 mm)
- Extremely eccentric calcium with minimal calcium coverage (limited benefit)
- Lesion length exceeding practical treatment range

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- IVUS or OCT to characterize calcium: arc (>270 degrees responds best), depth, length
- Assess ability to cross lesion with low-profile balloon (IVL catheter crossing profile is larger)
- Select IVL catheter size based on vessel reference diameter (2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 mm)
- Plan treatment zones: each catheter position treats approximately 12 mm segment
- Calculate total number of pulses needed (80 pulses per catheter maximum)

## Key Anatomy

- IVL is effective for both intimal (superficial) and medial (deep) calcium
- Circumferential calcium (>270 degrees arc) responds best to IVL
- Nodular calcium can be modified with IVL (advantage over scoring balloons)
- Calcium thickness: IVL penetrates deeper than rotational atherectomy
- Assess proximal vessel tortuosity for catheter deliverability

## Equipment

- Shockwave C2 IVL catheter (2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 mm diameters)
- Shockwave generator and connector cable
- Standard PCI equipment: guide catheter (6Fr compatible), guidewire, balloons
- Intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT) for pre/post assessment
- Each catheter contains two emitters; generates circumferential sonic waves

## Technique

1. Cross calcified lesion with guidewire; predilate with small balloon if needed
2. Advance IVL catheter to target lesion segment
3. Inflate IVL balloon to 4 atm (low pressure to appose emitters to vessel wall)
4. Deliver 10 pulses per cycle at each position (each pulse: 1 microsecond duration)
5. After 10 pulses: inflate balloon to 6 atm to assess compliance improvement
6. Reposition catheter and repeat for additional calcified segments
7. Maximum 80 pulses per catheter (8 cycles of 10 pulses)
8. After IVL: post-dilate with NC balloon to full vessel diameter
9. Deploy DES with target expansion
10. Confirm result with intravascular imaging: look for calcium fractures and stent expansion

## Complications

- Transient bradycardia during pulse delivery (electrical interference; resolves spontaneously)
- Coronary dissection (usually non-flow limiting; sealed by stenting)
- Vessel perforation (rare; <1%)
- No reflow (rare compared to atherectomy; IVL generates larger particles)
- Incomplete calcium modification (may need adjunctive balloon or atherectomy)
- Balloon rupture (rare; deliver pulses only at 4 atm)

## India-Specific Considerations

- IVL availability expanding across major Indian centers since 2022 regulatory approval
- Cost per IVL catheter: INR 1.5-2.5 lakh, making it the most expensive calcium modification tool
- Simpler technique compared to rotablation; shorter learning curve for operators
- No need for temporary pacemaker (advantage over rotablation for RCA lesions)
- 6Fr guide catheter compatibility is a practical advantage
- Cost-effectiveness studies comparing IVL vs. rotablation needed in Indian context
- Growing Indian operator experience through early adoption programs and proctorships
