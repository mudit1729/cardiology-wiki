# In-Stent Restenosis Management

In-stent restenosis (ISR) is the re-narrowing of a previously stented coronary segment due to neointimal hyperplasia or neoatherosclerosis. It occurs in 5-10% of drug-eluting stents and remains a significant clinical challenge requiring tailored treatment strategies based on pattern and mechanism.

## Indications

- Symptomatic ISR with angina or objective ischemia (stress test or FFR/iFR)
- Angiographically significant ISR (>70% diameter stenosis within stent)
- ACS presentation with ISR as culprit lesion
- Hemodynamically significant ISR confirmed by FFR <= 0.80 or iFR <= 0.89

## Contraindications

- Asymptomatic ISR without objective ischemia (medical therapy preferred)
- Diffuse ISR in small vessels (<2.0 mm) not amenable to intervention
- Multiple prior recurrent ISR at same site (consider CABG)
- Patient unable to tolerate additional DAPT course

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Classify ISR pattern (Mehran classification):
  - Type I: Focal (<10 mm; best prognosis)
  - Type II: Diffuse (>10 mm within stent)
  - Type III: Proliferative (>10 mm extending beyond stent edges)
  - Type IV: Total occlusion
- Intravascular imaging (OCT preferred) to determine mechanism:
  - Neointimal hyperplasia: smooth, homogeneous tissue
  - Neoatherosclerosis: lipid-rich neointima, thin-cap fibroatheroma within stent
  - Mechanical issues: underexpansion, fracture, malapposition
- Review prior stent details: type (BMS vs. DES), size, era of implantation

## Key Anatomy

- Previously stented coronary segment with neointimal tissue growth
- Assess stent integrity: fracture, crush, overlap zones
- Reference vessel size at edges of stented segment
- Disease progression beyond stent edges

## Equipment

- Drug-coated balloon (DCB): SeQuent Please NEO, Agent, Elutax SV
- Drug-eluting stent (DES): if DCB not suitable or stent underexpansion present
- Non-compliant balloons for lesion preparation and post-dilation
- Scoring/cutting balloons for neointimal tissue modification
- Intravascular imaging: OCT (preferred for ISR) or IVUS
- Rotational atherectomy (for calcified ISR or neoatherosclerosis)

## Technique

**Drug-Coated Balloon (Preferred for Most ISR):**
1. Cross ISR lesion with guidewire
2. Perform intravascular imaging (OCT) to assess mechanism and stent status
3. Address mechanical issues first (underexpansion: high-pressure NC balloon)
4. Prepare lesion: scoring balloon or NC balloon at high pressure
5. Select DCB sized 1:1 to reference vessel diameter
6. Inflate DCB at nominal pressure for 60 seconds (paclitaxel-coated)
7. Confirm result with intravascular imaging

**Repeat DES Implantation:**
1. Indicated when: stent underexpansion corrected but residual stenosis, or edge ISR
2. Select different DES platform/drug than original stent if possible
3. Ensure adequate stent expansion with imaging guidance

**Address Underlying Causes:**
- Stent underexpansion: high-pressure NC balloon, IVL, or rotablation
- Stent fracture: additional stent coverage
- Neoatherosclerosis: DCB or new DES with lesion preparation
- Geographic miss: extend stent coverage to diseased segment

## Complications

- Recurrent ISR (15-20% after DCB treatment; higher for repeat DES)
- Coronary dissection at stent edges
- Stent fracture propagation during aggressive dilation
- Distal embolization from neoatherosclerotic plaque
- Perforation (rare with appropriate sizing)

## India-Specific Considerations

- ISR rates may be higher in India due to historical use of older-generation DES and BMS
- DCB availability in India has improved significantly; multiple brands available
- DCB cost: INR 20,000-35,000; cost-effective alternative to repeat DES in suitable lesions
- Indian-made DCBs increasingly available and competitive in quality
- OCT-guided ISR assessment helps identify mechanism and guide therapy
- High diabetes prevalence in India contributes to higher ISR rates
- Patient counseling on modifiable risk factors essential to prevent recurrent ISR
