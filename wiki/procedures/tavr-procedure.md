# TAVR Procedure

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) is the percutaneous implantation of a bioprosthetic aortic valve via catheter-based delivery systems, avoiding open-heart surgery. Originally developed for inoperable patients, TAVR has expanded to all surgical risk categories and is now the dominant treatment for severe symptomatic aortic stenosis.

## Indications

- Severe symptomatic aortic stenosis (AVA <1.0 cm2, mean gradient >40 mmHg, Vmax >4.0 m/s)
- High or prohibitive surgical risk (STS score >8% or frailty/comorbidities)
- Intermediate surgical risk (STS 4-8%): shared decision-making
- Low surgical risk (STS <4%): approved based on PARTNER 3 and Evolut Low Risk trials
- Symptomatic severe AS in patients >= 65-70 years (age consideration for durability)
- Degenerated surgical bioprosthetic valve (valve-in-valve TAVR)
- Pure aortic regurgitation (emerging indication with newer-generation devices)

## Contraindications

- Inadequate annular size outside device range (<18 mm or >30 mm depending on device)
- Active endocarditis
- Bicuspid aortic valve (relative; improving with experience and newer devices)
- LV thrombus
- Iliofemoral arteries too small or too diseased for transfemoral access (consider alternative access)
- Life expectancy <1 year from non-cardiac causes
- Severe coronary artery disease requiring CABG (relative; staged PCI may be alternative)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Cardiac CT (gated): annular sizing (perimeter, area, diameters), calcium distribution, coronary height, sinus dimensions, LVOT anatomy
- Peripheral CT angiography: iliofemoral access assessment (minimum diameter, tortuosity, calcification)
- Echocardiography: valve hemodynamics, LV function, concomitant valve disease
- Coronary angiography: address significant coronary disease before or during TAVR
- Heart Team discussion: interventional cardiologist, cardiac surgeon, imaging specialist
- Frailty assessment: 6-minute walk test, grip strength, gait speed
- Select valve type and size based on CT measurements

## Key Anatomy

- Aortic annulus: virtual ring at the nadirs of the three aortic cusps (CT measurement plane)
- Annular perimeter determines valve size (manufacturer sizing charts)
- Coronary ostia height from annulus: LCA (12-16 mm), RCA (14-19 mm); low coronary height increases risk
- Sinus of Valsalva dimensions: adequate space for coronary perfusion after deployment
- LVOT calcium: increases risk of paravalvular leak and annular rupture
- Membranous septum length: predicts conduction disturbance risk

## Equipment

- Self-expanding valve: Medtronic Evolut PRO+, Evolut FX
- Balloon-expandable valve: Edwards SAPIEN 3, SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA
- Delivery systems: 14Fr (Evolut) or 14-16Fr (SAPIEN) expandable sheaths
- Pre-dilation balloon: standard or undersized balloon valvuloplasty
- Rapid ventricular pacing wire (for balloon-expandable deployment)
- Large-bore femoral access equipment and closure devices (Perclose ProGlide)

## Technique

**Transfemoral TAVR (Standard Approach):**
1. General anesthesia or conscious sedation (minimalist approach gaining popularity)
2. Obtain bilateral femoral arterial access (main access + contralateral pigtail for angiography)
3. Pre-close main access site with two Perclose ProGlide devices
4. Insert large-bore introducer sheath (14-16Fr expandable sheath)
5. Cross aortic valve with guidewire (AL1 catheter + straight wire, exchange to stiff wire)
6. Optional: balloon aortic valvuloplasty for pre-dilation
7. Advance valve delivery system across native valve
8. **Balloon-expandable (SAPIEN):** rapid ventricular pacing (180-200 bpm), deploy by balloon inflation
9. **Self-expanding (Evolut):** gradual deployment by sheath retraction; can recapture/reposition
10. Post-deployment: aortography and hemodynamics (assess paravalvular leak, gradients)
11. Remove delivery system; deploy Perclose sutures for hemostasis
12. Post-procedure echocardiography: valve function, paravalvular leak, pericardial effusion

## Complications

- Paravalvular leak (5-15%; mild acceptable; moderate-severe may require further intervention)
- Conduction disturbance requiring permanent pacemaker (10-25%; higher with self-expanding)
- Stroke (1-3%)
- Vascular access complications (5-10%)
- Coronary obstruction (<1%; catastrophic; more common in valve-in-valve)
- Annular rupture (<1%; associated with aggressive oversizing or heavy LVOT calcium)
- Valve embolization (<1%)
- Acute kidney injury (2-5%)

## India-Specific Considerations

- TAVR volume in India growing rapidly but remains limited to high-volume centers
- Cost: INR 15-25 lakh (valve + procedure); significantly more than SAVR (INR 3-6 lakh)
- Indian-made TAVR valves under development; may dramatically reduce costs
- Bicuspid aortic valve prevalence in younger Indian patients complicates TAVR selection
- Rheumatic aortic stenosis (younger patients) may not be ideal for TAVR due to durability concerns
- Minimalist TAVR (conscious sedation, next-day discharge) reducing costs and resource utilization
- Training: dedicated structural heart fellowships and proctored programs expanding
- Government insurance schemes generally do not cover TAVR; out-of-pocket expense for most patients
