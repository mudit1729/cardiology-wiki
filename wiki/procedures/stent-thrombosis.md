# Stent Thrombosis Management

Stent thrombosis (ST) is the acute thrombotic occlusion of a previously implanted coronary stent, representing a catastrophic complication with high mortality (20-40%). It typically presents as STEMI or sudden cardiac death and requires emergent intervention and thorough investigation of the underlying cause.

## Indications

- Definite stent thrombosis: angiographic confirmation of thrombus within or adjacent to stent
- Probable stent thrombosis: unexplained death within 30 days of stenting or acute MI in stent territory
- Classification by timing: acute (<24 hrs), subacute (1-30 days), late (1-12 months), very late (>12 months)

## Contraindications

- No specific contraindications to treating stent thrombosis (it is an emergency)
- Usual contraindications to PCI apply but are often overridden by emergency setting

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Treat as STEMI equivalent: emergent cath lab activation
- Assess antiplatelet compliance history (most common cause of early ST)
- Review prior stent implantation: type, size, post-dilation, imaging guidance
- Prepare for high thrombus burden: aspiration catheter, GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors
- Plan intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT) to identify underlying mechanism
- Consider mechanical support if hemodynamically unstable

## Key Anatomy

- Previously stented coronary segment with acute thrombotic occlusion
- Assess for stent underexpansion, malapposition, or edge dissection (causes of ST)
- Evaluate proximal and distal reference segments for disease progression
- Neoatherosclerosis as cause of very late ST (lipid-rich neointima within stent)

## Equipment

- Standard PCI equipment: guide catheter, guidewires, balloons
- Aspiration thrombectomy catheter
- GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors: abciximab, eptifibatide, or tirofiban
- Intravascular imaging: IVUS or OCT (essential to identify mechanism)
- Additional stents if needed for mechanical issues
- Drug-coated balloon for in-stent restenosis component

## Technique

1. Obtain emergent arterial access; engage culprit vessel with guide catheter
2. Cross thrombotic occlusion with guidewire (often easy; fresh thrombus is soft)
3. Consider initial aspiration thrombectomy to reduce thrombus burden
4. Restore TIMI 3 flow with balloon angioplasty (NC balloon to avoid further injury)
5. Administer GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor (IC or IV bolus + infusion)
6. **Perform intravascular imaging (IVUS or OCT) to identify underlying mechanism:**
   - Stent underexpansion: most common mechanical cause
   - Stent malapposition: incomplete wall apposition
   - Edge dissection: uncovered dissection at stent margins
   - Neoatherosclerosis: very late ST mechanism
   - Geographic miss: uncovered disease segment
7. Treat identified mechanism:
   - Underexpansion: high-pressure NC balloon dilation or IVL
   - Malapposition: post-dilation to achieve apposition
   - Edge dissection: additional stent coverage
   - Neoatherosclerosis: drug-coated balloon or additional DES
8. Optimize result with intravascular imaging guidance
9. Ensure adequate antiplatelet therapy: dual antiplatelet with potent P2Y12 inhibitor

## Complications

- Distal embolization and no-reflow (common with high thrombus burden)
- Cardiogenic shock from large MI territory
- Recurrent stent thrombosis (address underlying cause)
- Bleeding complications from aggressive antithrombotic therapy
- Arrhythmias (VT/VF) during acute presentation

## Key Trials

- [[ADAPT-DES]] - Predictors of stent thrombosis after DES implantation
- [[PARIS]] - Antiplatelet interruption and stent thrombosis risk
- [[DAPT]] - Prolonged DAPT reduces very late stent thrombosis
- [[HOST-EXAM]] - DAPT duration and stent thrombosis outcomes

## India-Specific Considerations

- Antiplatelet non-compliance is a leading cause of ST in India (cost and awareness barriers)
- Generic clopidogrel resistance reported to be higher in some South Asian populations
- Prasugrel and ticagrelor availability improving but cost remains a barrier for some patients
- Delayed presentation to cath lab for acute ST worsens outcomes
- Intravascular imaging for ST mechanism assessment remains underutilized
- Patient education on DAPT compliance is essential; structured counseling programs needed
- Government drug price control on antiplatelet agents has improved affordability
