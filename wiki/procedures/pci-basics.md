# PCI Basics

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is the cornerstone of interventional cardiology, involving balloon angioplasty and stent implantation to restore coronary blood flow. Modern PCI is predominantly performed using drug-eluting stents (DES) with radial access and intravascular imaging guidance.

## Indications

- ST-elevation myocardial infarction (primary PCI within 120 minutes)
- Non-ST-elevation ACS with high-risk features
- Stable coronary artery disease with significant ischemia and/or symptoms refractory to medical therapy
- Hemodynamically significant stenosis (FFR <= 0.80 or iFR <= 0.89)
- Angiographically significant lesions (>70% diameter stenosis, >50% for left main)

## Contraindications

- Lesion not amenable to PCI (e.g., diffuse disease better suited for CABG)
- Patient unable to tolerate DAPT (high bleeding risk without alternatives)
- No ischemic symptoms and no objective evidence of ischemia (for stable CAD)
- Terminal illness with limited life expectancy
- Active systemic infection

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Review coronary anatomy from diagnostic angiography or CCTA
- Calculate SYNTAX score for complex multivessel disease (Heart Team discussion)
- Plan stent sizing using QCA or intravascular imaging
- Ensure adequate antiplatelet loading (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor)
- Assess renal function; contrast minimization strategy for CKD patients
- Brief patient on risks, benefits, and alternatives including CABG

## Key Anatomy

- Understanding of coronary segments per AHA/ACC classification (15-segment model)
- Bifurcation anatomy: main branch, side branch, carina
- Ostial lesion anatomy and implications for stent positioning
- Collateral circulation pathways (Rentrop classification)
- Relationship of coronary arteries to cardiac chambers and valves

## Equipment

- Guide catheters: 6Fr or 7Fr (EBU, XB, JL, JR, Amplatz)
- Coronary guidewires: workhorse (BMW, Runthrough), polymer-jacketed, specialty wires
- Balloon catheters: semi-compliant (predilation), non-compliant (post-dilation)
- Drug-eluting stents: everolimus, zotarolimus, sirolimus-eluting platforms
- Adjunctive devices: aspiration catheters, cutting/scoring balloons

## Technique

1. Obtain arterial access; engage coronary ostium with guide catheter
2. Cross lesion with workhorse guidewire
3. Predilate with semi-compliant balloon (1:1 balloon-to-vessel ratio)
4. Size vessel with intravascular imaging (IVUS or OCT) when possible
5. Select stent based on vessel diameter and lesion length
6. Deploy stent at nominal pressure; post-dilate with non-compliant balloon
7. Confirm result with angiography and/or intravascular imaging
8. Optimize stent result: full expansion, good apposition, no edge dissection
9. Ensure TIMI 3 flow and no distal complications
10. Remove equipment; achieve hemostasis at access site

## Complications

- Coronary dissection (managed with additional stenting)
- Distal embolization and no-reflow phenomenon
- Side branch occlusion (especially at bifurcation lesions)
- Stent thrombosis (acute, subacute, late, very late)
- Coronary perforation (wire, balloon, or stent-related)
- Access site complications (see [[radial-access]] and [[femoral-access]])
- Contrast-induced nephropathy

## India-Specific Considerations

- Indian-made DES (Supraflex, Yukon, BioMatrix) are cost-effective alternatives (INR 15,000-25,000 vs. INR 40,000-70,000 for imported stents)
- NPPA price capping on coronary stents has improved affordability
- Radial-first approach increasingly standard; imaging-guided PCI adoption growing
- High prevalence of diabetes and small vessel disease in Indian patients affects outcomes
- Primary PCI networks for STEMI improving but geographic disparities remain significant
- Many patients present late due to pre-hospital delays, affecting outcomes
