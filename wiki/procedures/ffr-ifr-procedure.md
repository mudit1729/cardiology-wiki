# FFR/iFR Procedure

Fractional flow reserve (FFR) and instantaneous wave-free ratio (iFR) are invasive coronary physiological indices that assess the hemodynamic significance of coronary stenoses. They provide objective evidence of lesion-specific ischemia, guiding revascularization decisions beyond angiographic appearance alone.

## Indications

- Angiographically intermediate stenosis (40-70% diameter stenosis) in stable CAD
- Multivessel disease: determine which lesions require revascularization
- Serial lesions: assess contribution of individual stenoses to ischemia
- Post-PCI assessment: confirm physiological adequacy of result
- Discordant non-invasive testing and angiographic findings
- Left main assessment when stenosis severity is ambiguous

## Contraindications

- STEMI (culprit vessel assessment inappropriate; FFR unreliable in acute MI)
- Severe aortic stenosis (affects pressure measurements)
- Severe microvascular dysfunction (may overestimate FFR)
- Contraindication to adenosine (for FFR): severe asthma, high-grade AV block, theophylline use
- Hemodynamic instability or significant arrhythmia
- Very distal or diffuse disease (wire position affects measurement)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Hold caffeine for 12 hours before FFR (caffeine antagonizes adenosine)
- Hold theophylline / aminophylline for 24 hours
- Ensure adequate hemodynamic stability before measurement
- Calibrate pressure wire system; ensure aortic pressure transducer accuracy
- Plan hyperemic agent: IV adenosine (standard) or IC adenosine (faster, lower cost)

## Key Anatomy

- FFR/iFR measures pressure gradient across a specific stenosis
- Wire tip (pressure sensor) positioned distal to lesion; guide catheter at ostium
- Serial lesions require pullback assessment or individual lesion evaluation
- Diffuse disease may show gradual pressure drop along entire vessel
- Left main assessment: sensor in both LAD and LCx (use lower FFR value)

## Equipment

- Pressure guidewire: Abbott PressureWire X (FFR + iFR), Philips Verrata (FFR only)
- Pressure console: Abbott CoroFlow (displays FFR, iFR, RFR), Philips Volcano
- IV adenosine infusion pump (140 mcg/kg/min through central or large peripheral vein)
- IC adenosine syringes (100-200 mcg for LCA, 50-100 mcg for RCA)
- Pressure transducer and manifold system (zeroed and calibrated)

## Technique

**FFR Measurement:**
1. Calibrate and equalize pressure wire with aortic pressure (normalize at catheter tip)
2. Advance pressure wire distal to target lesion (at least 20 mm beyond)
3. Confirm pressure equalization; check for drift
4. Induce maximal hyperemia: IV adenosine 140 mcg/kg/min or IC adenosine bolus
5. Record Pd/Pa during steady-state hyperemia (for IV) or at nadir (for IC)
6. FFR = Pd/Pa during maximal hyperemia
7. FFR <= 0.80: hemodynamically significant; consider revascularization
8. FFR > 0.80: defer revascularization; medical therapy

**iFR Measurement:**
1. Same wire positioning as FFR
2. iFR measured at rest during wave-free period of diastole (no hyperemia needed)
3. Automatic calculation over 5 or more cardiac cycles
4. iFR <= 0.89: hemodynamically significant; consider revascularization
5. iFR > 0.89: defer revascularization; medical therapy

**Pressure Wire Pullback:**
1. During hyperemia (FFR) or at rest (iFR), slowly pull wire back through vessel
2. Identify focal pressure drops (focal lesion) vs. gradual decline (diffuse disease)
3. Helps plan targeted PCI for maximum physiological gain

## Complications

- Adenosine side effects: transient chest pain, dyspnea, flushing, AV block (seconds; resolves with stopping infusion)
- Coronary spasm from wire manipulation (<1%)
- Pressure wire-induced dissection (extremely rare)
- Pressure drift leading to inaccurate readings (check drift at end of pullback)

## India-Specific Considerations

- Physiology-guided PCI adoption increasing but still underutilized in India
- Pressure wire cost: INR 30,000-50,000 per wire limits routine use
- IC adenosine bolus method preferred in India (lower cost, no infusion pump needed)
- iFR gaining popularity due to no hyperemia requirement (saves time and adenosine cost)
- Cost-effectiveness of FFR/iFR demonstrated by avoiding unnecessary stenting
- Growing emphasis on physiology-guided PCI in Indian training programs
- Reuse of pressure wires practiced in some centers (manufacturer does not recommend)
