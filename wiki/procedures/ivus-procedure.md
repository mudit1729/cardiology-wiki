# IVUS Procedure

Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) provides cross-sectional tomographic imaging of the coronary artery wall and lumen using a miniaturized ultrasound transducer mounted on a catheter. It is the cornerstone of imaging-guided PCI, enabling accurate lesion assessment, stent sizing, and optimization.

## Indications

- Left main PCI (mandatory per guidelines)
- Complex PCI: bifurcation, CTO, long lesions, small vessels
- Ambiguous angiographic findings (intermediate stenosis)
- Assessment of stent optimization: expansion, apposition, edge dissection
- Characterization of plaque morphology and composition
- Assessment of disease progression or transplant vasculopathy

## Contraindications

- Inability to cross lesion with IVUS catheter (pre-dilate first)
- Severe coronary spasm unresponsive to vasodilators
- Extremely tortuous vessels precluding catheter advancement (relative)
- Hemodynamic instability precluding additional procedural time (relative)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Ensure adequate guide catheter size (6Fr minimum for most IVUS catheters)
- Select IVUS system: mechanical rotational (iLab/OptiCross) or solid-state phased array (Volcano Eagle Eye)
- Prepare flush solution and IVUS console
- Plan pullback length to cover target lesion plus reference segments

## Key Anatomy

- IVUS resolution: axial ~100 microns, lateral ~200 microns
- Three-layer appearance: intima (bright), media (dark), adventitia (bright)
- Lumen-intima border and external elastic membrane (EEM) clearly visualized
- Calcium appears as bright echo with acoustic shadowing behind
- Soft plaque: echolucent signal; fibrous plaque: intermediate echogenicity

## Equipment

- IVUS console: Philips (Volcano) or Boston Scientific (iLab)
- IVUS catheters: 3.2Fr-3.5Fr, compatible with 6Fr guide catheter
- Mechanical rotational: OptiCross HD (60 MHz), Revolution (45 MHz)
- Solid-state phased array: Eagle Eye Platinum (20 MHz)
- Motorized pullback device (0.5-1.0 mm/sec standard speed)
- Co-registration software available on newer platforms

## Technique

1. After guidewire placement across target lesion, advance IVUS catheter distally
2. Position catheter tip at least 10 mm beyond the distal lesion edge
3. Initiate motorized pullback at 0.5 mm/sec (standard) or 1.0 mm/sec (screening)
4. Record continuous pullback from distal reference to proximal reference
5. Analyze: minimum lumen area (MLA), minimum lumen diameter (MLD), plaque burden
6. Measure reference vessel size at EEM level for stent sizing
7. Assess calcium arc and length for calcium modification planning

**IVUS Criteria for Stent Optimization:**
- MLA in stented segment >= 5.5 mm2 (or >= 80% of distal reference lumen area)
- Complete stent apposition (no malapposition gap >0.4 mm)
- No significant edge dissection (intimal flap >60 degrees or >2 mm length)
- No geographic miss (plaque burden <50% at stent edges)

## Complications

- Coronary spasm during catheter advancement (1-3%; managed with IC nitroglycerin)
- Transient ischemia during pullback (<1%)
- Air embolism from flush port (rare; careful flushing technique)
- Vessel dissection from catheter (rare; <0.5%)
- Equipment entrapment (extremely rare)

## India-Specific Considerations

- IVUS adoption in India growing but still limited to high-volume centers
- Cost per IVUS catheter: INR 25,000-40,000, adding significant expense to PCI
- Reuse of IVUS catheters practiced in some centers (not recommended by manufacturers)
- IVUS guidance for left main PCI increasingly standard in Indian practice
- Training in IVUS interpretation available through CSI workshops and online platforms
- Co-registration and AI-assisted interpretation tools may improve adoption
