# Bifurcation PCI

Bifurcation lesions involve the main branch (MB) and side branch (SB) at a coronary branch point, accounting for 15-20% of all PCI procedures. Proper classification, strategy selection, and meticulous technique are essential for optimal outcomes.

## Indications

- Hemodynamically significant bifurcation stenosis involving main branch and/or side branch
- ACS with culprit lesion at a bifurcation point
- Symptomatic stable CAD with bifurcation disease and objective ischemia
- Restenosis at a bifurcation site

## Contraindications

- Bifurcation disease better suited for CABG (high SYNTAX score, diabetes, multivessel)
- Side branch too small (<2.0 mm) to warrant protection (not a true bifurcation)
- Inability to tolerate prolonged DAPT (especially for two-stent strategies)
- Severe vessel tortuosity or calcification precluding adequate stent deployment

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Classify lesion using Medina classification (1,1,1 through 0,0,1)
- Assess side branch size, angle, and territory supplied
- Determine if provisional vs. upfront two-stent strategy
- Provisional stenting is default for most bifurcation lesions (EBC consensus)
- Two-stent strategy for: large SB (>2.5 mm), long SB ostial lesion (>5 mm), Medina 1,1,1 with significant SB disease

## Key Anatomy

- Main branch (MB): larger vessel continuing distally
- Side branch (SB): vessel arising at the bifurcation point
- Carina: flow divider at the bifurcation
- Bifurcation angle: wide angle (>70 degrees) vs. narrow angle (<70 degrees)
- Common bifurcation sites: LAD-diagonal, LCx-OM, distal LM, RCA-PDA

## Equipment

- Guide catheter: 6Fr minimum (7Fr for two-stent strategies)
- Two guidewires: one for MB, one for SB
- Balloon catheters: semi-compliant for predilation, NC for post-dilation and POT
- DES: appropriately sized for MB and SB
- Dedicated bifurcation stents (Tryton, BiOSS) rarely used; conventional DES preferred

## Technique

**Provisional Strategy (Default):**
1. Wire both main branch and side branch
2. Predilate main branch; consider SB predilation if tight stenosis
3. Deploy stent from MB across SB origin
4. Perform proximal optimization technique (POT) with NC balloon
5. Assess SB: if TIMI 3 flow and <75% ostial stenosis, accept result
6. If SB compromised: rewire SB through stent struts (distal cell preferred)
7. Perform kissing balloon inflation (KBI) if SB intervention needed
8. Final POT after KBI

**Two-Stent Strategies (When Needed):**
- DK Crush: SB stent first, crush, MB stent, rewire SB, KBI, final POT
- Culotte: first stent through MB or SB, second stent through struts, KBI, POT
- T-stent / TAP: MB stent first, SB stent deployed at ostium with minimal protrusion

## Complications

- Side branch occlusion (5-8% with provisional strategy)
- Stent distortion during KBI or two-stent deployment
- Wire bias leading to suboptimal rewiring position (proximal cell rewiring)
- Carina shift causing SB compromise
- Higher rates of stent thrombosis with two-stent techniques
- Geographic miss at bifurcation point

## India-Specific Considerations

- Bifurcation PCI volumes increasing with growing operator experience across India
- Provisional strategy well-suited for Indian practice given cost considerations
- DK Crush technique widely adopted by Indian operators for complex LM bifurcation
- Two-stent strategies add INR 25,000-50,000 for additional stent and equipment
- Training in bifurcation PCI through dedicated workshops (CSI, NIC) with bench models
- IVUS/OCT guidance recommended but availability remains a barrier in many centers
