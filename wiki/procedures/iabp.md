# Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)

The intra-aortic balloon pump is the most widely used temporary mechanical circulatory support device. It provides counterpulsation by inflating during diastole (augmenting coronary perfusion and diastolic pressure) and deflating during systole (reducing afterload and myocardial oxygen demand). It provides modest hemodynamic support (0.5 L/min increase in cardiac output).

## Indications

- Cardiogenic shock as bridge to definitive therapy (though IABP-SHOCK II showed no mortality benefit)
- Mechanical complications of MI: acute mitral regurgitation, ventricular septal defect
- Refractory unstable angina as bridge to revascularization
- High-risk PCI: severe LV dysfunction, unprotected left main
- Pre-operative stabilization for high-risk cardiac surgery
- Refractory ventricular arrhythmias with hemodynamic compromise

## Contraindications

- Moderate-severe aortic regurgitation (counterpulsation worsens AR)
- Aortic dissection or aortic aneurysm
- Severe aortoiliac occlusive disease (cannot advance balloon)
- Uncontrolled sepsis (relative)
- Tachyarrhythmias with rate >150 bpm (cannot trigger effectively)
- Irreversible cardiac failure without definitive treatment plan

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Assess femoral and iliac arterial access (CT angiography if available)
- Select balloon size based on patient height (34-50 mL volumes)
- Ensure adequate anticoagulation protocol (heparin infusion during IABP use)
- Prepare for ECG or pressure triggering mode
- Identify recovery plan: bridge to decision, bridge to surgery, or bridge to advanced MCS

## Key Anatomy

- IABP tip positioned in descending thoracic aorta, 2 cm below left subclavian artery
- Distal end above renal arteries (confirmed by fluoroscopy or CXR)
- Balloon length: 25-27 cm; should not obstruct arch vessels or renal arteries
- Femoral artery puncture site: common femoral artery (same as standard femoral access)

## Equipment

- IABP console: Maquet (Getinge) CS300, Arrow AutoCAT
- Balloon catheter: 7Fr-8Fr, available in 34 mL, 40 mL, and 50 mL sizes
- Size selection: 34 mL (height <152 cm), 40 mL (152-183 cm), 50 mL (>183 cm)
- Helium gas (used to inflate balloon; low molecular weight for rapid shuttle)
- Sheathless or 8Fr sheath insertion options

## Technique

1. Obtain femoral arterial access (ultrasound-guided preferred)
2. Advance guidewire to descending aorta under fluoroscopy
3. Insert IABP catheter over wire (sheathless technique preferred)
4. Position balloon tip at 2nd-3rd intercostal space (2 cm below left subclavian origin)
5. Confirm position: fluoroscopy or CXR (tip at carina level)
6. Connect to console; select triggering mode (ECG preferred; pressure as backup)
7. Set timing: inflate at dicrotic notch (onset of diastole), deflate before systole
8. Confirm optimal timing on arterial waveform:
   - Diastolic augmentation should exceed unassisted systolic pressure
   - Assisted systolic pressure should be lower than unassisted systolic pressure
   - Assisted end-diastolic pressure should be lower than unassisted end-diastolic pressure
9. Start at 1:1 ratio; wean by reducing ratio (1:2, 1:3) before removal
10. Anticoagulate with heparin (target aPTT 50-70 seconds) during IABP support

## Complications

- Limb ischemia (2-5%): monitor distal pulses, Doppler assessment
- Vascular injury: dissection, perforation, pseudoaneurysm (1-3%)
- Balloon rupture: blood in helium tubing (remove immediately)
- Thrombocytopenia from mechanical platelet destruction
- Infection: catheter-related bloodstream infection with prolonged use
- Cerebral or visceral embolization (rare)
- Aortic injury or perforation (rare)

## Key Trials

- [[IABP-SHOCK-II]] - IABP did not reduce 30-day mortality in AMI cardiogenic shock
- [[CRISP-AMI]] - IABP in anterior STEMI without shock (no benefit)
- [[BCIS-1]] - IABP in high-risk PCI (no 30-day benefit; possible long-term benefit)

## India-Specific Considerations

- IABP is the most widely available MCS device in India across all tiers of cardiac centers
- Cost: INR 40,000-60,000 for balloon catheter; console reusable
- Remains commonly used in AMI-CS in India despite IABP-SHOCK II results
- Useful as bridge to surgery for mechanical complications (VSD, papillary muscle rupture)
- Sheathless insertion technique gaining adoption to reduce vascular complications
- Smaller femoral arteries in Indian patients may increase limb ischemia risk
- Nursing training for IABP management and troubleshooting essential in CCU settings
