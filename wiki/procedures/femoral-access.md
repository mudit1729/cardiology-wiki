# Femoral Access

Femoral arterial access via the common femoral artery (CFA) remains an essential technique in interventional cardiology, particularly for procedures requiring large-bore access, mechanical circulatory support, or structural heart interventions where radial access is insufficient.

## Indications

- Procedures requiring large-bore access (>7Fr): TAVR, Impella, IABP, ECMO
- Radial access failure or radial artery occlusion bilaterally
- Complex PCI requiring 8Fr guide catheters
- Right and left heart catheterization with hemodynamic assessment
- Peripheral vascular interventions
- Structural heart procedures (TAVR, MitraClip, paravalvular leak closure)

## Contraindications

- Severe aortoiliac occlusive disease
- Prior femoral artery bypass graft at intended access site
- Active groin infection or skin breakdown
- Known CFA pseudoaneurysm or AV fistula
- Severe scarring from prior access (relative)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Assess femoral pulses bilaterally; consider CT angiography for large-bore access
- Review prior imaging for iliac tortuosity, calcification, or aneurysmal disease
- Plan closure device strategy (Perclose ProGlide, Angio-Seal, manual compression)
- Manage anticoagulation: hold warfarin (INR <1.8), manage DOACs per protocol
- Ultrasound-guided access recommended (reduces complications by 50%)

## Key Anatomy

- Common femoral artery (CFA): between inguinal ligament and bifurcation into SFA/profunda
- Target puncture zone: CFA over the inferior border of the femoral head on fluoroscopy
- Femoral vein lies medial to the artery at the level of the inguinal ligament
- Femoral nerve lies lateral to the artery (nerve-artery-vein from lateral to medial)
- CFA diameter typically 6-8 mm; smaller in Indian women and elderly patients

## Equipment

- Access needle: 18G modified Seldinger needle
- Guidewire: 0.035" J-tip standard wire; 0.035" stiff wire for sheath exchanges
- Sheaths: 6Fr-8Fr for standard PCI; 14Fr-24Fr for large-bore access
- Closure devices: Angio-Seal, Perclose ProGlide (pre-close technique), MANTA
- Micropuncture kit (21G needle with 0.018" wire) for ultrasound-guided access

## Technique

1. Position patient supine; identify femoral head by fluoroscopy or ultrasound
2. Target the CFA at the level of the inferior femoral head (mid-femoral head)
3. Infiltrate 10-15 mL of 1% lidocaine in subcutaneous tissue and around artery
4. Puncture CFA with anterior wall technique (preferred) under ultrasound guidance
5. Confirm arterial blood return; advance J-tip wire under fluoroscopy
6. Confirm wire in abdominal aorta (not profunda or superficial femoral artery)
7. Insert appropriate sheath over wire
8. For large-bore access: pre-close with Perclose ProGlide devices before sheath upsizing
9. Post-procedure: achieve hemostasis via closure device or manual compression (15-20 min)
10. Bed rest for 2-6 hours depending on sheath size and closure method

## Complications

- Access site bleeding or hematoma (2-6%)
- Retroperitoneal hemorrhage (0.5-1%; high puncture above inguinal ligament)
- Pseudoaneurysm (0.5-2%; managed with ultrasound-guided thrombin injection)
- Arteriovenous fistula (0.2-0.5%)
- Limb ischemia from dissection or thrombosis (<0.5%)
- Infection at access site (<0.5%)

## India-Specific Considerations

- Femoral access still widely used in tier-2/3 city cath labs due to operator familiarity
- Ultrasound-guided access adoption limited by availability of bedside ultrasound
- Closure device costs (INR 8,000-25,000) may favor manual compression in cost-sensitive settings
- Smaller CFA diameter in Indian population may increase vascular complication rates
- High ambient temperatures in many regions increase infection risk requiring meticulous sterile technique
- Prolonged bed rest post-femoral access adds to hospital costs and patient discomfort
