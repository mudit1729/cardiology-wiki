# Radial Access

Transradial access (TRA) is the preferred arterial access route for coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention. It offers significant advantages over femoral access in reducing vascular complications and enabling early ambulation.

## Indications

- Diagnostic coronary angiography (default access in contemporary practice)
- Percutaneous coronary intervention (primary PCI, elective PCI)
- Peripheral angiography via radial approach
- Patients on anticoagulation or with high bleeding risk
- Outpatient / same-day discharge procedures

## Contraindications

- Abnormal Allen test or Barbeau test (absent collateral ulnar flow)
- Radial artery occlusion (prior access)
- AV fistula in the ipsilateral arm (hemodialysis patients)
- Severe peripheral vascular disease affecting upper extremity
- Need for large-bore access (>7Fr in most labs)
- Planned use of devices requiring femoral-only platforms

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Perform modified Allen test or Barbeau test with pulse oximetry
- Assess radial pulse quality, caliber, and course (tortuous radial artery)
- Left radial preferred for some operators (ergonomic advantage for LIMA graft engagement)
- Distal radial (anatomical snuffbox) increasingly used as alternative site
- Pre-procedure cocktail: verapamil 2.5-5 mg + nitroglycerin 200 mcg + heparin via sheath

## Key Anatomy

- Radial artery: branch of brachial artery at antecubital fossa
- Course: lateral forearm, superficial at the wrist between FCR tendon and styloid process
- Distal radial artery: located in anatomical snuffbox between EPL and EPB tendons
- Radial artery diameter: typically 2.2-3.0 mm (smaller in Indian women)
- Radial loop and high radial takeoff: important anatomical variants (5-10%)

## Equipment

- Radial sheath: 5Fr or 6Fr hydrophilic sheaths (Glidesheath Slender, Merit Prelude)
- Micropuncture needle (21G) or standard radial access needle (20G)
- 0.025" or 0.021" hydrophilic wire for initial access
- Radial compression band (TR Band, Safeguard) for hemostasis
- Spasmolytic cocktail: verapamil, nitroglycerin, heparin

## Technique

1. Position arm on arm board in supinated position; extend wrist over rolled towel
2. Palpate radial pulse 1-2 cm proximal to radial styloid (or in snuffbox for dTRA)
3. Administer local anesthesia (1% lidocaine subcutaneous, 0.3-0.5 mL)
4. Puncture radial artery with through-and-through or anterior wall technique
5. Advance 0.025" wire smoothly; watch for resistance (spasm or tortuosity)
6. Insert hydrophilic sheath over wire
7. Administer spasmolytic cocktail through sheath
8. Advance diagnostic or guide catheters to aortic root
9. At procedure end, remove sheath; apply patent hemostasis using compression band
10. Maintain patent hemostasis protocol: ensure antegrade flow during compression

## Complications

- Radial artery spasm (5-10%; managed with additional spasmolytics)
- Radial artery occlusion (2-5%; minimized by patent hemostasis technique)
- Access site hematoma (1-2%; usually minor)
- Radial artery perforation or dissection (<1%)
- Compartment syndrome (extremely rare, <0.01%)
- Sterile granuloma at puncture site

## India-Specific Considerations

- Radial access increasingly standard in metropolitan centers; adoption growing in tier-2 cities
- Smaller radial artery caliber in Indian patients requires careful sheath size selection
- High prevalence of radial artery spasm, especially in younger female patients
- Cost of hydrophilic sheaths and TR bands may limit use in resource-constrained settings
- Distal radial access gaining popularity among Indian interventionalists
- Training programs through CSI and ISCAI increasingly emphasize radial-first approach
