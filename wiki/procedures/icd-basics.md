# ICD Basics

An implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) is a device that continuously monitors cardiac rhythm and delivers therapies (anti-tachycardia pacing or defibrillation shocks) to terminate life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias. It is the primary therapy for secondary prevention and selected primary prevention of sudden cardiac death (SCD).

## Indications

**Secondary Prevention:**
- Survivors of cardiac arrest due to VF or hemodynamically significant VT
- Sustained VT with structural heart disease
- Syncope with inducible VT on electrophysiology study

**Primary Prevention:**
- Ischemic cardiomyopathy with LVEF <= 35% and NYHA II-III (>40 days post-MI, >90 days post-revascularization)
- Non-ischemic dilated cardiomyopathy with LVEF <= 35% and NYHA II-III (despite >= 3 months OMT)
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with high-risk features (SCD risk score >=6% at 5 years)
- Cardiac channelopathies with high-risk features (Brugada, LQTS, CPVT)
- ARVC with risk factors for SCD

## Contraindications

- Reversible causes of VT/VF (acute ischemia, electrolyte imbalance, drug toxicity)
- NYHA Class IV refractory heart failure not a candidate for transplant/LVAD
- Incessant VT/VF (address substrate first; ICD for storm management not primary therapy)
- Life expectancy <1 year from non-cardiac causes
- Severe psychiatric illness precluding device management
- Patients within 40 days of MI or 90 days of revascularization (primary prevention)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Echocardiography: LVEF assessment, wall motion, chamber size
- Optimize medical therapy for minimum 3 months before primary prevention ICD
- Cardiac MRI for substrate assessment (scar characterization) when indicated
- Risk stratification: HCM Risk-SCD calculator, Brugada risk assessment
- Assess need for single-chamber (VR), dual-chamber (DR), or subcutaneous ICD (S-ICD)
- Review indications vs. alternative: wearable defibrillator (LifeVest) as bridge

## Key Anatomy

- Same venous access anatomy as pacemaker implantation
- RV lead for ICD positioned at RV apex (optimal for defibrillation vector)
- Defibrillation coil(s): RV coil (+/- SVC coil) integrated in lead
- Generator pocket: prepectoral, typically left side (defibrillation vector consideration)
- S-ICD: subcutaneous lead along left parasternal border; generator at left lateral chest wall

## Equipment

- ICD generator: single-chamber, dual-chamber, or CRT-D (with LV lead)
- ICD lead: high-voltage defibrillation lead with sensing/pacing electrodes and shock coil(s)
- Subcutaneous ICD system (S-ICD): Boston Scientific EMBLEM
- Defibrillation threshold testing (DFT) equipment (external defibrillator backup)
- Same ancillary equipment as pacemaker implantation

## Technique

1. Obtain venous access and create pulse generator pocket (similar to pacemaker)
2. Advance ICD lead to RV apex under fluoroscopy; secure with active fixation
3. Test sensing (R-wave >5 mV), pacing threshold (<1.0 V), impedance
4. Check high-voltage impedance (defibrillation circuit integrity)
5. For dual-chamber: place RA lead in RAA (same as pacemaker)
6. Connect leads to ICD generator; place in pocket
7. DFT testing: induce VF and confirm successful defibrillation (practice-dependent; many centers no longer test routinely)
8. Program detection zones: VF zone (>200 bpm), VT zone (170-200 bpm), VT monitor zone
9. Program therapies: ATP for VT zone, shock for VF zone
10. Wound closure and post-op CXR

**S-ICD Implantation:**
- Left lateral chest wall generator pocket at 5th-6th intercostal space
- Subcutaneous lead tunneled along left parasternal border
- No transvenous leads; reduced infection and lead failure risk
- Cannot provide anti-bradycardia pacing or ATP

## Complications

- Same procedural complications as pacemaker (pneumothorax, hematoma, infection)
- Inappropriate shocks (5-15%): due to T-wave oversensing, SVT, electromagnetic interference
- Lead failure requiring extraction (2-3% over 5 years)
- Psychological impact: anxiety, depression, PTSD from shocks
- Generator recall or advisory management
- S-ICD specific: inability to pace, inappropriate detection from myopotentials

## Key Trials

- [[MADIT-II]] - Primary prevention ICD in ischemic cardiomyopathy
- [[SCD-HeFT]] - ICD vs. amiodarone in heart failure
- [[DANISH]] - ICD in non-ischemic cardiomyopathy (no mortality benefit)
- [[PRAETORIAN]] - S-ICD vs. transvenous ICD
- [[ATLAS]] - ICD programming to reduce inappropriate therapy

## India-Specific Considerations

- ICD implantation rates in India far below global averages due to cost barriers
- Single-chamber ICD cost: INR 3-5 lakh; dual-chamber: INR 5-7 lakh; S-ICD: INR 10-12 lakh
- Government price regulation has improved affordability somewhat
- Primary prevention ICD underutilized due to cost despite clear indications
- Wearable defibrillator (LifeVest) as bridge available at limited centers
- Remote monitoring challenges: connectivity, patient compliance, infrastructure
- Shared decision-making essential given cost burden on patients and families
- SCD burden in India: rheumatic heart disease, young CAD, and channelopathies contribute
