# No-Reflow Management

No-reflow is the failure to restore normal myocardial perfusion (TIMI flow <3) despite successful epicardial vessel recanalization. It results from microvascular obstruction due to distal embolization, microvascular spasm, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and endothelial dysfunction. Prompt recognition and treatment are critical to limit infarct size.

## Indications

- TIMI 0-2 flow after otherwise successful PCI with no mechanical cause
- Slow flow during primary PCI for STEMI
- Distal embolization after PCI of thrombus-laden or degenerated SVG lesions
- Progressive decline in TIMI flow during intervention
- ST-segment persistence or worsening despite open epicardial vessel

## Contraindications

- Mechanical cause for poor flow (dissection, thrombus, stent edge issue) -- treat cause first
- Residual stenosis or underexpanded stent causing flow limitation (not true no-reflow)
- Coronary spasm at the intervention site (responds to IC nitroglycerin at epicardial level)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Identify high-risk situations: large thrombus burden, degenerated SVG, large plaque volume
- Pre-treat with GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors in high-risk ACS (debated)
- Consider direct stenting (avoid predilation) in thrombus-laden lesions
- Prepare pharmacological agents in advance for high-risk cases
- Consider aspiration thrombectomy for large thrombus burden (routine use not recommended)

## Key Anatomy

- Microvascular bed distal to treated epicardial stenosis
- Myocardial territory at risk determines clinical significance
- Microvascular obstruction zone seen on cardiac MRI as late gadolinium enhancement
- Capillary plugging by platelet-fibrin microemboli and atherosclerotic debris

## Equipment

- Intracoronary drug delivery: dedicated microcatheter or guide catheter extension
- Aspiration catheter (Export, Eliminate, Pronto) for thrombus removal
- Medications: adenosine, nitroprusside, verapamil, nicardipine, epinephrine
- Distal protection devices (for SVG interventions): FilterWire, SpiderFX
- GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors: abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban

## Technique

**Immediate Assessment:**
1. Confirm no mechanical cause: review angiogram for dissection, thrombus, stent issue
2. Ensure guide catheter is not damping (check pressure waveform)
3. Rule out air embolism (usually transient, self-resolving)

**Pharmacological Treatment:**
4. Intracoronary adenosine: 100-200 mcg boluses via guide or microcatheter (repeat x3-5)
5. Intracoronary nitroprusside: 100-200 mcg boluses (potent microvascular vasodilator)
6. Intracoronary verapamil: 100-200 mcg boluses (watch for hypotension and bradycardia)
7. Intracoronary nicardipine: 100-200 mcg boluses (less hypotension than verapamil)
8. Intracoronary epinephrine: 50-200 mcg for refractory no-reflow (watch for arrhythmia)

**Adjunctive Measures:**
9. Aspiration thrombectomy if significant residual thrombus
10. GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor infusion (intracoronary bolus may be more effective)
11. Maintain adequate blood pressure with IV fluids and vasopressors if needed
12. Consider IABP for hemodynamic support in cardiogenic shock

## Complications

- Hemodynamic instability from persistent no-reflow (hypotension, cardiogenic shock)
- Extended myocardial infarction and larger infarct size
- Malignant arrhythmias (VT/VF) from ongoing ischemia
- Drug-induced hypotension (verapamil, nitroprusside)
- Drug-induced arrhythmia (adenosine causing transient AV block)

## Key Trials

- [[TAPAS]] - Thrombus aspiration in primary PCI
- [[TASTE]] - Routine aspiration thrombectomy in STEMI (no benefit)
- [[TOTAL]] - Routine aspiration thrombectomy in STEMI (no benefit, potential harm)
- [[INFUSE-AMI]] - Abciximab in primary PCI for large anterior STEMI

## India-Specific Considerations

- No-reflow frequently encountered in STEMI due to delayed presentation (longer ischemic times)
- High thrombus burden common in Indian STEMI patients presenting late
- IC adenosine widely available and inexpensive; first-line treatment
- Nitroprusside availability variable; should be stocked in all cath labs performing primary PCI
- Aspiration catheters widely available at low cost (Indian-made options available)
- Hemodynamic support (IABP) availability variable in smaller centers
