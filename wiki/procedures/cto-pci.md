# CTO PCI

Chronic total occlusion (CTO) PCI involves recanalization of a completely occluded coronary artery (TIMI 0 flow) present for at least 3 months. It represents the most technically challenging subset of PCI, requiring specialized expertise, equipment, and a structured algorithmic approach.

## Indications

- Symptomatic angina or dyspnea attributable to CTO territory despite optimal medical therapy
- Large area of viable myocardium supplied by the CTO vessel (viability testing)
- Ischemia in CTO territory documented by stress testing or FFR of donor vessel
- Complete revascularization strategy (CTO PCI as part of multivessel PCI)
- Reduction of ischemic burden to improve prognosis (debated)

## Contraindications

- No symptoms and no evidence of ischemia/viability in CTO territory
- CTO vessel supplies only small territory or fully infarcted myocardium
- Severe comorbidities with limited life expectancy
- Bilateral donor vessel disease without adequate collaterals (high procedural risk)
- Patient unwilling to accept higher procedural risk and radiation exposure

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Dual coronary injection mandatory (assess collateral channels: CC0, CC1, CC2)
- Score CTO complexity: J-CTO score, PROGRESS-CTO score
- Plan wiring strategy: antegrade wire escalation, antegrade dissection re-entry, retrograde
- Hybrid algorithm: start with antegrade wire escalation, escalate as needed
- CT angiography helpful for assessing CTO length, calcification, and course
- Dedicated CTO inventory required (specialized wires, microcatheters, re-entry devices)

## Key Anatomy

- CTO cap morphology: tapered (favorable) vs. blunt (unfavorable)
- CTO length: short (<20 mm favorable) vs. long (>20 mm challenging)
- Proximal cap ambiguity: side branch at occlusion site obscuring entry point
- Distal vessel quality: landing zone assessment for stenting
- Collateral anatomy: septal, epicardial, bridging collaterals
- Interventional collateral channels for retrograde approach (CC1/CC2 septals preferred)

## Equipment

- Guide catheters: 7Fr or 8Fr for antegrade; 6Fr-7Fr for retrograde (bilateral access)
- CTO guidewires: tapered (Gaia, Conquest Pro), stiff (Confianza Pro 12), polymer-jacketed (Fielder XT-A, Pilot 200)
- Microcatheters: Corsair Pro, Finecross, Turnpike, Caravel
- Re-entry devices: Stingray balloon and guidewire (Boston Scientific)
- Retrograde equipment: externalization wire (RG3, R350)
- Trapping balloons, snares, and guide extension catheters (GuideLiner, Telescope)

## Technique

**Antegrade Wire Escalation (AWE):**
1. Engage CTO vessel with supportive guide catheter
2. Advance microcatheter to proximal cap
3. Attempt crossing with escalating wire stiffness (soft polymer -> tapered -> stiff)
4. Confirm true lumen position with contrast injection through microcatheter
5. Complete revascularization with balloon and DES

**Antegrade Dissection Re-entry (ADR):**
1. Intentional subintimal entry with knuckle wire technique
2. Track subintimal space past CTO body
3. Re-enter true lumen using Stingray balloon and wire, or wire-based re-entry
4. Stent from true lumen to true lumen

**Retrograde Approach:**
1. Access donor vessel; select and wire collateral channel (septal preferred)
2. Advance microcatheter through collateral to distal CTO cap
3. Retrograde wire crossing or reverse CART technique
4. Externalize retrograde wire through antegrade guide
5. Complete antegrade PCI over externalized wire

## Complications

- Donor vessel injury during retrograde approach (0.5-1%)
- Collateral perforation and tamponade (1-3%)
- Coronary perforation (3-5%): wire perforation, balloon perforation
- Equipment loss or entrapment (rare)
- High contrast volume and radiation exposure
- Periprocedural MI from collateral damage

## India-Specific Considerations

- CTO PCI expertise concentrated in high-volume metropolitan centers
- CTO equipment costs are substantial (INR 1-3 lakh for dedicated CTO inventory per case)
- High radiation exposure concerns; operator protection essential
- Growing number of Indian CTO operators trained through international proctorships
- Dual injection and bilateral access increase procedural complexity and cost
- CTO PCI workshops at NIC, CSI, and dedicated CTO courses driving skill development
