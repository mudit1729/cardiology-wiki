# Left Main PCI

Left main coronary artery (LMCA) PCI is a complex intervention performed for significant stenosis of the left main stem. Once exclusively a surgical domain, advances in stent technology, intravascular imaging, and operator expertise have established PCI as a viable alternative to CABG in select patients.

## Indications

- Significant left main stenosis (>50% angiographic, confirmed by IVUS/FFR)
- Low-to-intermediate SYNTAX score (<=32) favoring PCI over CABG
- Patients with high surgical risk (STS score >8%) or prohibitive surgical risk
- STEMI with left main culprit lesion
- Patient preference after Heart Team discussion

## Contraindications

- High SYNTAX score (>32) with complex downstream disease
- Concurrent need for surgical revascularization (e.g., concomitant valve surgery)
- Inability to tolerate DAPT for minimum required duration
- Severely depressed LVEF with extensive multivessel disease (relative)
- Ostial left main with heavy calcification precluding stent optimization (relative)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Heart Team discussion mandatory: interventional cardiologist + cardiac surgeon
- Calculate SYNTAX I and II scores for risk stratification
- Intravascular imaging (IVUS preferred) for lesion assessment and sizing
- Plan stenting strategy based on disease pattern (ostial, body, distal bifurcation)
- Hemodynamic support consideration (IABP/Impella) for unprotected LM with poor LV function
- Ensure backup surgical availability

## Key Anatomy

- LMCA: 10-15 mm average length, 3.5-5.0 mm diameter
- Ostial LM: within 3 mm of aortic origin
- Distal LM bifurcation: most common site of disease (60-90%)
- Medina classification for distal LM bifurcation disease
- Relationship to LAD, LCx, and occasionally ramus intermedius (trifurcation)

## Equipment

- Guide catheters: 6Fr or 7Fr EBU/XB (strong backup support essential)
- Intravascular imaging: IVUS catheter (mandatory for LM PCI)
- DES: appropriately sized (typically 3.5-4.5 mm for LM)
- Non-compliant balloons for post-dilation (sized to proximal optimization)
- Two-stent bifurcation equipment if needed (DK crush, culotte, T-stent)

## Technique

1. Obtain arterial access; large-bore guide (7Fr) recommended for bifurcation stenting
2. Wire both LAD and LCx if distal bifurcation disease present
3. Perform IVUS run to determine true lumen size and disease extent
4. For ostial/body LM: single stent strategy with proximal optimization
5. For distal LM bifurcation: provisional stenting preferred as default strategy
6. Deploy stent from LM into LAD; rewire LCx through stent struts
7. Perform proximal optimization technique (POT) with short NC balloon in LM
8. Kissing balloon inflation if significant LCx compromise
9. Final POT after kissing inflation
10. Confirm optimal result with IVUS: MLA >= 8 mm2 in LM, >= 6 mm2 in LAD ostium

## Complications

- LCx occlusion during LM-LAD stenting (mitigated by protecting LCx with wire)
- Stent underexpansion (major predictor of stent thrombosis; use IVUS to guide)
- Hemodynamic collapse during LM intervention (especially with poor LV function)
- Stent thrombosis (catastrophic in LM territory)
- Aorto-ostial dissection with ostial LM stenting

## India-Specific Considerations

- Left main PCI increasingly performed in high-volume Indian centers with IVUS guidance
- Cost advantage of PCI over CABG significant in India (INR 2-4 lakh vs. 3-6 lakh)
- IVUS availability remains limited in smaller centers; IVUS guidance is mandatory for LM PCI
- Heart Team discussions may be informal in some settings; structured approach recommended
- High diabetes prevalence in Indian patients may favor CABG for complex LM + multivessel disease
- Training in complex LM PCI available through workshops by CSI and ISCAI
