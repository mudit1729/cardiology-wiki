# Cath Lab Complications Management

Comprehensive management of complications during cardiac catheterization and intervention is a core competency for every interventional cardiologist. A systematic approach with anticipation, early recognition, and rehearsed response protocols is essential to minimize patient harm.

## Indications

- Management of any complication arising during diagnostic or interventional cardiac catheterization
- Applies to: coronary angiography, PCI, structural heart procedures, electrophysiology procedures
- Preparation and protocol readiness for all cath lab procedures

## Contraindications

- None: complication management is always indicated when a complication occurs
- Prevention through meticulous technique and patient selection is the best strategy

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Team briefing: review patient anatomy, comorbidities, anticipated risks
- Equipment readiness: covered stent, pericardiocentesis kit, IABP, defibrillator checked
- Blood products: type and screen for high-risk procedures; crossmatched blood available
- Surgical backup: awareness and availability for emergent surgical conversion
- Informed consent: discuss specific procedural risks with the patient
- Cath lab safety checklist: verify all emergency equipment functional

## Key Anatomy

- Coronary artery perforation sites: distal wire perforation, balloon/stent perforation
- Pericardial space: site of tamponade from perforation
- Retroperitoneal space: site of hemorrhage from high femoral puncture
- Aortic root: site of dissection from catheter trauma
- Cerebral vasculature: site of atheroembolic stroke

## Equipment

**Emergency Equipment (Must Be Immediately Available):**
- Pericardiocentesis kit: long needle, pigtail catheter, drainage bag
- Covered stent: PK Papyrus (coronary) or BeGraft (peripheral)
- Autotransfusion system for massive bleeding
- IABP console and balloon catheter
- External defibrillator and transcutaneous pacing
- Endotracheal intubation equipment and crash cart
- Coil embolization materials for distal wire perforations
- Fat embolism or thrombin injection for vessel sealing
- Protamine sulfate for heparin reversal

## Technique

**Coronary Perforation (Ellis Classification):**
- Type I (extraluminal crater): observe; usually self-limited
- Type II (pericardial or myocardial blush): prolonged balloon tamponade at perforation site
- Type III (frank perforation with jet streaming): emergent covered stent or surgery
  1. Immediately inflate balloon proximal to perforation site (balloon tamponade)
  2. Reverse anticoagulation with protamine (1 mg per 100 units heparin)
  3. Monitor for tamponade: perform echocardiography immediately
  4. Deliver covered stent across perforation site
  5. For distal wire perforations: coil embolization or fat embolization
  6. If tamponade develops: emergent pericardiocentesis with drainage catheter

**Cardiac Tamponade:**
1. Recognize: hypotension, tachycardia, pulsus paradoxus, jugular venous distension
2. Confirm: emergent echocardiography (pericardial effusion with diastolic chamber collapse)
3. Pericardiocentesis: subxiphoid approach under fluoroscopic or echo guidance
4. Place pigtail drain in pericardial space for ongoing drainage
5. Autotransfuse pericardial blood if needed
6. Monitor for ongoing bleeding; surgical repair if bleeding persists

**Aortic Dissection:**
1. Recognize: sudden chest pain, aortic root haziness on angiography
2. Immediate angiography of aortic root to assess extent
3. If limited (Dunning Type I/II): conservative management; stent coronary ostium if needed
4. If extensive (Dunning Type III): emergent cardiac surgery consultation

**Acute Vessel Closure:**
1. Rewire vessel if possible
2. Aspirate thrombus if present
3. Balloon dilate and/or place additional stent
4. Administer GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor
5. Rule out underlying cause (dissection, thrombus, spasm)

**Air Embolism:**
1. Usually self-limited (seconds to minutes)
2. Forceful contrast injection to push air distally
3. Aspiration through guide catheter if large air bolus
4. Supportive care: oxygen, vasopressors if needed

## Complications

- Complications of complication management itself (e.g., pericardiocentesis injury)
- Delayed recognition leading to irreversible injury
- Inadequate equipment availability prolonging response time
- Communication failures during crisis management

## India-Specific Considerations

- Emergency equipment readiness varies significantly across Indian cath labs
- Covered stents should be stocked in all labs performing PCI (not always the case in smaller labs)
- Cardiac surgery backup: on-site surgery preferred; many Indian centers operate without on-site surgery
- Pericardiocentesis skills essential for all interventional cardiologists (not just radiologists)
- Blood bank proximity and turnaround time critical; pre-procedure type and screen recommended
- Cath lab safety checklists and structured time-out protocols improving but not universally adopted
- Regular simulation-based training for complication management recommended
- Morbidity-mortality conference culture growing across Indian cardiology programs
