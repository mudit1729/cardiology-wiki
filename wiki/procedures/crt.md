# Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy (CRT)

Cardiac resynchronization therapy delivers biventricular pacing to restore synchronous ventricular contraction in patients with heart failure and ventricular dyssynchrony. CRT improves symptoms, reduces heart failure hospitalizations, and reduces mortality in appropriately selected patients with LBBB and reduced LVEF.

## Indications

- LVEF <= 35%, NYHA Class II-IV (ambulatory), sinus rhythm, LBBB with QRS >= 150 ms (Class I)
- LVEF <= 35%, NYHA Class II-IV, sinus rhythm, LBBB with QRS 130-149 ms (Class IIa)
- LVEF <= 35%, NYHA Class II-IV, sinus rhythm, non-LBBB with QRS >= 150 ms (Class IIa)
- Patients with conventional pacemaker indication and LVEF <= 35% (CRT instead of RV pacing)
- High-burden RV pacing (>40%) with declining LVEF (upgrade to CRT)
- Atrial fibrillation with controlled rate and above criteria (requires high % biventricular pacing)

## Contraindications

- QRS <130 ms (no benefit; potential harm)
- Non-LBBB morphology with QRS 130-149 ms (limited evidence; Class III)
- NYHA Class I (no benefit)
- Expected survival <1 year from non-cardiac comorbidities
- Active infection or sepsis
- Unfavorable coronary sinus anatomy precluding LV lead placement

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- 12-lead ECG: QRS duration and morphology (LBBB vs. non-LBBB)
- Echocardiography: LVEF, LV dimensions, dyssynchrony assessment, mitral regurgitation
- Cardiac MRI: scar burden and location (posterolateral scar predicts non-response)
- CT coronary venous anatomy (optional; helps plan LV lead placement)
- Choose CRT-P (pacemaker) vs. CRT-D (defibrillator) based on ICD indication
- Optimize medical therapy before implantation (GDMT for heart failure)

## Key Anatomy

- Coronary sinus: main venous conduit accessed from RA
- CS os: located in low posterior RA, inferior to Thebesian valve
- Coronary venous branches: lateral, posterolateral, anterolateral, posterior
- Target LV lead position: lateral or posterolateral vein (concordant with latest mechanical activation)
- Avoid apical position: associated with worse outcomes
- Assess for phrenic nerve stimulation risk (lateral positions)

## Equipment

- CRT-P or CRT-D generator (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Biotronik)
- RA lead and RV lead (same as dual-chamber pacemaker/ICD)
- CS access tools: CS cannulation catheter (deflectable), 0.035" wire
- CS sub-selector catheter for venous branch cannulation
- LV pacing lead: over-the-wire (OTW) bipolar or quadripolar (preferred)
- CS venogram: contrast injection through balloon-tipped catheter
- Quadripolar LV leads: enable multiple pacing vectors without repositioning

## Technique

1. Place RA and RV leads as per standard pacemaker/ICD implantation
2. Cannulate coronary sinus ostium with CS guide catheter from left subclavian access
3. Advance guide into CS body; perform CS venogram (balloon-occluded angiography)
4. Identify target branch: lateral or posterolateral vein
5. Advance sub-selector catheter and 0.014" guidewire into target branch
6. Advance LV lead (quadripolar preferred) over wire to target position
7. Test LV lead: capture threshold (<3.5 V at 0.5 ms), impedance, absence of phrenic nerve stimulation
8. If phrenic stimulation: reprogram pacing vector (quadripolar advantage) or reposition
9. Connect all leads to CRT generator; place in pocket
10. Program: optimize AV delay and VV timing (echocardiography-guided or algorithm-based)
11. Target >98% biventricular pacing for maximum benefit

## Complications

- CS dissection or perforation during cannulation (1-2%)
- LV lead dislodgement (5-10%; higher than RA/RV leads)
- Phrenic nerve stimulation (managed by vector reprogramming with quadripolar leads)
- Failure to implant LV lead (5-10%): consider surgical epicardial lead or His-LBBAP
- All standard pacemaker/ICD complications
- CRT non-response (30-40%): multifactorial; optimize patient selection and lead position

## India-Specific Considerations

- CRT implantation rates in India far below Western standards due to cost barriers
- CRT-P cost: INR 2-3 lakh; CRT-D cost: INR 6-10 lakh
- Quadripolar LV leads improving implant success but add INR 50,000-1 lakh to cost
- Heart failure burden in India is enormous; CRT underutilized in eligible patients
- Conduction system pacing (His/LBBAP) emerging as alternative to CS-based CRT at lower cost
- Training: dedicated CRT workshops with simulation at CSI and IHRS conferences
- Remote monitoring challenges: connectivity and patient follow-up infrastructure
- Shared decision-making on CRT-P vs. CRT-D crucial given cost differential in India
