# Transcatheter Mitral Interventions

Transcatheter mitral valve interventions encompass a growing family of percutaneous techniques for treating mitral regurgitation (MR). Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) using the MitraClip or PASCAL system is the most established approach, replicating the surgical Alfieri stitch to reduce MR severity.

## Indications

**TEER (MitraClip/PASCAL):**
- Symptomatic severe primary (degenerative) MR at prohibitive surgical risk
- Symptomatic severe secondary (functional) MR despite GDMT with LVEF 20-50%, LVESD <= 70 mm
- Heart failure patients with severe secondary MR who meet COAPT trial criteria
- Bridge therapy in patients awaiting cardiac transplant or LVAD

**Other Transcatheter Mitral Interventions:**
- Transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR): severe MR or MS not suitable for TEER (investigational)
- Percutaneous mitral balloon valvuloplasty (PMBV): rheumatic mitral stenosis (well-established)
- Mitral annuloplasty devices: transcatheter annular reduction (investigational)

## Contraindications

**TEER:**
- Active endocarditis on mitral valve
- Rheumatic mitral valve disease (leaflets too rigid for TEER)
- Mitral valve area <4.0 cm2 (risk of mitral stenosis after clipping)
- Severe mitral annular calcification (MAC) limiting leaflet mobility
- Leaflet anatomy not suitable: short leaflets, large flail gap (>10 mm), wide flail width
- Intracardiac thrombus (LA or LAA thrombus)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE): detailed MR mechanism, jet localization, leaflet anatomy
- Assess MR etiology: primary (degenerative) vs. secondary (functional)
- Leaflet measurements: leaflet length (>10 mm), coaptation depth (<11 mm), coaptation length
- Assess grasping zone location and number of clips needed
- CT for transseptal puncture planning and LA anatomy
- Heart Team discussion: interventional cardiologist, cardiac surgeon, imaging specialist
- Right heart catheterization for hemodynamic assessment (PCWP, PA pressures)

## Key Anatomy

- Mitral valve: anterior leaflet (A1, A2, A3) and posterior leaflet (P1, P2, P3)
- Mitral annulus: saddle-shaped, fibromuscular structure
- Commissures: anterolateral and posteromedial
- Subvalvular apparatus: chordae tendineae, papillary muscles
- Interatrial septum: transseptal puncture site (fossa ovalis)
- Target puncture height: 4.0-4.5 cm above mitral annular plane for TEER

## Equipment

- MitraClip system (Abbott): NTR, NTW, XTR, XTW sizes; clip delivery system
- PASCAL system (Edwards): PASCAL, PASCAL Ace; broader paddles with spacer
- Transseptal puncture equipment: Brockenbrough needle, Mullins sheath, RF needle
- Steerable guide catheter: 24Fr steerable guide sheath for clip delivery
- TEE probe: real-time 3D echocardiographic guidance (mandatory)
- Hemodynamic monitoring: arterial line, venous access

## Technique

**TEER Procedure:**
1. General anesthesia with TEE guidance throughout
2. Femoral venous access (24Fr for guide sheath, additional venous access for PA catheter)
3. Transseptal puncture under fluoroscopic and TEE guidance (posterior-superior location)
4. Advance steerable guide catheter across septum into left atrium
5. Advance clip delivery system through guide to left atrium above mitral valve
6. Steer clip to target regurgitant jet location using TEE guidance (biplane/3D)
7. Advance clip below mitral valve into LV with arms open
8. Retract clip to grasp anterior and posterior leaflets simultaneously
9. Confirm adequate tissue capture on TEE (leaflet insertion into clip)
10. Close clip partially; assess MR reduction on color Doppler
11. Close clip fully; assess residual MR and transmitral gradient
12. If result acceptable (MR <= 2+, mean gradient <5 mmHg): deploy clip
13. If additional MR reduction needed: deploy second clip (or more)
14. Remove delivery system; achieve hemostasis at femoral venous access site

## Complications

- Clip detachment or partial leaflet detachment (1-2%)
- Mitral stenosis from excessive clipping (mean gradient >5 mmHg)
- Atrial septal defect (residual; usually small and clinically insignificant)
- Pericardial tamponade from transseptal puncture (1-2%)
- Clip embolization (rare; <0.5%)
- Bleeding from venous access site
- Stroke (1-2%)
- Residual significant MR requiring additional intervention

## Key Trials

- [[COAPT]] - TEER (MitraClip) vs. GDMT for severe secondary MR with HF
- [[MITRA-FR]] - TEER vs. GDMT for secondary MR (no benefit; patient selection differs from COAPT)
- [[EVEREST-II]] - MitraClip vs. surgical mitral repair for primary MR
- [[CLASP-IID/IIF]] - PASCAL system for MR
- [[RESHAPE-HF2]] - TEER in heart failure with FMR

## India-Specific Considerations

- TEER availability limited to approximately 15-20 high-volume centers in India
- Cost: INR 15-25 lakh (device + procedure); each additional clip adds INR 5-8 lakh
- PMBV for rheumatic MS remains far more commonly performed in India than TEER
- Rheumatic MR (common in India) is generally not suitable for TEER due to leaflet pathology
- Degenerative MR in elderly Indian patients represents the primary TEER-eligible population
- Structural heart fellowship training expanding but TEER experience requires high-volume exposure
- 3D TEE imaging expertise critical for TEER; requires dedicated imaging cardiologists
- Cost-effectiveness of TEER in Indian context needs further study given high device costs
