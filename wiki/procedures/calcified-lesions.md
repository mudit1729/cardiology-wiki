# Calcified Lesion Management

Severely calcified coronary lesions pose significant challenges during PCI, including difficulty crossing with equipment, inadequate lesion preparation, stent underexpansion, and increased complications. A systematic approach to calcium modification is essential for optimal outcomes.

## Indications

- Angiographically visible coronary calcification requiring PCI
- Lesion that cannot be crossed or dilated with standard balloon angioplasty
- Stent underexpansion due to calcification on intravascular imaging
- Calcified lesion at critical locations (ostial, bifurcation, left main)

## Contraindications

- Diffuse severe calcification not amenable to any modification technique
- Vessel too small for atherectomy devices (<2.0 mm for rotablation)
- Severe tortuosity proximal to calcified segment (limits device delivery)
- Large thrombus burden (rotablation contraindicated in ACS with visible thrombus)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- IVUS or OCT to characterize calcium: arc, thickness, length, depth
- Calcification grading: superficial vs. deep; focal vs. diffuse
- Choose modification strategy based on calcium characteristics
- Superficial, thin calcium: scoring/cutting balloon or IVL
- Deep, thick, concentric calcium: rotational atherectomy
- Nodular calcium: orbital atherectomy or IVL
- Ensure adequate guide catheter support (7Fr for atherectomy devices)

## Key Anatomy

- Calcium distribution: concentric (full circumference) vs. eccentric (partial arc)
- Calcium depth: intimal (superficial) vs. medial (deep)
- Calcium length: focal (<5 mm) vs. diffuse (>5 mm)
- Nodular calcium: protruding calcified nodule into lumen (distinct entity)
- Common sites: ostial LAD, proximal RCA, LM, heavily calcified aorta

## Equipment

- Scoring balloons: AngioSculpt, NSE Alpha, Scoreflex
- Cutting balloons: Wolverine (atherotome blades)
- Rotational atherectomy: RotaPro system (1.25-2.0 mm burrs)
- Orbital atherectomy: Diamondback 360 (CSI)
- Intravascular lithotripsy: Shockwave C2 catheter
- High-pressure NC balloons: OPN NC (rated to 35 atm), Super High Pressure NC
- Intravascular imaging: IVUS and OCT for calcium characterization

## Technique

**Scoring/Cutting Balloon:**
1. Cross lesion with guidewire and low-profile balloon
2. Predilate with scoring/cutting balloon at nominal pressure
3. Create controlled dissection planes within calcium
4. Follow with NC balloon dilation and stent deployment

**Rotational Atherectomy (see [[rotablation]]):**
1. Use dedicated RotaWire (RotaWire Floppy or Extra Support)
2. Start with small burr (1.25-1.5 mm); burr-to-artery ratio 0.5-0.7
3. Pecking runs at 140,000-180,000 RPM with gentle advancement
4. Avoid rpm drop >5,000 (indicates excessive tissue engagement)
5. Follow with balloon dilation and stent deployment

**Intravascular Lithotripsy (see [[intravascular-lithotripsy]]):**
1. Cross lesion with IVL catheter (Shockwave C2)
2. Inflate balloon to 4 atm; deliver 10 pulses per position
3. Advance and repeat to cover entire calcified segment
4. Follow with NC balloon post-dilation and stent deployment

## Complications

- Coronary perforation (higher risk with atherectomy devices)
- Slow flow or no reflow from distal embolization of calcium debris
- Burr entrapment (rotablation in severely calcified, tortuous vessels)
- Dissection propagation from aggressive balloon dilation
- Stent underexpansion despite calcium modification (may need further treatment)

## Key Trials

- [[PREPARE-CALC]] - Scoring balloon vs. rotablation for calcified lesions
- [[DISRUPT-CAD-III]] - IVL safety and efficacy for calcified lesions
- [[ROTAXUS]] - Rotational atherectomy before DES implantation
- [[ORBIT-II]] - Orbital atherectomy for calcified lesions

## India-Specific Considerations

- High prevalence of calcified coronary disease in elderly Indian patients with diabetes
- Rotablation available at most high-volume centers; orbital atherectomy limited
- IVL (Shockwave) availability expanding but cost remains high (INR 1.5-2.5 lakh per catheter)
- Scoring balloons are cost-effective first-line option for moderate calcification
- Super high-pressure NC balloons (OPN) increasingly available as alternative to atherectomy
- Training in calcium modification techniques through dedicated workshops and proctorship
