# Impella Heart Pump

The Impella (Abiomed) is a catheter-based axial flow pump that provides active LV unloading by aspirating blood from the LV and expelling it into the ascending aorta. Available in multiple sizes (CP, 5.0, 5.5), it provides graded hemodynamic support from 3.5 to 6.2 L/min, exceeding IABP capability significantly.

## Indications

- Cardiogenic shock complicating acute MI (AMI-CS): DanGer Shock evidence
- High-risk PCI with severe LV dysfunction (protected PCI)
- Bridge to decision in cardiogenic shock (bridge to recovery, LVAD, or transplant)
- Post-cardiotomy cardiogenic shock
- Acute decompensated heart failure with refractory shock
- LV venting during VA-ECMO support (ECPELLA strategy)

## Contraindications

- Mechanical aortic valve or LVAD in situ
- Severe aortic stenosis (valve area <0.6 cm2)
- Moderate-to-severe aortic regurgitation
- LV thrombus (risk of embolization)
- Ventricular septal defect (left-to-right shunting around pump)
- Severe peripheral arterial disease precluding large-bore access (13-21Fr)
- Isolated right ventricular failure (Impella RP available for RV support)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Echocardiography: LV size, function, aortic valve pathology, LV thrombus exclusion
- Assess femoral access with CT angiography (iliac diameter, tortuosity, calcification)
- Select Impella size: CP (14Fr, 3.5 L/min), 5.0 (21Fr, 5.0 L/min), 5.5 (19Fr, 6.2 L/min)
- Plan access site: femoral artery for CP; femoral or axillary for 5.0/5.5
- Discuss with cardiac surgery for potential escalation (LVAD, transplant) or axillary cutdown
- Ensure hemodynamic monitoring team availability (dedicated Impella-trained nurses)

## Key Anatomy

- Impella inlet (aspiration area) positioned 3.5 cm below aortic valve in LV cavity
- Outlet (ejection area) positioned in ascending aorta above aortic valve
- Crosses aortic valve retrogradely; positioned using fluoroscopy and echocardiography
- Pigtail marker at inlet helps confirm position
- Monitor position: echo, fluoroscopy, and Impella console placement signal

## Equipment

- Impella CP Smart Assist system (14Fr, percutaneous femoral access)
- Impella 5.0 (21Fr, surgical cutdown; axillary or femoral)
- Impella 5.5 with SmartAssist (19Fr, surgical cutdown or percutaneous)
- Impella RP (for right ventricular support, 22Fr, femoral venous access)
- Automated Impella Controller console
- Large-bore femoral access equipment (Perclose pre-close technique for CP)
- Repositioning sheath for Impella CP

## Technique

**Impella CP Insertion:**
1. Obtain bilateral femoral angiography to confirm CFA suitability
2. Pre-close femoral artery with two Perclose ProGlide devices (suture-based closure)
3. Insert 14Fr peel-away sheath over stiff wire
4. Advance Impella CP catheter over 0.018" guidewire across aortic valve into LV
5. Position inlet area 3.5 cm below aortic annulus (fluoroscopic markers)
6. Confirm position with placement signal on console and echocardiography
7. Set performance level (P-level): start at P2, titrate to P8 based on hemodynamics
8. Initiate purge solution: heparinized 5% dextrose at 10 mL/hr through purge line
9. Monitor: motor current, placement signal, hemolysis markers (q6-8 hourly)
10. Repositioning if placement signal suboptimal (inlet too deep or too shallow)

**Weaning and Removal:**
11. Wean P-level by 2 levels every 4-6 hours as hemodynamics improve
12. Minimum P-level (P2) for 2-4 hours before removal
13. Pull Impella across aortic valve to ascending aorta
14. Remove device; deploy pre-closed Perclose sutures for hemostasis
15. Confirm hemostasis and distal limb perfusion

## Complications

- Hemolysis (10-15%): monitor plasma-free hemoglobin, LDH; reposition if persistent
- Limb ischemia (5-10%): monitor with continuous pulse oximetry on ipsilateral foot
- Access site bleeding and vascular complications (5-10%)
- Device migration or malposition requiring repositioning
- Aortic valve injury (rare; careful positioning)
- Stroke (2-3%)
- Infection with prolonged use (>5-7 days)

## India-Specific Considerations

- Impella availability limited to select high-volume centers in metropolitan cities
- Cost: INR 8-12 lakh per device; makes it prohibitive for most patients
- DanGer Shock results may increase demand; reimbursement pathways needed
- Training requirements significant: dedicated team (cardiologist, surgeon, nursing, perfusionist)
- Axillary cutdown access for Impella 5.0/5.5 requires surgical collaboration
- Patient selection critical given cost constraints: identify those with highest benefit potential
- VA-ECMO more available than Impella at many Indian centers; consider ECPELLA strategy
