# Pacemaker Basics

Permanent pacemaker implantation involves placing transvenous leads into cardiac chambers connected to a subcutaneous pulse generator to treat symptomatic bradyarrhythmias. Modern pacemakers offer sophisticated programming including rate response, automatic mode switching, and remote monitoring capabilities.

## Indications

- Symptomatic sinus node dysfunction (sick sinus syndrome, chronotropic incompetence)
- High-grade or complete AV block (second-degree Mobitz Type II, third-degree)
- Symptomatic first-degree AV block with hemodynamic compromise (PR >300 ms)
- Alternating bundle branch block
- Post-ablation AV block
- Neurocardiogenic syncope with significant cardioinhibitory component (refractory cases)
- Post-TAVR complete heart block (persistent beyond 48-72 hours)

## Contraindications

- Active systemic infection or bacteremia
- Active endocarditis
- Reversible cause of bradycardia (medication-related, metabolic, acute MI)
- Lack of venous access bilaterally (consider epicardial or leadless pacing)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- 12-lead ECG and Holter/event monitor to document arrhythmia
- Echocardiography: assess LV function, tricuspid valve, cardiac anatomy
- Review medications for reversible causes of bradycardia (beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin)
- Assess venous access: subclavian, axillary, or cephalic vein approach
- Anticoagulation management: continue warfarin (INR <3) or hold DOACs per protocol
- Choose pacemaker type: single-chamber (VVI/AAI), dual-chamber (DDD), or leadless (Micra)

## Key Anatomy

- Subclavian or axillary vein access for transvenous lead placement
- Cephalic vein cutdown as alternative (avoids subclavian crush)
- Right atrial appendage: typical RA lead position
- RV apex or RV septum (mid-septal pacing): RV lead positioning
- His bundle or left bundle branch area pacing (LBBAP): conduction system pacing targets
- Pulse generator pocket: prepectoral (subcutaneous) position, typically left side

## Equipment

- Pulse generator: single-chamber (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Biotronik)
- Pacing leads: active fixation (screw-in) or passive fixation (tined)
- Lead lengths: 52-65 cm (standard); stylet-driven or lumen-less
- Introducer sheaths and guide wires for venous access
- Pacing system analyzer (PSA) for intraoperative threshold testing
- Fluoroscopy system and lead positioning tools

## Technique

1. Obtain venous access: axillary vein puncture (preferred) or cephalic vein cutdown
2. Create pulse generator pocket in prepectoral space (left side standard)
3. Advance RV lead via introducer to RV apex or mid-septum under fluoroscopy
4. Test RV lead parameters: sensing (>5 mV), pacing threshold (<1.0 V at 0.5 ms), impedance (400-1500 ohms)
5. For dual-chamber: advance RA lead to right atrial appendage
6. Test RA lead parameters: P-wave sensing (>2 mV), pacing threshold (<1.5 V at 0.5 ms)
7. Secure leads with suture sleeves at venous entry point
8. Connect leads to pulse generator; place generator in pocket
9. Close pocket in layers; sterile dressing
10. Post-op CXR to confirm lead position and rule out pneumothorax
11. Program pacemaker: mode, rate, AV delay, rate response settings

## Complications

- Pneumothorax (1-2%; higher with subclavian puncture)
- Lead dislodgement (1-3% within first month)
- Pocket hematoma (2-5%; higher on anticoagulation)
- Infection: pocket infection or endocarditis (1-2% long-term)
- Cardiac perforation and tamponade (<1%)
- Subclavian vein thrombosis (1-2%)
- Lead fracture or insulation failure (long-term)
- Pacemaker syndrome (loss of AV synchrony with VVI pacing)

## India-Specific Considerations

- Pacemaker implantation widely available across India; MRP-controlled pricing since 2017
- Single-chamber pacemaker cost: INR 30,000-50,000; dual-chamber: INR 80,000-1,50,000
- Government price caps have significantly improved access to pacemaker therapy
- Conduction system pacing (His/LBBAP) adoption growing at select centers
- Leadless pacemakers (Micra) available but cost-prohibitive (INR 8-10 lakh)
- Remote monitoring adoption limited by connectivity and infrastructure
- Tropical climate and patient factors increase pocket infection risk; meticulous sterile technique essential
