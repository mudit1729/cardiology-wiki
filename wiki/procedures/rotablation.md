# Rotational Atherectomy

Rotational atherectomy (rotablation) uses a diamond-coated elliptical burr rotating at high speed to ablate calcified atherosclerotic plaque, facilitating subsequent balloon dilation and stent deployment. It operates on the principle of differential cutting, preferentially ablating hard calcified tissue while deflecting off soft elastic tissue.

## Indications

- Heavily calcified lesions that cannot be crossed or dilated with standard balloons
- Undilatable lesions after failed balloon angioplasty
- Calcified ostial lesions requiring precise plaque modification
- Calcified in-stent restenosis (select cases)
- Lesion preparation before stent deployment in severely calcified vessels

## Contraindications

- Presence of thrombus in target vessel (risk of distal embolization)
- Saphenous vein graft lesions
- Coronary dissection (may propagate)
- Vessel diameter <2.0 mm (burr cannot be safely sized)
- Severe tortuosity proximal to lesion (risk of burr entrapment)
- Last remaining vessel without hemodynamic support (relative)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT) to assess calcium arc, depth, and length
- Ensure adequate guide catheter support (7Fr minimum for 1.5 mm burr, 8Fr for larger burrs)
- Temporary pacemaker for RCA or dominant LCx lesions (risk of heart block)
- Prepare vasopressor infusion and atropine for bradycardia
- RotaPro system setup: burr selection, Dynaglide advancement mode, flush solution
- Flush solution: heparinized saline with verapamil and nitroglycerin

## Key Anatomy

- Target calcium characteristics: deep medial calcium responds best
- Avoid rotablation in segments with significant vessel angulation (>45 degrees)
- Assess landing zones proximal and distal to calcified segment
- Identify side branches at risk during burr passage

## Equipment

- RotaPro system (Boston Scientific): console, advancer, drive shaft
- Diamond-coated burrs: 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.15, 2.25, 2.38, 2.50 mm
- RotaWire: Floppy (primary) or Extra Support (for tortuous anatomy)
- Flush solution bag pressurized to 100-150 mmHg
- Temporary pacemaker (for RCA, dominant LCx, or degenerated bypass grafts)
- Guide catheter: 7Fr (burr <=1.75 mm) or 8Fr (burr >=2.0 mm)

## Technique

1. Ensure adequate guide catheter engagement with coaxial alignment
2. Cross lesion with workhorse wire; exchange to RotaWire using microcatheter or OTW balloon
3. Advance RotaPro advancer to lesion in Dynaglide mode (60,000 RPM)
4. Activate high-speed rotation (140,000-180,000 RPM for burrs <=1.75 mm)
5. Advance burr through lesion with gentle pecking motion (2-3 second runs)
6. Monitor RPM: avoid deceleration >5,000 RPM (excessive tissue engagement)
7. Allow RPM recovery between passes; limit total ablation time
8. Start with small burr (1.25-1.5 mm); step up if needed (burr-to-artery ratio 0.5-0.7)
9. Retract burr to guide catheter in Dynaglide mode after each run
10. After adequate modification: exchange to standard workhorse wire, balloon dilate, deploy stent

## Complications

- Slow flow / no reflow (most common; managed with IC nitroprusside, adenosine, or verapamil)
- Coronary perforation (1-2%; more common with oversized burrs)
- Burr entrapment (rare but serious; managed by deep guide intubation and gentle counter-rotation)
- Heart block / bradycardia (especially in RCA territory; temporary pacing)
- Coronary dissection or spasm
- Thermal injury from prolonged ablation runs
- Heat generated microparticle embolization

## India-Specific Considerations

- RotaPro system available at most large tertiary care centers across India
- Cost per burr: INR 40,000-60,000; total case cost significantly higher than standard PCI
- Single-burr strategy (1.25 mm rota-wire facilitated approach) helps reduce costs
- High prevalence of calcified disease in elderly diabetic Indian population drives demand
- Temporary pacemaker needed in many cases; adds to procedural complexity and cost
- Operator training: dedicated rotablation workshops at major Indian cardiology conferences
