# Cardiogenic Shock Management

Cardiogenic shock (CS) is a state of critical end-organ hypoperfusion due to severe cardiac dysfunction, most commonly resulting from acute myocardial infarction. Despite advances in revascularization and mechanical circulatory support, mortality remains 40-50%. A systematic, team-based approach with early hemodynamic assessment and escalation is essential.

## Indications

- Acute MI complicated by cardiogenic shock (AMI-CS): most common scenario
- Acute decompensated heart failure with hemodynamic collapse
- Post-cardiotomy shock
- Acute myocarditis with hemodynamic compromise
- Acute valvular catastrophe (acute MR, acute AR, prosthetic valve dysfunction)
- Massive pulmonary embolism with RV failure and shock

## Contraindications

- DNR/comfort care status (respect patient preferences)
- Irreversible end-organ damage without recovery potential
- Terminal malignancy with limited life expectancy (relative)
- Severe aortic regurgitation (contraindication for IABP and Impella; consider other support)

## Pre-Procedure Planning

- Rapid hemodynamic assessment: SCAI shock classification (A through E)
- Place arterial line for continuous BP monitoring and pulmonary artery catheter for hemodynamics
- Assess end-organ function: lactate, renal function, hepatic markers, mental status
- Establish team: interventional cardiologist, intensivist, cardiac surgeon, heart failure specialist
- Decide revascularization strategy: primary PCI of culprit lesion (immediate)
- Determine mechanical support needs based on hemodynamic profile

## Key Anatomy

- LV function: assess wall motion, LVEF, mechanical complications (VSD, free wall rupture, papillary muscle rupture)
- RV function: RV dilation, TAPSE, hemodynamic profile (RV vs. LV vs. biventricular shock)
- Coronary anatomy: identify culprit vessel for emergent PCI
- Assess for mechanical complications on echocardiography

## Equipment

- Inotropes and vasopressors: norepinephrine (first-line), dobutamine, milrinone, vasopressin
- Intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP): see [[iabp]]
- Impella heart pump: see [[impella]]
- VA-ECMO: percutaneous or surgical cannulation
- Pulmonary artery catheter (Swan-Ganz) for hemodynamic monitoring
- Point-of-care echocardiography

## Technique

**Initial Stabilization (SCAI Stage C-D):**
1. Secure airway if needed; avoid excessive PEEP (reduces preload)
2. Start norepinephrine for MAP target >= 65 mmHg
3. Add dobutamine or milrinone for inotropy (if MAP adequate on vasopressor)
4. Avoid fluid overload; use targeted fluid boluses only if RV-dependent physiology
5. Place arterial line and PA catheter for hemodynamic-guided management

**Hemodynamic Assessment and Classification:**
6. Obtain hemodynamic profile: CI, PCWP, SVR, PAPi (for RV function)
7. Classify phenotype: wet-cold (LV failure), isolated RV shock, biventricular
8. Calculate cardiac power output: CPO = MAP x CO / 451 (strongest predictor of mortality)

**Revascularization:**
9. Emergent PCI of culprit vessel (do not delay for multivessel PCI in acute setting)
10. Culprit-only PCI preferred in acute shock (per CULPRIT-SHOCK trial)
11. Staged multivessel PCI during index hospitalization if indicated

**Mechanical Circulatory Support Escalation:**
12. IABP: modest hemodynamic support; consider as bridge or adjunct
13. Impella CP/5.0: greater LV unloading; consider for refractory shock
14. VA-ECMO: biventricular support for refractory cardiogenic shock
15. Combination strategies (ECPELLA: ECMO + Impella for LV venting)

## Complications

- Multi-organ failure from prolonged hypoperfusion
- Limb ischemia from large-bore arterial access devices
- Hemolysis from mechanical support devices (Impella)
- Bleeding complications from antithrombotic therapy and access sites
- Infection from indwelling catheters and devices
- Futile escalation of support without recovery potential

## Key Trials

- [[CULPRIT-SHOCK]] - Culprit-only vs. multivessel PCI in AMI-CS
- [[IABP-SHOCK-II]] - IABP did not reduce mortality in AMI-CS
- [[DanGer-Shock]] - Impella CP in AMI-CS (first RCT showing MCS mortality benefit)
- [[ECLS-SHOCK]] - ECMO in AMI-CS
- [[SHOCK]] - Early revascularization in AMI-CS

## India-Specific Considerations

- AMI-CS mortality in India exceeds global averages due to delayed presentation
- IABP widely available across Indian cardiac centers; Impella limited to select centers
- VA-ECMO availability expanding in metropolitan centers but remains limited
- Cost of mechanical support: IABP ~INR 50,000; Impella ~INR 8-12 lakh; ECMO ~INR 5-10 lakh/day
- Hub-and-spoke model for CS: transfer protocols from spoke to hub centers needed
- PA catheter use for hemodynamic monitoring underutilized; promotes algorithmic approach
- SCAI shock classification adoption in Indian ICUs improving standardization
