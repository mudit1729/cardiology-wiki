# OPTIMAL Trial: IVUS-Guided PCI in Unprotected Left Main Coronary Artery Disease

> **2026 Results Now Available — see "OPTIMAL 2026 Results" section below.**
> The body of this page summarizes the original 2022 study **protocol**.
> Final results from the completed trial were published in NEJM on March 30, 2026
> and presented at ACC.26.

## Clinical Question
In patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for unprotected left main coronary artery (LMCA) disease, does an intravascular ultrasound (IVUS)-guided strategy reduce clinical events compared to standard angiography-guided PCI?

## PICO

| Component      | Description                                                                                                           |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Population** | Patients ≥18 years with *de novo* unprotected LMCA disease (ostial, shaft, or distal) or ostial LAD/LCx compatible with one Medina class of LM disease; silent ischemia, stable angina, unstable angina, or NSTEMI |
| **Intervention** | IVUS-guided PCI with mandatory post-procedural IVUS and predefined optimization criteria (Kang's criteria: MSA >8 mm² in LMCA body, >7 mm² at polygon of confluence, >6 mm² at LAD ostium, >5 mm² at LCx ostium) |
| **Comparator** | Qualitative angiography-guided PCI (IVUS strongly discouraged unless required for safety)                           |
| **Outcome**    | Primary: Patient-oriented Composite Endpoint (PoCE) at 2 years — all-cause death, any stroke, any MI, any repeat revascularization |

## Protocol Design
- **Original protocol status:** PLoS ONE 2022 study protocol.
- **Planned sample size:** 800 patients (1:1 randomization).
- **Assumptions for power calculation:** Expected 2-year PoCE of 17% in IVUS-guided arm vs. 26% in angiography-guided arm, anticipating a 35% relative risk reduction with IVUS guidance (based on prior observational data), with 80% statistical power (two-sided α=0.05).
- **Expected cross-over rates:** 4% (angio→IVUS), 3% (IVUS→angio).
- **Primary analysis:** Modified intention-to-treat (all randomized patients who underwent PCI).
- **Key secondary outcomes:** Device-oriented composite endpoint (cardiovascular death, target-vessel MI, clinically-indicated target lesion revascularization), stent thrombosis (ARC II definition), and hospitalization for heart failure.

## What Changed?
- The protocol was an important attempt to generate definitive randomized evidence for IVUS guidance in LMCA PCI.
- At the protocol stage, European guidelines provided a **class IIa, level B** recommendation for IVUS in LMCA evaluation and stent optimization; the completed OPTIMAL results should now be considered alongside that older context.
- The protocol mandates **specific, quantifiable IVUS optimization targets** (Kang's criteria) rather than subjective assessment, addressing a gap in prior observational studies.
- Unlike prior evidence limited to observational data (e.g., MAIN-COMPARE registry, IVUS-TRONCO-ICP study) and subgroup analyses (EXCEL, NOBLE), this is a randomized trial with IVUS as the primary objective.

## What Did Not Change?
- The comparator was **qualitative angiography-guided PCI**, which remains common and was not inferior in the completed OPTIMAL trial.
- The protocol does **not** compare IVUS against other imaging modalities like OCT.
- The trial excludes STEMI or cardiogenic shock patients, thus **not** addressing IVUS in these scenarios.
- Only everolimus-eluting stents (Synergy/Synergy Megatron) are mandated, so the study does **not** evaluate other stent types.
- The protocol does **not** test functional assessment tools (FFR/iFR) against IVUS for lesion severity assessment.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient
The left main artery is a critical blood vessel supplying your heart. When placing a stent there, doctors may use X-ray dye pictures alone or add intravascular ultrasound (IVUS), a tiny ultrasound probe inside the artery, to check plaque, stent size, and stent expansion. The completed OPTIMAL trial did not show better clinical outcomes with routine IVUS guidance compared with angiography guidance for left main PCI overall, so IVUS should be considered in the context of anatomy, operator judgment, cost, and other complex-PCI evidence.

## Relevance for Indian Practice
- **Cost considerations:** IVUS catheters add significant procedural cost (₹40,000-60,000 per catheter), a major barrier in India where many patients pay out-of-pocket.
- **Availability:** IVUS is mostly limited to tertiary cardiac centers in metropolitan areas; rural and smaller centers often lack the equipment and trained personnel.
- **Training implications:** The protocol requires expertise in IVUS interpretation and optimization, highlighting a training gap in India, though high-volume operators in major centers are increasingly skilled.
- **Follow-up feasibility:** The 2-year follow-up with hospital visits at 1 and 2 years may be challenging due to patient migration, loss to follow-up, and limited infrastructure for long-term tracking.
- **Relevance to Indian anatomy:** Kang's criteria, developed in Asian populations with smaller LMCA dimensions (5.2±1.8 mm² vs. 6.2±1.4 mm² in Western patients, p<0.0001), may be more applicable to Indian patients.
- **Completed result:** The trial did not show superiority for routine IVUS guidance in unprotected LMCA PCI. In India, this makes selective use based on anatomy, uncertainty, operator experience, and affordability more defensible than a blanket "IVUS for every left-main PCI" policy.

## Related Trials
- **MAIN-COMPARE Registry:** Observational data suggesting better outcomes with IVUS-guided PCI in LMCA disease.
- **IVUS-TRONCO-ICP Study:** Observational study supporting IVUS use in complex coronary interventions.
- **EXCEL and NOBLE Trials:** Subgroup analyses indicating potential benefits of IVUS in LMCA PCI, though not as the primary focus.

## OPTIMAL 2026 Results

The completed OPTIMAL trial was published in NEJM on **March 30, 2026** and presented at ACC.26
([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41911017/),
[ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2026/03/25/21/27/mon-1045am-combo-acc-2026)).

### Headline Result

- **806 patients** randomized with unprotected left main coronary artery disease.
- At median **2.9 years** of follow-up, the primary composite endpoint occurred in:
  - **33.7%** of the IVUS-guided PCI group
  - **30.9%** of the angiography-guided PCI group
  - HR **1.11**, 95% CI 0.87–1.42, **p = 0.40**
- **Conclusion:** IVUS-guided PCI **did not** show a benefit over angiography-guided PCI for the
  composite of stroke, MI, repeat revascularization, or all-cause death in unprotected LMCA disease.

### What This Means

- The protocol's pre-specified expectation of a 35% relative risk reduction with IVUS was **not** observed.
- For unprotected LMCA PCI specifically, this result tempers the previously expected magnitude of benefit
  from routine IVUS guidance.
- Class IIa, level B European guideline recommendations for IVUS in LMCA evaluation may be
  re-evaluated as this evidence is incorporated.
- Note: this does **not** invalidate IVUS use in other complex PCI scenarios — separate trials such as
  RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI, ULTIMATE, ILUMIEN IV (OCT), and OCTOBER (OCT, bifurcations) report
  different findings in different anatomic contexts.
