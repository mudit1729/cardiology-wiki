# Bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation for patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (ABLATION): rationale, design and study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial

## Paper Type
This BMJ Open article is a rationale, design, and study protocol paper for the ABLATION multicentre randomised controlled trial. It does not report completed trial outcomes.

## Clinical Question
In patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and persistent or long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation undergoing mitral valve surgery, does bi-atrial ablation improve rhythm outcomes compared with left atrial ablation alone without increasing permanent pacemaker implantation?

## PICO

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Population | Adults aged >=18 years with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation undergoing mitral valve surgery. Key exclusions include paroxysmal AF, prior AF ablation, left atrial diameter >70 mm, right ventricular dysfunction, moderate-to-severe tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary artery systolic pressure >60 mmHg, and need for CABG. |
| Intervention | Bi-atrial ablation using a Cox-Maze IV lesion set with right atrial lesions plus the left atrial lesion set, performed during mitral valve surgery with standardised radiofrequency clamp/pen technique. |
| Comparator | Left atrial ablation alone, including pulmonary vein isolation, posterior left atrial isolation, left atrial appendage management, and mitral isthmus-related lesions. |
| Outcome | Primary endpoint: freedom from AF, atrial flutter, or atrial tachycardia lasting >=30 seconds at 12 months after surgery, off antiarrhythmic drugs and after the 3-month blanking period. Key secondary endpoint: freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation at 12 months. |

## Key Results
No outcome results are reported in this protocol. No clinical practice change should be inferred until the trial results are published.

### Design Assumptions
The planned sample size is 320 patients randomised 1:1. The sample-size calculation assumes 70% freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias at 12 months in the left atrial ablation group and 85% in the bi-atrial ablation group. These percentages are assumptions for power calculation, not reported trial results. The key secondary endpoint uses an assumed 97% freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation in the left atrial group and a non-inferiority margin of -5%.

## What Changed?
Nothing changes in clinical practice yet. The protocol describes a trial designed to answer an unresolved surgical question in rheumatic mitral valve disease: whether adding right atrial lesions to left atrial ablation improves rhythm control without an unacceptable pacemaker penalty.

## What Did Not Change?
The optimal surgical lesion set for AF ablation in rheumatic mitral valve disease remains unproven. The risk-benefit tradeoff between broader bi-atrial ablation and permanent pacemaker implantation remains the central uncertainty.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient
During mitral valve surgery, surgeons can also treat atrial fibrillation by creating lines of scar tissue to block abnormal electrical signals. This study compares treating both upper chambers of the heart with treating only the left upper chamber. The study is still a protocol here, so it tells us how the trial will be run, not which approach is better.

## Relevance for Indian Practice
The question is relevant in India because rheumatic heart disease and rheumatic mitral valve disease remain common, and AF in this setting is strongly linked to stroke and heart failure. The protocol's applicability depends on surgical expertise with Cox-Maze IV lesion sets, access to reliable rhythm follow-up such as Holter monitoring, and the cost/availability implications of any increase in permanent pacemaker implantation. Warfarin-based anticoagulation after valve surgery remains relevant to Indian practice, especially when mechanical valves or rheumatic mitral stenosis are involved.

## Related Trials
- Wang et al. (2014) is cited by the protocol as the only prior RCT comparing bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation in AF with mitral valve disease; it did not establish clear superiority of bi-atrial ablation and had limited pacemaker data.
