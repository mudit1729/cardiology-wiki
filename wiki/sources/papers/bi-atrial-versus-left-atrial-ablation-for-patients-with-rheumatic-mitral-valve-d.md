# ABLATION Trial: Bi-atrial vs Left Atrial Ablation in Rheumatic MV Disease

## Clinical Question
In rheumatic MV disease patients with persistent AF undergoing valve surgery, does bi-atrial ablation improve rhythm outcomes versus left atrial ablation alone?

## PICO

| Component        | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| **Population**   | Adults with rheumatic MV disease and non-paroxysmal AF undergoing MV surgery |
| **Intervention** | Bi-atrial Cox-Maze IV ablation (right + left atrial lesion sets) |
| **Comparator**   | Left atrial ablation alone (PVI, posterior wall, LAA, mitral isthmus) |
| **Outcome**      | Freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia ≥30s at 12 months, off AADs |

## Key Results
No outcome results are reported — this is a protocol paper only. No practice change should be inferred.

### Design Assumptions
- **Sample size**: 320 patients, 1:1 randomization across 19 Chinese centers
- **Primary endpoint assumption**: 70% rhythm success (LA-only) vs 85% (bi-atrial)
- **Pacemaker non-inferiority**: assumed 97% freedom from PPM in LA group, −5% margin
- **Timeline**: recruitment from May 2022, follow-up through April 2025

## What Changed?
- **No practice change yet** — protocol only, no results available
- **Formal RCT design** addresses an unresolved question: whether adding right atrial lesions improves rhythm control without unacceptable pacemaker risk
- **Standardized lesion sets** using RF clamp/pen technique may improve reproducibility across centers

## What Did Not Change?
- **Optimal AF lesion set in rheumatic MV disease** remains unproven
- **Bi-atrial vs LA-only tradeoff** (rhythm benefit vs pacemaker risk) is still the central uncertainty
- **No head-to-head data** yet to guide surgical ablation strategy at time of MV surgery

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient
During your valve surgery, surgeons can also treat your irregular heartbeat by creating scar lines that block abnormal signals. This study is comparing treating both upper chambers versus only the left one. Results aren't out yet, so we don't know which is better.

## Relevance for Indian Practice
- **Population relevance**: Rheumatic MV disease with persistent AF remains common across India
- **Cost**: Pacemaker implantation if needed adds ₹60,000–₹2,00,000+ to surgical cost
- **Training**: Cox-Maze IV requires specialized surgical expertise, limited to high-volume centers
- **Follow-up**: Serial Holter monitoring and echo follow-up at 3, 6, 12 months may be challenging outside tertiary centers

## Related Trials
- **Wang et al. (2014)** — only prior RCT of bi-atrial vs LA ablation with MV surgery; no clear superiority for bi-atrial, limited pacemaker data
