# Bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation for patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (ABLATION): rationale, design and study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial

## Clinical Question

In adults with [[../../concepts/rheumatic_mitral_valve_disease|rheumatic mitral valve disease]] and persistent or long-standing persistent [[../../concepts/atrial_fibrillation|atrial fibrillation]] undergoing mitral valve surgery, does adding right atrial lesions to a standard left atrial surgical ablation strategy improve freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia at 12 months, without increasing the need for permanent pacemaker implantation?

## PICO

| Component        | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| **Population**   | Adults aged **≥18 years** with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal AF — persistent or long-standing persistent AF — undergoing mitral valve surgery with concomitant surgical ablation. Rheumatic mitral valve disease is defined by history of acute rheumatic fever, valve morphology, echocardiographic findings, intraoperative findings, and/or pathology, including leaflet thickening/retraction, commissural fusion, and/or chordal fusion/shortening. |
| **Intervention** | **Bi-atrial ablation** using Cox-Maze IV-type lesion sets: right atrial lesions plus a left atrial lesion set, performed with standardized radiofrequency bipolar clamp and radiofrequency pen techniques during mitral valve surgery. |
| **Comparator**   | **Left atrial ablation alone** using the standardized left atrial lesion set during mitral valve surgery. |
| **Outcome**      | Primary planned endpoint: freedom from any documented atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence — AF, atrial flutter, or atrial tachycardia lasting **≥30 seconds** — off antiarrhythmic drugs at **12 months** after surgery, assessed by **3-day Holter monitoring** after a **3-month blanking period**. Key secondary endpoint: freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation at **12 months**. Other secondary outcomes include freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence with antiarrhythmic drugs, AF burden, adverse events, and echocardiographic cardiac function at **12 months**. |

## Key Results

- **No clinical outcome results:** This is a BMJ Open 2022 study protocol/design paper; **no outcome results are reported**.
- **Trial design:** ABLATION is planned as a **prospective, multicentre, open-label, two-arm, single-blind, parallel randomized controlled trial**.
- **Planned sample size:** **320 patients** will be randomized **1:1** to bi-atrial ablation versus left atrial ablation.
- **Study sites:** Recruitment is planned across **19 large academic cardiac centres in mainland China**.
- **Randomization:** Randomization is stratified by centre and balanced using randomly permuted blocks of **4 or 6 patients**, with allocation concealment through an interactive web-based response system.
- **Blinding:** Surgeons are aware of treatment allocation; participants and research staff are blinded to allocation.
- **Timeline:** Recruitment began in **May 2022**, with planned completion of recruitment by **end of April 2024** and follow-up by **end of April 2025**.
- **Primary endpoint definition:** Freedom from AF, atrial flutter, or atrial tachycardia lasting **≥30 seconds**, off antiarrhythmic drugs, at **12 months** after surgery, assessed by **3-day Holter monitoring** after a **3-month blanking period**.
- **Key secondary endpoint:** Freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation at **12 months**.
- **Sample-size assumption for rhythm outcome:** Investigators assumed freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia at **12 months** would be **70%** in the left atrial ablation group and **85%** in the bi-atrial ablation group.
- **Sample-size assumption for pacemaker outcome:** Investigators assumed freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation at **12 months** would be **97%** in the left atrial group.
- **Non-inferiority margin:** The non-inferiority margin for freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation was set at **−5%**.
- **Withdrawal allowance:** The total planned sample size of **320 patients** accounts for both the primary and key secondary endpoints and a **10% withdrawal rate**.
- **Key exclusions:** Exclusions include paroxysmal AF, degenerative or ischaemic mitral valve disease, prior catheter or surgical AF ablation, absolute contraindication to anticoagulation, left atrial thrombosis, left atrial diameter **>70 mm**, moderate-to-severe tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary artery systolic pressure **>60 mm Hg**, right ventricular dysfunction, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, uncontrolled thyroid disease, and need for coronary artery bypass grafting in patients with coronary heart disease.
- **Follow-up plan:** At **3 months**, patients undergo remote video interview, questionnaire, and **24-hour Holter monitoring**; at **6 months**, remote video interview, questionnaire, and **3-day Holter monitoring**; at **12 months**, in-person hospital visit or remote video interview, questionnaire, **3-day Holter monitoring**, and echocardiography.
- **Core-lab assessment:** Holters, 12-lead ECGs, and echocardiograms are to be analysed by a blinded core laboratory.

## What Changed?

Nothing changes in clinical practice yet. This paper is a protocol, not a completed clinical trial, and it does **not** report whether bi-atrial ablation improves rhythm control compared with left atrial ablation alone. It also does **not** show whether adding right atrial lesions increases or does not increase permanent pacemaker implantation, perioperative complications, or longer-term adverse events. The trial is important because rheumatic mitral valve disease with non-paroxysmal AF is a distinct population with atrial remodeling that may not behave like degenerative mitral valve disease or non-valvular AF. However, the assumed benefit — **85%** freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia with bi-atrial ablation versus **70%** with left atrial ablation — remains a planning assumption, not a proven result. No practice change should be inferred until the randomized outcome data are available.

## What Did Not Change?

For patients undergoing mitral valve surgery with persistent or long-standing persistent AF, the choice of surgical ablation lesion set remains individualized and should depend on surgical expertise, atrial size, valve pathology, expected operative risk, and institutional protocols. Left atrial ablation remains a commonly used approach during mitral valve surgery, while bi-atrial ablation is often considered when more extensive atrial substrate is suspected, but this protocol does not prove that one strategy is clinically superior in rheumatic disease. The concern that more extensive right atrial lesions could increase sinus node dysfunction, atrioventricular conduction problems, or permanent pacemaker requirement also remains unresolved. The trial specifically excludes several higher-risk groups, including patients with left atrial diameter **>70 mm**, pulmonary artery systolic pressure **>60 mm Hg**, moderate-to-severe tricuspid regurgitation, and right ventricular dysfunction, so its eventual findings may not automatically apply to all rheumatic mitral patients seen in routine practice. Anticoagulation decisions, valve surgery decisions, and postoperative rhythm surveillance remain guided by existing standards rather than by this protocol.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

“You have rheumatic mitral valve disease and atrial fibrillation, and when we operate on the mitral valve, we can also create small ablation lines in the heart to try to reduce future AF. Some surgeons treat only the left upper chamber, while others also treat the right upper chamber. This study is designed to find out whether treating both upper chambers gives better rhythm control without causing more patients to need a pacemaker. But the study paper only describes the plan — it does not yet tell us which approach is better. So for now, we would still decide the ablation strategy based on your heart’s structure, your operation, and the surgical team’s experience.”

## Relevance for Indian Practice

This protocol is highly relevant to India because rheumatic mitral valve disease remains common in many Indian regions, and many patients present with persistent or long-standing persistent AF at the time of mitral valve surgery. If the ABLATION trial eventually shows a clear rhythm benefit without excess pacemaker implantation, it could influence surgical ablation practice in Indian cardiac centres that already perform mitral valve replacement or repair for rheumatic disease. However, implementation would require availability of surgical radiofrequency ablation systems, including bipolar clamps and RF pens, which may add substantial procedural cost; depending on hospital procurement and whether devices are reusable or single-use, the incremental cost can be significant and may not be uniformly reimbursed in public-sector or insurance-based care. Permanent pacemaker implantation, if needed, also carries major cost implications in India, often ranging from approximately **₹60,000–₹2,00,000+** depending on device type, hospital sector, and follow-up requirements. Training is important because a Cox-Maze IV-type bi-atrial lesion set requires standardized lesion creation, coordination with valve surgery, and awareness of conduction-system risk. Follow-up feasibility is another concern: this protocol uses structured visits, ECGs, **24-hour Holter**, **3-day Holter**, echocardiography, and core-lab interpretation, whereas many Indian patients may have travel, cost, or access barriers to repeated Holter monitoring. The population relevance is strong, but the trial is being conducted in mainland China, and Indian applicability will depend on similarity in patient age, atrial size, valve pathology severity, pulmonary hypertension burden, surgical expertise, and follow-up infrastructure.

## Related Trials

- **ABLATION trial** — The multicentre randomized trial described in this protocol; compares bi-atrial Cox-Maze IV-type surgical ablation with left atrial ablation alone during mitral valve surgery in patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal AF. No clinical outcome results are reported in this protocol.
- **Cox-Maze IV surgical ablation experience** — The lesion strategy referenced by the intervention; relevant because the ABLATION trial uses Cox-Maze IV-type bi-atrial and left atrial lesion sets with radiofrequency tools, but this protocol is designed to test the approach specifically in rheumatic mitral valve disease.
- **Surgical ablation during mitral valve surgery studies** — Prior studies and practice patterns support concomitant surgical ablation during mitral valve surgery in selected patients with AF, but this protocol addresses the unresolved question of whether a bi-atrial lesion set is preferable to a left atrial-only strategy in rheumatic, non-paroxysmal AF.
