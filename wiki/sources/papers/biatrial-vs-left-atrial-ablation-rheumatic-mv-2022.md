# Bi-atrial versus Left Atrial Ablation in Rheumatic Mitral Valve Disease and Non-paroxysmal AF (ABLATION Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation undergoing mitral valve surgery, is bi-atrial ablation superior to left atrial ablation alone for achieving freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias at 12 months without increasing the risk of permanent pacemaker implantation?

## PICO

| Component      | Description                                                                                                           |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Population** | Patients ≥18 years with rheumatic mitral valve disease and persistent or long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation undergoing mitral valve surgery (LA diameter ≤70 mm, no RV dysfunction, no moderate-severe TR, PASP ≤60 mmHg) |
| **Intervention** | Bi-atrial ablation (Cox-Maze IV lesion set including right atrial lesions: SVC to IVC line, RAA clamp, tricuspid annulus line; plus full left atrial lesion set) |
| **Comparator** | Left atrial ablation alone (pulmonary vein isolation, LA roof/floor lines, LAA amputation, mitral isthmus line)       |
| **Outcome**    | **Primary:** Freedom from any recurrence of atrial tachyarrhythmias (AF, atrial flutter, AT ≥30s) off antiarrhythmic drugs at 12 months on 3-day Holter. **Key secondary:** Freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation at 12 months |

## Key Results
This is a study protocol; no results are available yet. The trial began recruitment in May 2022, with expected completion of recruitment by April 2024 and follow-up by April 2025. The planned sample size is 320 patients (1:1 randomization, 144 per group + 10% withdrawal). Power calculations assume a primary endpoint of 70% freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias in the left atrial group versus 85% in the bi-atrial group (90% power, one-sided α=0.05), and a key secondary endpoint of non-inferiority for permanent pacemaker implantation with a -5% margin (80% power, one-sided α=0.05). Hierarchical testing ensures the secondary endpoint is tested only if the primary endpoint is significant, preserving overall type I error at 5%.

## What Changed?
This trial is the first multicentre RCT with a large sample size specifically evaluating bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation in rheumatic mitral valve disease, distinct from degenerative mitral valve disease. Prior evidence was limited, with the only previous RCT (Wang et al., 2014) showing no superiority of bi-atrial ablation (p=0.09) and inconclusive pacemaker data due to small sample size. The trial mandates unified ablation tools (bipolar radiofrequency clamp + pen) and standardized lesion sets across 19 centres, with video review of surgeon technique before initiation. Patients with significant right heart pathology (RV dysfunction, moderate-severe TR, PASP >60 mmHg) are excluded, recognizing that left atrial ablation alone would be unethical in those with severe right atrial remodelling.

## What Did Not Change?
The optimal lesion set for surgical AF ablation in rheumatic mitral valve disease remains unproven until trial results are available. Whether bi-atrial ablation increases the risk of permanent pacemaker implantation is unresolved and is the key secondary endpoint. The blanking period (first 3 months post-op) remains standard, with recurrences during this period not counted. Anticoagulation protocol is unchanged, using warfarin for 3 months post-op routinely and lifetime for mechanical valves. Antiarrhythmic drugs are prescribed only for 2 months if perioperative AF/flutter occurs.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient
If you have rheumatic heart disease affecting your mitral valve and also have persistent atrial fibrillation, surgeons can perform a procedure called ablation during your valve surgery. Ablation creates scar lines in the heart to block abnormal electrical signals causing irregular heartbeats. There’s a question about whether to treat just the left upper chamber of the heart or both upper chambers. Treating the right chamber as well might increase the chance of needing a permanent pacemaker later. This study randomly assigns patients to one of these two approaches and monitors their heart rhythm and need for a pacemaker over one year. The results will help us find the best strategy for patients like you with rheumatic valve disease, which may differ from other types of valve problems.

## Relevance for Indian Practice
This trial is highly relevant to India, where rheumatic heart disease remains endemic, with about one-third of patients developing atrial fibrillation and 80% of strokes in RHD occurring in those with mitral stenosis and AF. Bi-atrial ablation adds procedural time but no extra device cost (using the same bipolar RF clamp + pen); however, potential pacemaker implantation, if more frequent with bi-atrial ablation, would add significant cost. The trial requires experienced surgeons proficient in Cox-Maze IV technique, with high hospital and surgeon case volumes (>100 and >20 cases annually, respectively), potentially limiting applicability to high-volume Indian centres. Follow-up using 3-day Holter monitoring at 6 and 12 months (mailed devices) and remote video consultations offers a practical model for Indian practice if similar infrastructure exists. Key exclusion criteria (LA >70 mm, RV dysfunction, moderate-severe TR, PASP >60 mmHg) are common in advanced RHD in India, so results may not apply to the sickest patients. The warfarin-based anticoagulation protocol aligns with Indian practice, where NOACs are often not indicated for rheumatic mitral stenosis.

## Related Trials
- Wang et al. (2014): The only prior RCT comparing bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation in atrial fibrillation with mitral valve disease, which showed no significant superiority of bi-atrial ablation (p=0.09) but was limited by small sample size and inconclusive data on pacemaker implantation risk. Further related trials will be updated as results from the ABLATION trial become available.
