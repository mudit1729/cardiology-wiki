# Single Antiplatelet or Anticoagulant Followed by Antiplatelet After TAVR (TAORTA Trial Protocol)

## Clinical Question
In patients without anticoagulation indications who undergo successful transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), is early 6-month use of oral anticoagulation (warfarin) superior to single-antiplatelet therapy (aspirin) for preventing bioprosthetic valve thrombosis and improving net clinical outcomes?

## PICO

| Population | Intervention | Comparator | Outcome |
|------------|--------------|------------|---------|
| Patients aged 18–85 years with severe aortic stenosis who underwent successful TAVR via femoral approach with self-expanding bioprosthetic valve, without anticoagulation indications | Oral anticoagulation (warfarin, target INR 1.8–2.5) for 6 months, followed by aspirin 75–100 mg for 6 months | Single-antiplatelet therapy (aspirin 75–100 mg) for 6 months, followed by aspirin 75–100 mg for 6 months | **Primary:** Net clinical benefit (composite of all-cause mortality, MI, stroke/TIA, peripheral artery thrombosis, intracardiac thrombosis, major bleeding [BARC 3a], disabling/life-threatening bleeding [BARC 3b,3c,5]) at 1 year; Bioprosthetic valve thrombosis (BPVT) on 4D-CT at 6 and 12 months |

## Key Results
This is a protocol paper (BMJ Open 2023) with no results available yet. The TAORTA trial is ongoing with the following design parameters:

- **Sample size:** 650 patients (325 per group) from 13 TAVR centers in China.
- **Assumed event rates:** Net clinical benefit endpoint ~28% with SAPT vs ~16.9% with OAC; subclinical leaflet thrombosis ~15% with antiplatelet vs ~4% with OAC.
- **Power:** 90% at alpha=0.05 (two-sided).
- **Primary analysis:** Intention-to-treat; hazard ratios with 95% CI; Kaplan-Meier curves with log-rank test.
- **Enrolment period:** First patient enrolled in 2023, completion expected in 2025, results anticipated in 2026.

## What Changed?
- No practice change yet, as this is a protocol for an ongoing trial.
- The TAORTA trial is the first randomized controlled trial of antithrombotic strategies specifically in Chinese TAVR patients, evaluating both clinical events and 4D-CT-based bioprosthetic valve thrombosis (BPVT) endpoints.
- It addresses the evidence gap for early (6-month) use of oral anticoagulation (OAC) versus single-antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) in patients without anticoagulation indications.
- The trial uses warfarin (not DOACs) with a lower INR target (1.8–2.5) to balance efficacy and bleeding risk in an Asian population.

## What Did Not Change?
- The optimal antithrombotic strategy after TAVR in patients without anticoagulation indications remains uncertain.
- Long-term OAC continues to be associated with increased bleeding risk.
- The role of warfarin (versus DOACs) in preventing BPVT is not yet established.
- Whether early OAC followed by SAPT improves valve durability or long-term outcomes remains unproven.
- The clinical significance of subclinical leaflet thrombosis (HALT/RLM) is still controversial.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient
"After your aortic valve replacement, we need to decide which blood thinner is best to prevent clots on the new valve. This study compares two options: taking aspirin alone for 6 months, or taking warfarin, a stronger blood thinner, for 6 months before switching to aspirin. Both groups take aspirin after 6 months. The study uses special CT scans to check for tiny clots on the valve that regular tests might miss. We're trying to find the best balance between preventing clots and avoiding bleeding. The results aren't out yet, as the study is still enrolling patients in China."

## Relevance for Indian Practice
- **High relevance** due to the increasing TAVR volume in India and the high prevalence of bicuspid aortic valve disease, similar to the Chinese population (47.5% in China).
- **Cost considerations:** Aspirin is inexpensive and widely available; warfarin is also low-cost but requires regular INR monitoring, which may be challenging in resource-limited settings.
- **Follow-up challenges:** The protocol mandates 4D-CT at 6 and 12 months, which may not be feasible in many Indian centers due to cost and availability.
- **Bleeding risk:** Indian patients may have lower BMI and higher frailty, potentially increasing bleeding risk with OAC; the trial’s lower INR target (1.8–2.5) is relevant.
- **Practice implications:** If OAC proves superior, warfarin-based strategies could be implemented at low drug cost in India, though INR monitoring infrastructure would need strengthening.
- The trial addresses Asian-specific anatomical characteristics (bicuspid valves, heavy calcification) that align with Indian TAVR populations.
