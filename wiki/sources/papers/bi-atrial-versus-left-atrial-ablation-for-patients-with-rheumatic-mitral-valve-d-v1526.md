# Bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation for patients with rheumatic mitral valve disease and non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (ABLATION): rationale, design and study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial

## Clinical Question

Is bi-atrial ablation superior to left atrial ablation for reducing atrial tachyarrhythmias in rheumatic mitral valve disease with non-paroxysmal AF?

## PICO

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Population** | Adults with rheumatic mitral valve disease and persistent AF undergoing mitral valve surgery |
| **Intervention** | Bi-atrial ablation during mitral valve surgery |
| **Comparator** | Left atrial ablation during mitral valve surgery |
| **Outcome** | Freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias and permanent pacemaker implantation at 12 months |

## Key Results

- **Outcomes:** No clinical outcomes reported yet; this is a study protocol.
- **Practice impact:** No efficacy or safety conclusions can be drawn.

### Design Assumptions

- **Sample size:** 320 patients.
- **Power:** 90% for the primary endpoint; 80% for the secondary endpoint.
- **Timeline:** Recruitment by April 2024; follow-up by April 2025.

## What Changed?

- **Research focus:** Establishes a large RCT comparing bi-atrial versus left atrial ablation in rheumatic mitral valve disease.
- **Guideline potential:** May inform future surgical AF ablation guidance after outcomes are reported.

## What Did Not Change?

- **Clinical practice:** No current change; outcomes are not yet available.
- **Safety uncertainty:** Permanent pacemaker implantation risk remains unproven.
- **Efficacy uncertainty:** Superiority of bi-atrial ablation over left atrial ablation remains unknown.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

During mitral valve surgery, surgeons can treat irregular heartbeat using different ablation patterns. This study compares a more extensive approach with a left-sided approach. We do not yet know which is better or safer. The results may help future patients choose the best option.

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Cost:** Procedure-cost impact not reported; ₹ figures not specified.
- **Availability:** Relevant where rheumatic mitral surgery and surgical AF ablation are available.
- **Training:** Requires surgeons comfortable with both bi-atrial and left atrial ablation techniques.
- **Population relevance:** Highly relevant given India’s rheumatic heart disease burden.
