# 2024 ESC Atrial Fibrillation Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. AF classification simplified into a new clinical staging framework: Stage 1 (risk factors present), Stage 2 (pre-AF with atrial abnormalities), Stage 3 (AF diagnosed, subdivided 3a paroxysmal through 3d permanent), Stage 4 (permanent AF with accepted rate control).
2. DOACs are now the exclusive first-line anticoagulant for non-valvular AF; warfarin is explicitly downgraded except for mechanical valves and moderate-severe mitral stenosis.
3. CHA2DS2-VA score replaces CHA2DS2-VASc, dropping the sex category (Sc) from the scoring system. Sex is no longer a risk modifier; anticoagulation threshold is score >=2 (Class I) or >=1 (Class IIa).
4. Early rhythm control strategy recommended for all symptomatic AF patients within the first year of diagnosis, reflecting EAST-AFNET 4 and EARLY-AF data showing improved outcomes.
5. Catheter ablation as first-line therapy (Class IIa) for rhythm control in selected patients with paroxysmal AF, superseding the traditional drug-first approach.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| DOAC over warfarin | DOACs preferred for stroke prevention in eligible AF patients | I | A | Apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, or dabigatran |
| Anticoagulation if CHA2DS2-VA >=2 | Oral anticoagulation recommended | I | A | Do not use antiplatelet as substitute for anticoagulation |
| Rate control target | Lenient rate control (<110 bpm) acceptable initially | I | B | Strict control (<80 bpm) only if symptomatic despite lenient control |
| Screening for AF in stroke | Prolonged rhythm monitoring (>=72 hours) after cryptogenic stroke | I | B | Insertable cardiac monitors detect more AF than short-term Holter |
| Integrated AF management | AF-CARE approach: Comorbidity, Anticoagulation, Rate/Rhythm, Evaluation | I | B | Structured multidisciplinary approach improves outcomes |
| Cardiovascular risk factor management | Address obesity, hypertension, OSA, diabetes, alcohol | I | B | Weight loss >10% reduces AF burden significantly |
| Echocardiography | TTE at diagnosis to assess structure and function | I | C | Rule out valvular disease, assess LA size, LV function |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) | May be considered when long-term OAC is contraindicated | LAAOS III showed benefit in surgical setting; percutaneous LAAO (Watchman) lacks definitive superiority data over DOACs |
| AF screening in general population | Opportunistic screening in >=65 years | Systematic screening may detect AF, but treatment benefit in screen-detected asymptomatic AF is less established |
| Ablation for HFrEF | Catheter ablation may improve outcomes in AF with HFrEF | CASTLE-AF positive, but results not consistently replicated; patient selection matters |
| Pill-in-the-pocket flecainide | Self-administered flecainide for infrequent paroxysmal AF | Requires initial supervised trial; contraindicated in structural heart disease |
| Upstream therapy with MRA | Spironolactone may reduce new-onset AF in HF patients | Indirect evidence; not a primary AF prevention strategy |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Apixaban**: 5 mg BID (2.5 mg BID if >=2 of: age >=80, weight <=60 kg, creatinine >=1.5 mg/dL); best safety profile among DOACs
- **Rivaroxaban**: 20 mg daily with food (15 mg if CrCl 15-49 mL/min); once-daily dosing aids compliance
- **Dabigatran**: 150 mg BID (110 mg BID if age >=80 or high bleeding risk); only DOAC with reversal agent (idarucizumab)
- **Edoxaban**: 60 mg daily (30 mg if CrCl 15-50, weight <=60 kg, or on P-gp inhibitors)
- **Beta-blockers**: Metoprolol, bisoprolol for rate control; first-line in most patients
- **Diltiazem/Verapamil**: Rate control alternative; avoid in HFrEF
- **Amiodarone**: Most effective for rhythm control but significant long-term toxicity; reserved for patients with structural heart disease
- **Flecainide/Propafenone**: For rhythm control in AF without structural heart disease
- **Dronedarone**: Moderate efficacy; safer than amiodarone but contraindicated in HFrEF and permanent AF

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: All four DOACs are available in India. Apixaban and rivaroxaban generics cost INR 15-30/day, making them increasingly accessible. Dabigatran is the most affordable DOAC (INR 10-20/day). Warfarin remains widely used due to familiarity, low cost (INR 1-2/day), and availability of INR monitoring at primary health centers.
- **Cost considerations**: The shift from warfarin to DOACs is happening but unevenly. Urban patients increasingly receive DOACs; rural patients often remain on warfarin. Lack of DOAC reversal agents (except idarucizumab, which is expensive at INR 1-2 lakh/dose) is a concern. DOAC levels cannot be routinely monitored, which some physicians find uncomfortable.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Periprocedural anticoagulation management is critical. Uninterrupted DOAC strategy during catheter ablation is increasingly adopted. For patients on warfarin undergoing PCI, target INR 2-2.5 and minimize triple therapy duration. AF ablation services are expanding in India but remain concentrated in major electrophysiology centers.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Anticoagulation compliance is a major issue. Many patients discontinue DOACs due to cost, fear of bleeding, or lack of follow-up. The CHA2DS2-VA simplification may help communication. Structured AF clinics with nurse-led anticoagulation management are rare but effective where established. Rheumatic AF remains prevalent in India and mandates warfarin use; DOACs are contraindicated with mechanical valves and significant mitral stenosis.
