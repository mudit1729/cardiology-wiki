# 2025 ACC/AHA Acute Coronary Syndromes Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Unified STEMI and NSTEMI into a single ACS management pathway, replacing the prior separate documents from 2013 (STEMI) and 2014 (NSTE-ACS).
2. De-escalation of P2Y12 inhibitor therapy from prasugrel/ticagrelor to clopidogrel after 1-3 months is now formally endorsed for selected patients (high bleeding risk, stable post-PCI).
3. Routine pre-treatment with P2Y12 inhibitors in NSTEMI is no longer recommended; administer only after coronary anatomy is defined.
4. Intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT) during PCI receives a stronger recommendation (Class IIa upgraded) for complex lesions and left main interventions.
5. Early invasive strategy timing refined: very early (<2 hours) catheterization recommended only for hemodynamic instability or refractory symptoms; otherwise within 24 hours for high-risk NSTEMI.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| STEMI reperfusion | Primary PCI within 90 min of first medical contact when available | I | A | Door-to-balloon remains the gold standard metric |
| Antiplatelet loading | Aspirin 325 mg chewed at first medical contact for all ACS | I | A | Non-enteric coated preferred for faster absorption |
| P2Y12 in STEMI PCI | Prasugrel or ticagrelor preferred over clopidogrel after PCI | I | B | Prasugrel contraindicated with prior stroke/TIA |
| Anticoagulation | UFH or bivalirudin during primary PCI | I | A | Bivalirudin may reduce bleeding in high-risk patients |
| High-intensity statin | Initiate high-intensity statin before discharge | I | A | Atorvastatin 80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg |
| Beta-blocker | Oral beta-blocker within 24 hours in hemodynamically stable patients | I | B | Avoid in cardiogenic shock or severe bradycardia |
| ACE inhibitor/ARB | Start within 24 hours for anterior STEMI, HF, or LVEF <=40% | I | A | Titrate to target dose before discharge |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Complete revascularization | PCI of non-culprit lesions during index hospitalization or staged | Data from COMPLETE trial supportive, but timing and lesion selection remain debated |
| Colchicine post-MI | May be considered for residual inflammatory risk reduction | COLCOT showed modest benefit; GI side effects limit adherence |
| Routine thrombectomy in STEMI | Not recommended routinely | TOTAL trial showed no benefit and increased stroke risk |
| Ticagrelor monotherapy | After 1-3 months DAPT, drop aspirin and continue ticagrelor alone | TWILIGHT data strong, but real-world compliance data mixed |
| Fibrinolysis in late presenters | Consider in 12-24 hour window if ongoing ischemia | Evidence limited beyond 12 hours; must weigh bleeding risk |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Aspirin**: 81 mg daily indefinitely after loading dose; 325 mg if intolerant to lower dose
- **Prasugrel**: 60 mg load, 10 mg daily; reduce to 5 mg if age >75 or weight <60 kg; avoid with prior stroke
- **Ticagrelor**: 180 mg load, 90 mg BID; dyspnea is a class effect, usually transient
- **Clopidogrel**: 600 mg load, 75 mg daily; acceptable when prasugrel/ticagrelor contraindicated or bleeding risk is high
- **DAPT duration**: Minimum 6 months post-DES, may shorten to 1-3 months (high bleeding risk) or extend to >12 months (high ischemic risk)
- **Heparin**: UFH 60 U/kg bolus (max 4000 U) for PCI; enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC BID for conservative strategy
- **Statin**: High-intensity regimen; add ezetimibe if LDL remains >55 mg/dL; consider PCSK9 inhibitor if still above target
- **Beta-blocker**: Metoprolol, bisoprolol, or carvedilol; titrate based on heart rate and blood pressure
- **ACEi/ARB**: Ramipril, perindopril, or valsartan; start low and titrate

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Prasugrel and ticagrelor are widely available as affordable generics (INR 5-15/tablet). Clopidogrel remains the most commonly used P2Y12 inhibitor due to familiarity and cost. PCSK9 inhibitors are available but remain expensive (INR 10,000-40,000/month) and are rarely used outside metro centers.
- **Cost considerations**: Generic atorvastatin (INR 3-8/tablet) makes high-intensity statin therapy feasible. Bivalirudin is significantly more expensive than UFH and used selectively. Drug-eluting stent costs have dropped substantially, making primary PCI more accessible.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Primary PCI is increasingly available in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities but remains inconsistent in rural India. Hub-and-spoke models with pharmaco-invasive strategy (fibrinolysis at spoke, transfer for angiography within 3-24 hours) remain essential for STEMI networks. Radial access is preferred and is standard practice for most Indian interventionalists.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Medication discontinuation is a major challenge. Cost, lack of follow-up infrastructure, and patient education gaps drive high rates of DAPT and statin discontinuation at 6-12 months. Fixed-dose combinations (aspirin + ticagrelor, statin + ezetimibe) may improve compliance. Structured cardiac rehabilitation programs are limited outside major centers.
