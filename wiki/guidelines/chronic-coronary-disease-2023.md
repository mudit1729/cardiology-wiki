# 2023 AHA/ACC Chronic Coronary Disease Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Replaced the term "stable ischemic heart disease" with "chronic coronary disease" (CCD) to encompass the full post-ACS continuum and emphasize lifelong management.
2. SGLT2 inhibitors receive a Class IIa recommendation for patients with CCD and type 2 diabetes or heart failure, reflecting cardiorenal benefits beyond glucose control.
3. Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) elevated as a first-line diagnostic test for intermediate-risk symptomatic patients, on par with stress testing.
4. Shared decision-making for revascularization is now a Class I requirement, with emphasis on documenting the conversation.
5. Colchicine receives a IIb recommendation for residual inflammatory risk in CCD patients, based on LoDoCo2 and COLCOT data.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Antiplatelet therapy | Aspirin 75-100 mg daily for all CCD patients | I | A | Lifelong unless bleeding risk prohibits |
| High-intensity statin | Target LDL <70 mg/dL (ideally <55 mg/dL for very high risk) | I | A | Add ezetimibe or PCSK9i if not at target |
| ACEi/ARB | Recommended for CCD with hypertension, diabetes, LVEF <=40%, or CKD | I | A | Ramipril or perindopril have strongest outcome data |
| Beta-blocker | For patients with prior MI or LVEF <=50% | I | B | No longer mandated for all CCD patients without MI/LV dysfunction |
| Exercise and cardiac rehab | Structured exercise-based cardiac rehab for all CCD | I | A | Improves functional capacity and quality of life |
| Annual influenza vaccination | Recommended for all CCD patients | I | B | Reduces cardiovascular events during flu season |
| Blood pressure target | <130/80 mmHg for CCD patients | I | B | Avoid excessive diastolic lowering below 60 mmHg |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Colchicine 0.5 mg daily | May be considered for anti-inflammatory risk reduction | Benefit modest; GI intolerance and drug interactions are concerns |
| Ranolazine for refractory angina | Add-on to max tolerated anti-anginal therapy | Limited large outcome data; useful in patients intolerant to other agents |
| Revascularization for survival benefit | PCI may be considered for significant ischemia | ISCHEMIA trial showed no mortality benefit for routine revascularization; symptom relief is the primary indication |
| Long-term DAPT beyond 1 year | Extended DAPT for high ischemic, low bleeding risk | Incremental ischemic benefit vs. increased bleeding; patient selection is key |
| Coronary calcium scoring | Useful for risk reclassification in borderline-risk patients | Not useful if CCD already established; best for primary prevention |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Aspirin**: 75-100 mg daily, lifelong; consider clopidogrel if aspirin intolerant
- **Statin**: High-intensity (atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg); ezetimibe 10 mg as add-on; PCSK9i for persistent elevation
- **Beta-blocker**: Metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol, or carvedilol for post-MI or reduced LVEF; not mandatory for all CCD
- **Nitrates**: Sublingual nitroglycerin for acute angina; isosorbide mononitrate for chronic symptom control
- **Calcium channel blocker**: Amlodipine or diltiazem for angina when beta-blocker is contraindicated or insufficient
- **Ranolazine**: 500-1000 mg BID as add-on for refractory angina; watch for QTc prolongation
- **ACEi/ARB**: Ramipril 10 mg or equivalent for vascular protection
- **SGLT2 inhibitor**: Empagliflozin or dapagliflozin if concomitant T2DM or HF
- **Colchicine**: 0.5 mg daily if elevated inflammatory risk and tolerable

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: All first-line agents (aspirin, generic statins, beta-blockers, ACEi) are widely available and affordable. Ranolazine is available but underutilized. SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) are available as generics at INR 10-25/tablet, making them increasingly feasible.
- **Cost considerations**: Generic atorvastatin 40 mg costs INR 3-6/day, making high-intensity therapy very accessible. PCSK9 inhibitors remain prohibitively expensive for most patients. Fixed-dose combinations (statin + aspirin, statin + ezetimibe) improve adherence and reduce pill burden.
- **Cath lab practicality**: The ISCHEMIA trial results have tempered enthusiasm for revascularization in stable CCD. Medical therapy optimization should precede any invasive strategy. CCTA is increasingly available in urban India but remains less accessible in Tier 2/3 cities. Stress echocardiography and treadmill testing remain the dominant non-invasive modalities.
- **Follow-up adherence**: CCD requires lifelong medication and risk factor management. Polypill strategies (aspirin + statin + ACEi in a single pill) are being explored in Indian population studies and show improved adherence. Annual follow-up visits with medication reconciliation are recommended but challenging in overburdened public health systems. Yoga-based cardiac rehabilitation programs have shown promise as adjuncts in the Indian context.
