# 2020 ESC Antithrombotic Therapy Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. DAPT duration is now individualized using ischemic and bleeding risk scores (DAPT score, PRECISE-DAPT, ARC-HBR criteria) rather than applying blanket durations for all patients.
2. Short DAPT (1-3 months) followed by P2Y12 monotherapy (dropping aspirin) is a validated strategy for high bleeding risk patients after PCI, based on TWILIGHT, TICO, and STOPDAPT-2 trials.
3. Triple therapy (OAC + DAPT) duration minimized in AF patients requiring PCI: default to 1 week of triple therapy, then OAC + single antiplatelet (preferably clopidogrel) for up to 12 months.
4. De-escalation from potent P2Y12 (prasugrel/ticagrelor) to clopidogrel at 1-3 months post-ACS is endorsed (Class IIb), with or without platelet function testing, based on TOPIC and TROPICAL-ACS data.
5. Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID added to aspirin (COMPASS regimen) endorsed for stable ASCVD patients at high ischemic risk without high bleeding risk, providing a dual-pathway inhibition strategy.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| DAPT post-ACS PCI | Aspirin + potent P2Y12 inhibitor for 12 months as default | I | A | Prasugrel or ticagrelor preferred; clopidogrel if high bleeding risk |
| DAPT post-elective PCI | Aspirin + clopidogrel for 6 months as default | I | A | Shorten to 3 months if HBR; extend to 12 months if high ischemic risk |
| OAC in AF + PCI | DOAC preferred over warfarin as the OAC component | I | A | Reduce DOAC to tested lower dose (rivaroxaban 15 mg, apixaban 5 mg BID) |
| Triple therapy minimization | Limit triple therapy to 1 week (max 1 month) in AF + PCI | I | A | Then OAC + clopidogrel; drop aspirin early |
| Aspirin in stable ASCVD | Aspirin 75-100 mg daily for lifelong secondary prevention | I | A | Enteric-coated for GI protection |
| PPI co-prescription | PPI recommended with any DAPT regimen | I | A | Reduces GI bleeding by 60-70%; omeprazole interaction with clopidogrel is clinically insignificant at standard doses |
| Assess bleeding risk | Use ARC-HBR criteria or PRECISE-DAPT score at PCI | I | B | Guides DAPT duration decision |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| P2Y12 monotherapy (aspirin-free) | Drop aspirin after 1-3 months and continue ticagrelor alone | TWILIGHT supports this; but unclear if applies to all subsets (diabetes, CTO, bifurcation) |
| De-escalation to clopidogrel | Switch from ticagrelor/prasugrel to clopidogrel at 1-3 months | TOPIC trial supports in ACS; platelet function testing optional but adds cost |
| Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg + aspirin (COMPASS) | For high-risk stable ASCVD without high bleeding risk | COMPASS showed MACE reduction but increased bleeding; net clinical benefit positive |
| Extended DAPT beyond 12 months | May be considered in high ischemic, low bleeding risk patients | Incremental benefit diminishes over time; shared decision-making essential |
| Cangrelor bridging | IV cangrelor for patients requiring urgent surgery on DAPT | Limited availability; cost is prohibitive in many settings |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Aspirin**: 75-100 mg daily lifelong; 325 mg loading in ACS
- **Clopidogrel**: 600 mg load, 75 mg daily; standard for elective PCI; de-escalation target in ACS
- **Prasugrel**: 60 mg load, 10 mg daily (5 mg if >75 years or <60 kg); more consistent platelet inhibition than clopidogrel
- **Ticagrelor**: 180 mg load, 90 mg BID (60 mg BID for extended therapy beyond 1 year); dyspnea, bradycardia
- **Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID**: Dual-pathway inhibition with aspirin for stable ASCVD (COMPASS regimen)
- **Rivaroxaban 15 mg daily**: With clopidogrel for AF + PCI (PIONEER AF-PCI)
- **Apixaban 5 mg BID**: With clopidogrel for AF + PCI (AUGUSTUS); best bleeding profile in triple therapy studies
- **PPI**: Pantoprazole 40 mg or esomeprazole 20 mg daily with DAPT
- **Cangrelor**: 30 mcg/kg bolus then 4 mcg/kg/min IV; bridges P2Y12 inhibition periprocedurally

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Clopidogrel dominates Indian practice due to cost (INR 2-5/day) and familiarity. Prasugrel generics cost INR 5-10/day and ticagrelor INR 10-20/day. Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg for COMPASS regimen is available but underutilized. All DOACs are available as generics. PPIs are universally available and affordable (INR 2-5/day).
- **Cost considerations**: Platelet function testing (VerifyNow, Multiplate) is available at major centers but costs INR 2000-5000, limiting guided de-escalation. Empiric de-escalation based on clinical judgment is the pragmatic approach. The cost difference between 12-month ticagrelor and de-escalation to clopidogrel at 1 month is approximately INR 3000-5000, which is meaningful for many patients.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Loading dose timing and drug choice should be documented in cath lab protocols. Pre-treatment with P2Y12 inhibitors before diagnostic angiography in NSTEMI should be avoided to preserve surgical options. Radial access reduces bleeding and allows more liberal antithrombotic use. Bivalirudin is expensive in India and reserved for selected cases.
- **Follow-up adherence**: DAPT discontinuation is a leading cause of stent thrombosis in India. Patients must understand that stopping medication without medical advice is dangerous. Prescription of individual drugs rather than fixed-dose combinations increases pill burden and reduces compliance. SMS/WhatsApp-based medication reminders have shown promise in Indian studies. Economic hardship is the most common reason for medication discontinuation; identifying at-risk patients and prescribing affordable generics from the start is essential.
