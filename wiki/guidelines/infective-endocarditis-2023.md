# 2023 ESC Infective Endocarditis Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. PET/CT imaging with 18F-FDG integrated into modified Duke criteria as a major criterion for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) and cardiac device infection, improving diagnostic sensitivity from 70% to 90% for PVE.
2. Earlier surgical referral: surgery recommended within days (not weeks) for patients with large vegetations (>10 mm), severe valvular regurgitation with heart failure, or uncontrolled infection despite appropriate antibiotics.
3. Oral step-down antibiotic therapy validated: partial oral treatment (POET trial approach) endorsed after initial IV phase (>=10 days) for selected left-sided endocarditis with clinical stability and no complicated course.
4. Endocarditis Team concept formalized: multidisciplinary team (cardiologist, cardiac surgeon, infectious disease, microbiologist, neurologist) mandated for all IE management decisions.
5. Device-related IE management updated: complete device extraction recommended for all confirmed CIED infections, including lead-only infections without valve involvement.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Blood cultures before antibiotics | At least 3 sets of blood cultures from separate venipuncture sites | I | B | Do not delay if septic; but obtain cultures before antibiotics whenever possible |
| Echocardiography at diagnosis | TTE as first-line; TEE if TTE inadequate, prosthetic valve, or high clinical suspicion | I | B | TEE sensitivity ~95% vs TTE ~65% for vegetations |
| Empiric antibiotic therapy | Start after blood cultures; cover Staphylococci, Streptococci, Enterococci | I | B | Native valve: amoxicillin + cloxacillin + gentamicin; prosthetic: vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampicin |
| Surgery for heart failure | Urgent surgery for severe acute regurgitation causing refractory pulmonary edema or shock | I | B | Do not delay for prolonged antibiotic courses |
| Surgery for uncontrolled infection | Operate if persistent bacteremia >7 days, abscess, or enlarging vegetation on therapy | I | B | Ring abscess and fistula formation are surgical emergencies |
| Complete CIED extraction | Extract all leads and generator in confirmed device infection | I | B | Reimplantation after adequate antibiotic course and negative cultures |
| IE prophylaxis | Antibiotic prophylaxis for dental procedures in high-risk patients | I | B | Prosthetic valves, prior IE, certain congenital heart disease |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Oral step-down therapy | Switch to oral antibiotics after >=10 days IV in stable patients | POET trial was practice-changing but excluded complex cases; real-world application requires careful selection |
| Surgery for stroke | Consider early surgery despite recent stroke if no hemorrhagic transformation | Optimal timing after CNS embolism remains debated; 2-4 week delay may be warranted for large infarcts |
| Anticoagulation in PVE | Continue anticoagulation in PVE unless CNS event occurs | Risk of hemorrhagic transformation vs. mechanical valve thrombosis; switch to UFH for close monitoring |
| Prophylaxis scope expansion | Extend prophylaxis beyond dental to GI/GU procedures in high-risk | Evidence for non-dental prophylaxis is weak; practice varies widely |
| Empiric therapy for culture-negative IE | Doxycycline-based regimen for suspected Bartonella or Coxiella | Culture-negative IE represents 10-30% of cases; requires systematic serological evaluation |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Native valve Streptococcal IE**: Penicillin G 12-18 MU/day IV or ceftriaxone 2 g daily x 4 weeks; add gentamicin for 2 weeks in complicated cases
- **Staphylococcal IE (MSSA)**: Cloxacillin/nafcillin 12 g/day IV divided q4h x 4-6 weeks; add rifampicin for prosthetic valves
- **Staphylococcal IE (MRSA)**: Vancomycin 30-60 mg/kg/day IV or daptomycin 10-12 mg/kg/day
- **Enterococcal IE**: Ampicillin 12 g/day IV + gentamicin (or ceftriaxone if gentamicin-resistant) x 4-6 weeks
- **Prosthetic valve empiric**: Vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampicin pending cultures
- **Oral step-down options**: Amoxicillin 1 g q6h, linezolid 600 mg BID, or fluoroquinolone + rifampicin based on susceptibility
- **Fungal IE**: Amphotericin B + flucytosine; surgical intervention almost always required

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Penicillin G, ceftriaxone, vancomycin, and gentamicin are all widely available and affordable. Daptomycin is available but expensive (INR 3000-5000/day). Rifampicin is readily available given its use in TB treatment. Blood culture facilities are available in most urban hospitals but quality varies significantly.
- **Cost considerations**: Prolonged IV antibiotic therapy (4-6 weeks) represents a major financial burden. Oral step-down strategies could dramatically reduce costs and length of hospital stay. TEE availability has improved in major centers but is not universally accessible. PET/CT is available at tertiary centers in major cities but cost (INR 15,000-25,000) limits routine use.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Many IE patients require coronary angiography pre-operatively if surgical intervention is planned. Vegetation size and mobility assessment by echo guides surgical timing. Rheumatic valves with superimposed IE are a common scenario in India, often requiring complex surgical decisions.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Completing the full antibiotic course is challenging due to cost, IV access complications, and patient fatigue. OPAT (outpatient parenteral antibiotic therapy) services are emerging in India but remain limited. Culture-negative endocarditis rates are high in India (up to 40-50%) due to prior antibiotic use before presentation. Dental hygiene education and access to dental care are poor, contributing to ongoing IE risk. IV drug use-related IE is increasing in parts of India, particularly in the northeast, and presents unique management challenges.
