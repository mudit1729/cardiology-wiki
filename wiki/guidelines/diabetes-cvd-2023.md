# 2023 ESC Diabetes and Cardiovascular Disease Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. CV risk-based drug selection replaces glucose-centric approach: SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RAs recommended as first-line for T2DM patients with established ASCVD, HF, or CKD, independent of HbA1c or metformin use.
2. GLP-1 receptor agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide) receive Class I recommendation for reducing CV events in T2DM patients with established atherosclerotic disease, no longer requiring metformin failure first.
3. SGLT2 inhibitors receive Class I recommendation for T2DM patients with HF or CKD, with dual cardiorenal benefit recognized regardless of baseline LVEF or diabetes status.
4. Comprehensive cardiovascular risk factor management emphasized equally with glucose control: target LDL <55 mg/dL, BP <130/80, smoking cessation, and antiplatelet therapy for secondary prevention.
5. Risk stratification simplified: three categories (very high risk with organ damage/established CVD, high risk with duration >10 years, moderate risk for young patients without complications) replace prior complex algorithms.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| SGLT2i for HF in T2DM | Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin for T2DM with HF | I | A | Benefit independent of LVEF; initiate regardless of HbA1c |
| GLP-1 RA for ASCVD in T2DM | Liraglutide, semaglutide, or dulaglutide for T2DM with established ASCVD | I | A | CV benefit proven in LEADER, SUSTAIN-6, REWIND |
| SGLT2i for CKD in T2DM | SGLT2i for T2DM with CKD (eGFR >=20) | I | A | Continue even when eGFR drops below 45; renal benefit persists |
| High-intensity statin | LDL-C <55 mg/dL for very high-risk diabetic patients | I | A | Add ezetimibe or PCSK9i if needed |
| ACEi/ARB | For diabetic patients with hypertension, albuminuria, or CKD | I | A | Renoprotective and cardioprotective |
| Aspirin for secondary prevention | Aspirin 75-100 mg for diabetic patients with established ASCVD | I | A | Not recommended for primary prevention in most diabetic patients |
| Comprehensive lifestyle intervention | Diet, exercise, weight management, smoking cessation | I | A | >=150 min/week moderate exercise; Mediterranean or DASH diet |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Metformin as first-line | Metformin no longer mandated as the universal first drug | Paradigm shift: CV-protective drugs (SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA) should be prioritized over metformin in high-risk patients |
| Aspirin for primary prevention in T2DM | May consider in high-risk patients without elevated bleeding risk | ASCEND trial: modest CV benefit vs. increased bleeding; net benefit marginal |
| Finerenone for diabetic kidney disease | Consider for T2DM with CKD and albuminuria on max RAS blockade | FIDELIO/FIGARO positive, but role alongside SGLT2i still being defined |
| Tirzepatide for CV risk reduction | Not yet recommended for CV risk reduction per se | SURPASS data impressive for glucose/weight; SURPASS-CVOT pending for CV outcomes |
| Intensive glucose control (HbA1c <6.5%) | Not recommended for most patients | ACCORD, ADVANCE, VADT suggest harm with very intensive glucose lowering; target 6.5-7.5% for most |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **SGLT2 inhibitors**: Empagliflozin 10-25 mg, dapagliflozin 10 mg, canagliflozin 100-300 mg; monitor for genital infections, euglycemic ketoacidosis
- **GLP-1 RAs**: Semaglutide 0.5-1.0 mg SC weekly (or 14 mg oral), liraglutide 1.2-1.8 mg SC daily, dulaglutide 0.75-1.5 mg SC weekly; GI side effects are dose-limiting
- **Metformin**: 500-2000 mg daily; still useful for glucose control but no longer the mandatory starting point
- **Insulin**: Basal-bolus or premixed regimens when needed for glucose control; does not confer CV protection
- **Pioglitazone**: May reduce CV events in T2DM (PROactive); limited by weight gain, edema, fracture risk
- **Finerenone**: 10-20 mg daily; non-steroidal MRA for CKD progression in T2DM; monitor K+
- **Statin + ezetimibe**: Foundation for lipid management; target LDL <55 mg/dL
- **ACEi/ARB**: First-line antihypertensive for diabetic patients; maximize dose before adding other agents

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Generic SGLT2 inhibitors are widely available in India at INR 10-25/day, making them accessible for a large population. GLP-1 RAs are available but more expensive: liraglutide INR 200-300/day, semaglutide SC INR 3000-4000/month. Oral semaglutide is available at INR 100-150/day. Metformin remains the cheapest option at INR 1-3/day.
- **Cost considerations**: The shift from glucose-centric to CV risk-centric management is particularly relevant in India, where T2DM prevalence is epidemic (estimated 100+ million). SGLT2 inhibitors offer the best cost-benefit ratio among newer agents. GLP-1 RAs remain aspirational for many patients. Fixed-dose combinations (metformin + SGLT2i) are available and improve adherence.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Diabetic patients constitute 30-40% of patients undergoing PCI in India. Multivessel disease is common. CABG with bilateral IMA grafts may offer advantage in diabetics (FREEDOM trial), though surgeons vary in bilateral IMA adoption. Drug-coated balloons for small vessel disease in diabetics are gaining traction.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Integrated cardio-metabolic clinics are rare in India; patients often see cardiologist and diabetologist separately with poor coordination. HbA1c monitoring every 3 months is recommended but often performed less frequently. Medication costs, complex regimens, and cultural dietary preferences (high carbohydrate Indian diet) challenge adherence. AYUSH (traditional medicine) use alongside modern medications is common and should be discussed openly. Diabetic foot care and retinopathy screening are often neglected in the focus on cardiac management.
