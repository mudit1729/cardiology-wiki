# 2024 AHA Obesity and Cardiovascular Disease Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Obesity formally classified as a cardiovascular disease risk equivalent, not merely a risk factor. BMI >=30 (or >=25 for South Asians) with cardiometabolic complications warrants aggressive treatment.
2. GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly) and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists (tirzepatide) endorsed as first-line pharmacotherapy for obesity with CV risk factors, based on SELECT and SURMOUNT trial data.
3. Bariatric/metabolic surgery receives expanded indications: recommended for BMI >=35 with comorbidities or BMI >=40 (lower thresholds for South Asians: >=32.5 and >=37.5 respectively), with demonstrated CV event reduction.
4. Adiposity-Based Chronic Disease (ABCD) staging system introduced to classify obesity by degree of cardiometabolic impairment rather than BMI alone, incorporating waist circumference, visceral adiposity, and metabolic parameters.
5. Semaglutide 2.4 mg demonstrated 20% reduction in MACE in SELECT trial in overweight/obese adults with established CVD but without diabetes, establishing anti-obesity medications as cardiovascular therapeutics.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Comprehensive lifestyle intervention | Diet, physical activity (>=150 min/week), behavioral modification | I | A | 500-750 kcal/day deficit; 5-10% weight loss produces CV benefit |

| Multidisciplinary approach | Comprehensive team (dietitian, exercise physiologist, psychologist, physician) | I | B | Isolated dietary advice is insufficient for sustained weight loss |
| CV risk factor management | Aggressively treat hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes in obese patients | I | A | Weight loss augments efficacy of all CV medications |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Tirzepatide for CV risk reduction | Likely beneficial but formal CV outcome data pending | SURMOUNT-1 showed 20-26% weight loss; SURPASS-CVOT will define CV endpoint benefit |
| Long-term GLP-1 RA use | Likely requires indefinite treatment | Weight regain of 65-70% after discontinuation (STEP-1 extension); sustainability and cost concerns |
| Metabolic surgery for BMI 30-35 | May be considered with poorly controlled metabolic disease | Data from STAMPEDE trial supportive for diabetes remission; broader CV data in this BMI range limited |
| Orlistat, phentermine, naltrexone/bupropion | Older agents may be considered if GLP-1 RAs unavailable or unaffordable | Much less effective for weight loss (3-7% vs. 15-20%); limited CV outcome data |
| Obesity paradox in HF | Lower BMI targets may not apply to established HFrEF | Observational data suggest overweight/obese HF patients have better short-term outcomes; may reflect lead-time bias |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Semaglutide 2.4 mg**: SC weekly; dose escalation over 16 weeks; GI side effects (nausea, vomiting) common early; proven MACE reduction in SELECT
- **Tirzepatide**: 5-15 mg SC weekly; dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist; greatest weight loss (up to 26%); CV outcome data pending
- **Liraglutide 3.0 mg**: SC daily; FDA-approved for obesity; lesser weight loss (~8%) than semaglutide; CV data from LEADER (diabetes dose)
- **Oral semaglutide 50 mg**: Under investigation for obesity; may improve access if approved
- **Naltrexone/bupropion**: 8/90 mg tablets, max 32/360 mg daily; 5-7% weight loss; avoid with seizure disorder or opioid use
- **Orlistat**: 120 mg TID with meals; blocks fat absorption; 3-4% weight loss; GI side effects limit tolerance
- **Phentermine**: Short-term use only (<12 weeks); sympathomimetic; avoid in CVD
- **Metformin**: Modest weight loss (2-3%); not approved for obesity but commonly used off-label

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy equivalent) is available in India at INR 3000-5000/month for diabetes dosing. Obesity-specific dosing (2.4 mg) is becoming available through branded generics. Tirzepatide is available at higher cost. Liraglutide 3.0 mg is available but expensive. Orlistat, naltrexone/bupropion, and metformin are available as affordable generics.
- **Cost considerations**: GLP-1 RA therapy for obesity is a paradigm shift but currently accessible mainly to upper-middle-class and affluent patients. Bariatric surgery costs INR 2-5 lakh in India (compared to USD 20,000-30,000 in the US), making it relatively more accessible. Insurance coverage for bariatric surgery is improving. Weight management is rarely reimbursed by Indian insurance as a standalone indication.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Obese patients present technical challenges in the cath lab: difficult vascular access, higher contrast requirements, radiation dosing concerns, and increased procedural complications. Radial access may be more challenging in very obese patients. Weight-based dosing of anticoagulants and contrast volume must be carefully calculated.
- **Follow-up adherence**: The Indian obesity epidemic is driven by urbanization, sedentary lifestyles, and dietary transition. South Asian-specific BMI thresholds (overweight >=23, obese >=25) are critical to apply, as cardiometabolic risk manifests at lower BMI in this population. Cultural factors (vegetarian diets high in refined carbs, limited exercise infrastructure, social eating patterns) must inform dietary counseling. Yoga and traditional exercise forms can be integrated into activity prescriptions. Sustained weight management requires long-term follow-up that is challenging in the Indian healthcare system.
