# 2023 ESH Arterial Hypertension Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Office blood pressure threshold for treatment initiation lowered to >=140/90 mmHg for all adults, with a treatment target of 120-129/70-79 mmHg for most patients (if tolerated), reflecting SPRINT trial influence.
2. Out-of-office BP measurement (ABPM or HBPM) strongly recommended to confirm diagnosis and guide treatment, reducing white-coat hypertension misdiagnosis.
3. Initial combination therapy is now standard: single-pill combination (SPC) of two agents at low dose preferred over monotherapy for most patients with grade 1 or higher hypertension.
4. SGLT2 inhibitors recognized as BP-lowering agents with cardiorenal benefits, recommended in hypertensive patients with T2DM, HF, or CKD.
5. Renal denervation receives cautious recognition (Class IIb) as an adjunctive option for resistant hypertension, based on SPYRAL HTN and RADIANCE trials.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Diagnostic confirmation | ABPM or HBPM to confirm office hypertension | I | A | Avoid treating white-coat hypertension unnecessarily |
| Initial combination therapy | Start with dual combination (ACEi/ARB + CCB or thiazide-like diuretic) | I | A | Single-pill combinations improve adherence by 25-30% |
| BP target <130/80 | Target 120-129 systolic in most adults if tolerated | I | A | Exception: frail elderly, target <140 systolic |
| Lifestyle intervention | Sodium restriction (<5 g NaCl/day), weight loss, exercise, DASH diet | I | A | 5-10 mmHg systolic reduction achievable with lifestyle alone |
| Screening for secondary causes | Evaluate if age <40, resistant HTN, or sudden-onset HTN | I | B | Renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, pheochromocytoma |
| Statin co-prescription | Consider statin in hypertensive patients with moderate-high CV risk | I | A | Synergistic CV risk reduction beyond BP lowering |
| HMOD assessment | Evaluate hypertension-mediated organ damage at diagnosis | I | B | ECG, urine albumin, eGFR, fundoscopy, echocardiography |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Renal denervation | May be considered for resistant hypertension after excluding pseudo-resistance | Long-term durability data lacking; limited to specialized centers; patient selection critical |
| BP target in elderly >80 years | Target <150 systolic may be acceptable if frail | Overly aggressive targets may cause falls, syncope, and AKI in frail elderly |
| Aspirin for primary prevention in HTN | Not routinely recommended | ARRIVE trial showed no net benefit; bleeding risk offsets CV benefit in primary prevention |
| Beta-blockers as first-line | Not preferred unless compelling indication (HF, post-MI, rate control) | Inferior to ACEi/ARB, CCB, and thiazide-like diuretics for primary outcomes |
| Device-based therapies | Baroreceptor activation, central arteriovenous anastomosis | Experimental; insufficient outcome data for routine recommendation |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **ACE inhibitors**: Ramipril 5-10 mg, perindopril 5-10 mg, enalapril 10-20 mg; first-line in diabetics, CKD, post-MI
- **ARBs**: Telmisartan 40-80 mg, olmesartan 20-40 mg, valsartan 160-320 mg; use when ACEi not tolerated (cough)
- **CCBs**: Amlodipine 5-10 mg; effective across age groups; ankle edema is dose-limiting
- **Thiazide-like diuretics**: Chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg or indapamide 1.5 mg; preferred over hydrochlorothiazide for outcome data
- **MRA**: Spironolactone 25-50 mg as fourth-line for resistant hypertension (PATHWAY-2 trial)
- **Beta-blockers**: Bisoprolol, nebivolol; reserved for rate control, post-MI, HFrEF
- **Alpha-blockers**: Doxazosin 4-8 mg; useful as add-on, especially with concomitant BPH
- **Single-pill combinations**: ACEi/ARB + CCB or ACEi/ARB + diuretic; triple SPC (ARB + CCB + diuretic) for step-up

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: All major antihypertensives are available as affordable generics. Amlodipine 5 mg costs INR 1-3/tablet. Telmisartan is the most commonly used ARB in India due to low cost and once-daily dosing. Single-pill combinations are widely available from Indian manufacturers (Cipla, Sun Pharma, Lupin) at INR 5-15/day.
- **Cost considerations**: Chlorthalidone is paradoxically less available than hydrochlorothiazide in India despite guideline preference. ABPM devices are available in urban centers but rarely used in primary care; HBPM with validated devices should be encouraged. Validated BP monitors cost INR 1500-3000.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Hypertension is the most common comorbidity in patients undergoing coronary intervention. Periprocedural BP management is critical. Holding ACEi/ARB on the morning of catheterization is debated; most Indian operators continue them unless concern for contrast nephropathy.
- **Follow-up adherence**: India has an enormous burden of undiagnosed and uncontrolled hypertension. The India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI) has demonstrated that simplified treatment protocols with fixed-dose combinations improve control rates. ASHA workers and community health programs are essential for rural hypertension management. Salt intake is high in Indian diets (average 10-11 g/day); culturally appropriate dietary counseling is needed. Seasonal and festive dietary excesses are important to address.
