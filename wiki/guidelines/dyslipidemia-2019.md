# 2019 ESC/EAS Dyslipidemia Management Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. LDL-C targets lowered aggressively: <55 mg/dL for very high-risk patients and <40 mg/dL for recurrent events within 2 years, reflecting the "lower is better" paradigm.
2. Combination therapy endorsed early: if high-intensity statin alone cannot reach targets, add ezetimibe first, then PCSK9 inhibitor, rather than waiting and retesting.
3. Lipoprotein(a) recognized as an independent cardiovascular risk factor; measurement recommended at least once in every adult to identify very high levels (>50 mg/dL).
4. Bempedoic acid introduced as an option for statin-intolerant patients or those not at target, acting upstream of HMG-CoA reductase.
5. Triglyceride management updated: icosapent ethyl (high-dose EPA) receives recommendation for elevated triglycerides (135-499 mg/dL) in statin-treated patients at high risk, based on REDUCE-IT.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| LDL-C target very high risk | LDL-C <55 mg/dL AND >=50% reduction from baseline | I | A | Applies to established ASCVD, DM with organ damage, severe CKD, FH with ASCVD |
| High-intensity statin first-line | Maximum tolerated statin as foundation of therapy | I | A | Atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg |
| Ezetimibe add-on | Add ezetimibe if target not met on max statin | I | B | LDL reduction of additional 15-20% expected |
| PCSK9 inhibitor | Add if target not met on statin + ezetimibe | I | A | Evolocumab or alirocumab; reduces LDL by 50-60% |
| Statin for primary prevention | High-risk patients (SCORE >=5%) with LDL >=70 mg/dL | I | A | Consider risk enhancers: CAC, family history, South Asian ethnicity |
| Lifestyle modification | Diet, exercise, weight management as foundation | I | A | Mediterranean diet has strongest evidence |
| Statin in ACS | High-intensity statin initiated in-hospital | I | A | Do not delay initiation; in-hospital start improves adherence |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| PCSK9 inhibitors in primary prevention | Not yet recommended for primary prevention alone | Cost-effectiveness data insufficient for widespread primary prevention use |
| Omega-3 fatty acids (mixed EPA/DHA) | Not recommended for CV risk reduction | STRENGTH trial negative; benefit specific to icosapent ethyl (EPA-only) |
| Bempedoic acid | Consider in statin-intolerant patients | CLEAR Outcomes trial shows benefit but magnitude smaller than statins |
| Niacin | Not recommended for LDL lowering | AIM-HIGH and HPS2-THRIVE showed no benefit with increased adverse effects |
| LDL-C <40 mg/dL target | For recurrent events in very high-risk patients | Limited long-term safety data at very low LDL levels; apply with clinical judgment |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Atorvastatin**: 40-80 mg daily; most commonly used high-intensity statin; hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4
- **Rosuvastatin**: 20-40 mg daily; slightly more potent per mg; less drug interaction potential
- **Ezetimibe**: 10 mg daily; blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption; well-tolerated, few side effects
- **Evolocumab**: 140 mg SC every 2 weeks or 420 mg monthly; PCSK9 monoclonal antibody
- **Alirocumab**: 75-150 mg SC every 2 weeks; dose titrated to LDL target
- **Bempedoic acid**: 180 mg daily; ACL inhibitor; does not cause myalgia (not active in skeletal muscle)
- **Icosapent ethyl**: 2 g BID with food; pure EPA for elevated triglycerides on statin background
- **Inclisiran**: 284 mg SC at 0, 3 months, then every 6 months; siRNA targeting PCSK9 synthesis; offers dosing convenience

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Generic atorvastatin and rosuvastatin are extremely affordable (INR 2-8/tablet for highest doses). Ezetimibe generics cost INR 5-10/tablet. PCSK9 inhibitors are available but cost INR 10,000-40,000/month, limiting use to affluent patients or select insurance-covered cases. Inclisiran is becoming available in India. Bempedoic acid has limited availability.
- **Cost considerations**: The statin + ezetimibe combination is highly cost-effective in India. Fixed-dose combinations (atorvastatin + ezetimibe) are available at INR 10-15/day, improving adherence. Icosapent ethyl is available but generic fish oil supplements are often substituted despite lacking equivalent evidence.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Lipid panels should be drawn at admission for all ACS patients. In-hospital statin initiation before discharge is critical and should be part of discharge checklists. Follow-up lipid panels at 4-6 weeks post-initiation to assess target achievement are often missed.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Statin discontinuation is a major problem. Cultural myths about statin harm, fear of lifelong medication, and cost sensitivity all contribute. Patient education in local languages, fixed-dose combinations, and involvement of family members in counseling improve outcomes. South Asian patients have higher baseline cardiovascular risk and may benefit from earlier and more aggressive lipid lowering. Familial hypercholesterolemia is underdiagnosed in India; cascade screening programs are needed.
