# 2019 ESC Chronic Coronary Syndromes Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Renamed "stable coronary artery disease" to "chronic coronary syndromes" (CCS), emphasizing that the disease process is dynamic and patients may transition between stable and unstable phases throughout their lives.
2. Pre-test probability (PTP) of CAD substantially lowered for all age-sex categories based on contemporary registries, reducing the proportion of patients classified as intermediate risk and therefore reducing unnecessary testing.
3. Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) recommended as the first-line diagnostic test for patients with low-intermediate PTP, challenging the traditional exercise ECG-first approach.
4. Non-invasive functional imaging (stress echo, SPECT, stress CMR, PET) recommended for intermediate-high PTP patients, with exercise ECG downgraded to situations where imaging is unavailable.
5. Lifelong secondary prevention and risk factor control emphasized as the cornerstone of CCS management, with revascularization reserved primarily for symptom improvement rather than prognostic benefit in most patients.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Anti-ischemic first-line | Beta-blockers or CCBs as initial anti-anginal therapy | I | A | Choose based on HR, BP, and comorbidities |
| Sublingual nitroglycerin | For immediate symptom relief of anginal episodes | I | B | Educate patients on proper use and when to seek emergency care |
| High-intensity statin | For all CCS patients; target LDL <55 mg/dL if very high risk | I | A | Add ezetimibe or PCSK9i if not at target |
| Aspirin 75-100 mg | Lifelong antiplatelet in all CCS patients | I | A | Clopidogrel if aspirin intolerant |
| ACEi/ARB | For CCS with hypertension, diabetes, LV dysfunction, or CKD | I | A | Ramipril or perindopril based on HOPE/EUROPA data |
| CCTA for low-intermediate PTP | First-line non-invasive diagnostic test | I | B | Excellent negative predictive value; rules out CAD effectively |
| Lifestyle and risk factor control | Smoking cessation, diet, exercise, weight management | I | A | Most impactful long-term intervention |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Revascularization for prognosis | PCI for stable CCS with significant ischemia | ISCHEMIA trial showed no mortality benefit of routine revascularization over optimal medical therapy; symptom relief remains the primary indication |
| Ranolazine as add-on | Consider for refractory angina after beta-blocker and CCB | Limited outcome data; mechanism (late sodium current) distinct; useful niche agent |
| Long-acting nitrates | Isosorbide mononitrate for chronic angina prevention | Tolerance develops; nitrate-free interval required; headache limits use |
| Trimetazidine | Metabolic anti-anginal as second-line add-on | Widely used globally; modest angina relief; limited large outcome trials |
| Exercise ECG as first test | Acceptable where imaging not available | Sensitivity ~50% and specificity ~80% are inferior to imaging modalities; retains role in exercise capacity assessment |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Beta-blockers**: Bisoprolol 2.5-10 mg, metoprolol 50-200 mg, atenolol 25-100 mg; first-line anti-anginal; target HR 55-60 bpm
- **CCBs (dihydropyridine)**: Amlodipine 5-10 mg; use when beta-blocker contraindicated or insufficient; combine with beta-blocker for synergy
- **CCBs (non-dihydropyridine)**: Diltiazem 120-360 mg, verapamil 120-480 mg; avoid with beta-blockers (bradycardia, heart block risk)
- **Nitrates**: Isosorbide mononitrate 30-120 mg daily; asymmetric dosing for tolerance prevention; sublingual GTN for rescue
- **Nicorandil**: 10-30 mg BID; potassium channel opener + nitrate; useful second-line agent
- **Ranolazine**: 375-750 mg BID; inhibits late sodium current; avoid if QTc prolonged
- **Trimetazidine**: 35 mg BID (modified release); metabolic modulator; widely used in India and Europe
- **Ivabradine**: 5-7.5 mg BID; selective If inhibitor; useful if beta-blocker intolerant in sinus rhythm
- **Aspirin**: 75-100 mg daily lifelong
- **Statin**: High-intensity as per risk category

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: All anti-anginal drugs are available as affordable generics in India. Trimetazidine is particularly popular and widely prescribed (INR 5-10/day), more so than in the US (where it is not available). Nicorandil is commonly used as a second-line agent. Ranolazine generics cost INR 10-15/day.
- **Cost considerations**: Exercise ECG (TMT) remains the most commonly used first-line test in India due to availability and cost (INR 500-1000). CCTA is available in urban centers at INR 5000-10,000 but is not yet the default first test. Stress echocardiography offers a cost-effective imaging alternative (INR 2000-3000). Nuclear SPECT imaging costs INR 5000-8000 and is available at tertiary centers.
- **Cath lab practicality**: There is a tendency toward early invasive strategy in stable angina in India, often driven by symptom burden and patient expectation. The ISCHEMIA trial data supporting an initial conservative strategy with optimal medical therapy should inform shared decision-making. FFR/iFR use is growing but still not standard for all intermediate lesions due to cost (INR 25,000-35,000 per wire).
- **Follow-up adherence**: CCS requires lifelong medication and risk factor management. Many patients discontinue therapy when symptoms improve, mistaking stability for cure. Regular follow-up (every 3-6 months initially, then annually) is essential but challenging in the Indian healthcare system. Community pharmacy refill programs and generic fixed-dose combinations (aspirin + statin + beta-blocker) are strategies to improve long-term compliance. Cardiac rehabilitation programs with yoga integration have shown benefits in Indian studies.
