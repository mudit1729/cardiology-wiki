# 2022 AHA/ACC STEMI Systems of Care Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Emphasis on regionalized STEMI care networks with standardized protocols for EMS activation, inter-facility transfer, and bypass to PCI-capable hospitals, reducing total ischemic time.
2. First medical contact-to-device (FMC-to-D) time replaces door-to-balloon as the primary quality metric, acknowledging that delays begin before hospital arrival and transfer times must be counted.
3. Pharmaco-invasive strategy formally endorsed as equivalent to primary PCI for patients presenting to non-PCI hospitals where transfer time exceeds 120 minutes from FMC.
4. Telemedicine-guided STEMI diagnosis validated: trained EMS or remote physicians interpreting prehospital ECGs via digital transmission for cath lab activation before patient arrival.
5. Quality improvement programs mandated: STEMI receiving centers must participate in data registries, conduct regular case reviews, and track performance metrics including FMC-to-D time, total ischemic time, and in-hospital mortality.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Prehospital ECG | 12-lead ECG acquisition by EMS within 10 minutes of FMC | I | B | Transmit electronically to PCI center for advance cath lab activation |
| FMC-to-device time | Target <=90 minutes for direct presentation; <=120 minutes for transfer patients | I | B | Count from first 12-lead ECG diagnosis or clinical recognition |
| Primary PCI as preferred strategy | Primary PCI when achievable within time targets | I | A | 24/7 PCI capability with experienced operators |
| Fibrinolysis when PCI delayed | Administer fibrinolysis within 30 minutes if PCI not available within 120 min of FMC | I | A | Tenecteplase weight-adjusted single bolus preferred for ease of administration |
| Transfer for angiography post-lysis | Routine transfer within 3-24 hours after successful fibrinolysis | I | A | Do not wait for recurrence of symptoms; early angiography improves outcomes |
| Rescue PCI | Immediate transfer for PCI if fibrinolysis fails (<50% ST resolution at 60-90 min) | I | B | Clinical judgment combined with ECG criteria guides decision |
| STEMI registry participation | All PCI centers participate in a quality registry | I | B | Enables benchmarking, identifies system delays, drives improvement |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Late presentation (12-48 hours) | Consider PCI in late presenters with ongoing symptoms or hemodynamic compromise | OAT trial showed no benefit for routine PCI of occluded infarct artery >24 hours without ongoing ischemia |
| Mechanical circulatory support in STEMI shock | IABP or Impella for cardiogenic shock complicating STEMI | IABP-SHOCK II showed no mortality benefit; Impella data from DanGer Shock trial are emerging |
| Hypothermia after cardiac arrest | Targeted temperature management at 33-36 degrees C | TTM2 trial showed no benefit of 33 C vs. normothermia; practice shifting toward fever avoidance |
| Non-culprit PCI timing in STEMI | Immediate complete revascularization vs. staged PCI | COMPLETE supports either approach; operator preference and clinical stability guide timing |
| Community AED programs | Public access defibrillation in high-risk areas | Cost-effectiveness varies by setting; bystander CPR training may have greater impact in resource-limited settings |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Tenecteplase**: Weight-adjusted single IV bolus (30-50 mg); preferred fibrinolytic for ease of prehospital administration
- **Alteplase**: 15 mg bolus, then 0.75 mg/kg over 30 min, then 0.5 mg/kg over 60 min; max 100 mg total
- **Streptokinase**: 1.5 MU IV over 60 min; cheapest option; cannot re-use within 5 days to 2 years
- **Aspirin**: 325 mg chewed at FMC
- **Clopidogrel**: 300 mg loading with fibrinolysis (600 mg if primary PCI); 75 mg daily maintenance
- **Ticagrelor/Prasugrel**: Preferred with primary PCI; not studied extensively with fibrinolysis
- **Enoxaparin**: 30 mg IV bolus then 1 mg/kg SC BID with fibrinolysis; weight-adjusted, no aPTT monitoring
- **UFH**: 60 U/kg bolus (max 4000 U) then 12 U/kg/hr (max 1000 U/hr) for primary PCI
- **Morphine**: 2-4 mg IV PRN for pain; use cautiously (may delay antiplatelet absorption)
- **Oxygen**: Only if SpO2 <90%; routine oxygen may be harmful (DETO2X-AMI)

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Streptokinase remains the most widely used fibrinolytic in India due to low cost (INR 3000-5000/dose) compared to tenecteplase (INR 25,000-35,000/dose). Recent Indian trial data (STREAM-like protocols) support tenecteplase use in pharmaco-invasive strategy. The Indian government has included tenecteplase in essential drug lists at some state levels. All standard antiplatelet and anticoagulant agents are available.
- **Cost considerations**: Primary PCI costs INR 1.5-3 lakh in India (procedure + stent + hospital stay). Government schemes (Ayushman Bharat/PMJAY) cover STEMI treatment up to INR 1.5 lakh at empaneled hospitals, significantly improving access. However, out-of-pocket costs remain significant for many families. The pharmaco-invasive strategy with streptokinase is the most cost-effective approach for rural India.
- **Cath lab practicality**: India has an estimated 3000+ cardiac catheterization laboratories, but distribution is highly urban-centric. The STEMI India network, Tamil Nadu STEMI network, and Kerala ACS registry are successful models of regionalized STEMI care. Hub-and-spoke models with spoke hospitals administering fibrinolysis and transferring to hub PCI centers are the backbone of STEMI care in non-metro India. EMS integration remains a major gap; most STEMI patients arrive by private transport. Prehospital ECG programs using handheld ECG devices with telemedicine interpretation are being piloted.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Post-STEMI medication adherence at 1 year is estimated at 50-60% in Indian registries, significantly below global benchmarks. Key barriers include cost, medication literacy, and fragmented follow-up. Structured post-MI clinics with nurse-led follow-up, SMS-based medication reminders, and community health worker involvement have shown improvement in pilot programs. Cardiac rehabilitation access remains limited to major urban centers. The CREATE registry has provided important Indian-specific data on STEMI outcomes and practice patterns.
