# 2023 ACC/AHA Heart Failure Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) added as a foundational pillar for HFrEF, making the quadruple therapy paradigm official: ARNI + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i.
2. SGLT2 inhibitors receive Class IIa recommendation for HFpEF (LVEF >40%), the first drug class to show benefit in this population (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER trials).
3. Rapid sequencing of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) endorsed: initiate all four pillars simultaneously or within 1-2 weeks rather than sequential up-titration over months.
4. HF staging updated: Stage A (at risk), Stage B (pre-HF with structural abnormalities), Stage C (symptomatic HF), Stage D (advanced HF). New terminology replaces prior classifications.
5. GLP-1 receptor agonists recognized for HF prevention in patients with obesity and T2DM, based on SELECT and STEP-HFpEF data.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| ARNI in HFrEF | Sacubitril/valsartan preferred over ACEi/ARB in NYHA II-III | I | A | 36-hour washout from ACEi before switching; no washout from ARB |
| Beta-blocker in HFrEF | Carvedilol, bisoprolol, or metoprolol succinate | I | A | Titrate to max tolerated dose; do not start in decompensated state |
| MRA in HFrEF | Spironolactone or eplerenone for NYHA II-IV with LVEF <=35% | I | A | Monitor potassium and creatinine; avoid if K+ >5.0 or eGFR <30 |
| SGLT2i in HFrEF | Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin regardless of diabetes status | I | A | Can initiate during hospitalization; monitor for genital infections |
| ICD for primary prevention | LVEF <=35% despite 3 months of optimal GDMT in NYHA II-III | I | A | Exclude if life expectancy <1 year or reversible cause |
| CRT | LVEF <=35%, LBBB, QRS >=150 ms, NYHA II-IV | I | A | CRT-D preferred; greatest benefit in true LBBB morphology |
| Diuretics for congestion | Loop diuretics for volume management in symptomatic HF | I | B | Furosemide, torsemide, or bumetanide; not mortality-reducing but essential for symptom relief |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| SGLT2i in HFpEF | Reasonable to use for HFpEF (LVEF >40%) | Hospitalization benefit clear; mortality benefit not definitively established |
| Vericiguat | May be considered in recently decompensated HFrEF | VICTORIA trial showed modest benefit; role in addition to quadruple therapy unclear |
| Ivabradine | Consider if HR >=70 bpm on max beta-blocker in sinus rhythm | Limited to sinus rhythm patients already on optimal beta-blocker dose |
| Iron replacement (IV) | IV iron for iron-deficient HFrEF patients | IRONMAN positive for events; HEART-FID showed mixed results; define iron deficiency properly |
| LVAD as destination therapy | Consider in advanced HF not eligible for transplant | Stroke, infection, and device malfunction remain significant complications |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Sacubitril/valsartan**: 24/26 mg BID titrated to 97/103 mg BID; monitor BP, K+, renal function
- **Carvedilol**: 3.125 mg BID titrated to 25 mg BID (50 mg BID if >85 kg)
- **Bisoprolol**: 1.25 mg daily titrated to 10 mg daily
- **Metoprolol succinate**: 12.5-25 mg daily titrated to 200 mg daily
- **Spironolactone**: 12.5-25 mg daily, max 50 mg; gynecomastia limits use in men
- **Eplerenone**: 25 mg daily, max 50 mg; fewer endocrine side effects than spironolactone
- **Dapagliflozin**: 10 mg daily; no dose titration needed; check eGFR (use if >=20)
- **Empagliflozin**: 10 mg daily; similar profile to dapagliflozin
- **Furosemide**: 20-240 mg daily (oral or IV); adjust based on volume status
- **Hydralazine/isosorbide dinitrate**: Alternative to ACEi/ARB/ARNI if intolerant; particularly effective in Black patients
- **Ivabradine**: 5-7.5 mg BID; only in sinus rhythm with HR >=70 bpm

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Generic sacubitril/valsartan is available from Indian manufacturers (INR 20-40/day for target dose), dramatically improving access. All beta-blockers, MRAs, and diuretics are available at very low cost. SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) are available as generics at INR 10-25/day, making quadruple therapy financially feasible. Ivabradine generics cost INR 10-15/day.
- **Cost considerations**: Quadruple therapy (ARNI + BB + MRA + SGLT2i) costs approximately INR 80-120/day with Indian generics, which is achievable for middle-class patients but remains significant for economically weaker sections. ICD and CRT devices cost INR 3-8 lakh, with limited insurance coverage. IV iron (ferric carboxymaltose) costs INR 4000-6000/infusion.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Coronary angiography is essential to exclude ischemic etiology in new HF. Viability assessment (stress echo, CMR, or nuclear) helps guide revascularization decisions. CRT implantation is available in most major cardiac centers but LV lead implantation expertise varies.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Heart failure management requires frequent follow-up and dose titration. Many Indian patients present late with advanced disease (NYHA III-IV). Rapid GDMT initiation before discharge is critical since outpatient titration often fails due to loss to follow-up. Home-based HF monitoring programs (daily weight, symptom diaries) should be emphasized. Fluid and salt restriction counseling must account for Indian dietary patterns. Cardiac rehabilitation and palliative care for advanced HF are significantly underdeveloped.
