# 2021 ESC/EACTS Valvular Heart Disease Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. TAVR indications expanded to patients aged >=75 years with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, regardless of surgical risk, based on cumulative evidence from PARTNER 3, Evolut Low Risk, and long-term follow-up data.
2. Heart Valve Team (multidisciplinary) approach mandated for all valve intervention decisions, analogous to the Heart Team in coronary disease.
3. Criteria for early intervention in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis updated: consider surgery/TAVR if exercise testing provokes symptoms, LVEF <55%, very severe AS (mean gradient >60 mmHg, Vmax >5 m/s), or BNP >3x age-adjusted normal.
4. Mitral valve repair preferred over replacement for primary mitral regurgitation (degenerative disease) with expected repair durability, with emphasis on referral to high-volume surgical centers.
5. Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER, MitraClip) receives Class IIb recommendation for secondary MR in selected HFrEF patients not responding to optimal medical therapy, following COAPT criteria.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Intervention for severe symptomatic AS | AVR (SAVR or TAVR) recommended for all symptomatic severe AS | I | A | Symptom onset: angina, syncope, dyspnea mark critical transition |
| TAVR in elderly (>=75 years) | TAVR preferred over SAVR in patients >=75 with suitable anatomy | I | A | Assess vascular access, anatomy with CT planning |
| SAVR in younger patients (<75) | SAVR preferred for younger patients with low surgical risk | I | B | Better long-term durability data; mechanical valve if <60 and accepts anticoagulation |
| MV repair for primary MR | Surgical repair preferred over replacement when feasible | I | B | Center experience is a key determinant of repair success |
| Anticoagulation for mechanical valves | Lifelong warfarin; target INR based on valve type and position | I | B | DOACs contraindicated (RE-ALIGN trial) |
| IE prophylaxis for high-risk patients | Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures in prosthetic valves | I | B | Amoxicillin 2 g orally 30-60 min prior |
| Echocardiographic surveillance | Regular echo follow-up for all moderate or greater valve disease | I | C | Annual for severe disease; every 2-3 years for moderate |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| TAVR in low-risk patients <75 years | May be considered after Heart Team discussion | 5-10 year TAVR durability data still maturing; structural valve degeneration risk |
| TEER for secondary MR | Consider in selected patients meeting COAPT criteria | MITRA-FR was negative; strict patient selection is essential; surgical MV repair has limited evidence in secondary MR |
| Intervention for asymptomatic severe AS | Consider if very severe (Vmax >5.5) or rapid progression | Natural history of truly asymptomatic severe AS is debated; exercise testing is critical to unmask symptoms |
| Sutureless/rapid-deployment valves | May reduce cross-clamp time in selected patients | Limited long-term outcome data; paravalvular leak rates may be higher |
| Anticoagulation after bioprosthetic AVR | Aspirin alone may be sufficient after 3 months | Some data support early anticoagulation for 3 months post-SAVR, but practice varies widely |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Warfarin**: For mechanical prosthetic valves; INR targets vary (2.5-3.5 for mitral, 2.0-3.0 for aortic depending on generation)
- **Aspirin**: 75-100 mg daily after bioprosthetic valve replacement (after initial 3-month period)
- **DOACs**: Contraindicated for mechanical valves; may be used in AF patients with bioprosthetic valves after 3 months
- **Diuretics**: For symptomatic congestion in valvular HF; loop diuretics as needed
- **Beta-blockers/CCBs**: For rate control in AF associated with valvular disease
- **ACEi/ARB**: For LV dysfunction or hypertension with valvular disease; avoid excessive afterload reduction in severe AS
- **Statins**: No evidence they slow AS progression; continue for atherosclerotic risk management
- **IE prophylaxis**: Amoxicillin 2 g PO (or clindamycin 600 mg if penicillin allergic) pre-dental procedures

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: Warfarin is universally available and affordable (INR 1-2/day). INR monitoring is accessible even at PHC level. DOACs are available but the specific contraindication in mechanical valves must be communicated clearly, as many patients in India have rheumatic mechanical valves.
- **Cost considerations**: TAVR valve costs (INR 15-25 lakh for imported; INR 5-8 lakh for Meril MyVal or similar Indian devices) are a major barrier. SAVR with imported bioprosthetic valves costs INR 3-5 lakh; mechanical valves are cheaper but require lifelong anticoagulation. MitraClip/TEER procedures cost INR 15-20 lakh and are available only at select centers.
- **Cath lab practicality**: TAVR programs are expanding rapidly in India. Major centers (AIIMS, Medanta, Narayana Health, Fortis) have established programs with excellent outcomes. CT planning for TAVR requires high-quality cardiac CT with 3D reconstruction capability. Indian-manufactured TAVR valves have significantly improved access. Balloon aortic valvuloplasty remains relevant as a bridge in hemodynamically unstable patients.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Rheumatic heart disease remains the predominant valvular pathology in India, unlike the degenerative disease seen in Western populations. Young patients with rheumatic valve disease on warfarin face lifelong INR monitoring challenges. Home-based INR monitoring devices (INR 5000-8000) can improve compliance. Secondary prophylaxis with penicillin for rheumatic fever prevention is frequently discontinued. Pregnancy in women with prosthetic valves requires specialized management and is a common clinical challenge in India.
