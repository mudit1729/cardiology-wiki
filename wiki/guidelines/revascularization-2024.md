# 2024 ESC/EACTS Myocardial Revascularization Guideline

## Top 5 Things That Changed

1. Intravascular imaging (IVUS or OCT) during PCI elevated to Class I recommendation for complex PCI, left main, and multivessel disease, moving from optional to standard-of-care.
2. CABG retains superiority for three-vessel disease and left main with high SYNTAX scores (>32), but PCI receives expanded indications for left main with low-intermediate SYNTAX scores when surgery risk is elevated.
3. Physiology-guided revascularization (FFR or iwFR) remains Class I for intermediate lesions (40-90% stenosis) in stable patients; angiography-only decisions are discouraged.
4. Drug-coated balloons (DCB) receive a Class IIa recommendation for in-stent restenosis and small vessel disease, reflecting growing evidence base.
5. Heart Team approach mandated for all left main, multivessel, and complex decisions, with formal documentation requirements.

## Strong Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Class | Level | Practical Note |
|-------|---------------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Intravascular imaging in left main PCI | IVUS or OCT for all left main interventions | I | A | Minimum stent area targets: 8 mm2 ostial LM, 7 mm2 polygon of confluence |
| FFR/iwFR for intermediate stenosis | Physiology-guided decision in stable CAD | I | A | FFR <=0.80 or iwFR <=0.89 indicates hemodynamic significance |
| CABG for three-vessel disease | CABG preferred when SYNTAX >32 and acceptable surgical risk | I | A | LIMA-to-LAD mandatory; consider bilateral IMA in younger patients |
| Radial artery access | Default access for diagnostic and interventional procedures | I | A | Reduces bleeding and vascular complications vs. femoral |
| DES over BMS | DES for all PCI indications | I | A | Modern thin-strut DES have very low late thrombosis rates |
| Antiplatelet post-PCI | DAPT with aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor | I | A | Duration based on ischemic vs. bleeding risk balance |
| Complete revascularization in STEMI | Treat non-culprit lesions during index hospitalization or staged | I | A | Based on COMPLETE and FIRE trials |

## Controversial / Weak Recommendations

| Topic | Recommendation | Why Uncertain |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| PCI for left main (low SYNTAX) | Acceptable alternative to CABG for SYNTAX <23 | 10-year EXCEL and NOBLE data show diverging outcomes; depends on center expertise |
| Chronic total occlusion PCI | Consider in experienced centers for symptom relief | No mortality benefit proven; high procedural complexity and resource use |
| Bioresorbable vascular scaffolds | Not recommended for routine use | Scaffold thrombosis concerns persist; may reconsider with next-generation devices |
| Mechanical circulatory support in high-risk PCI | Consider Impella or IABP for unprotected left main or severe LV dysfunction | PROTECT IV data mixed; operator experience and patient selection critical |
| Hybrid revascularization | LIMA-LAD + PCI to non-LAD vessels may be considered | Logistically complex; limited randomized data |

## Drug Therapy Summary

- **Aspirin**: 75-100 mg lifelong post-revascularization
- **Clopidogrel**: 75 mg daily post-PCI (6-12 months DAPT); preferred after CABG
- **Prasugrel/Ticagrelor**: Preferred over clopidogrel in ACS patients undergoing PCI
- **Short DAPT (1-3 months)**: Consider in high bleeding risk (HBR) patients, then P2Y12 monotherapy
- **Anticoagulation bridging**: Discontinue warfarin/DOAC pre-CABG; bridge with UFH if high thrombotic risk
- **Statin**: High-intensity pre-procedure and lifelong; periprocedural loading may reduce contrast nephropathy
- **Cangrelor**: IV P2Y12 inhibitor for periprocedural use when oral loading is not feasible

## India Practice Note

- **Drug availability**: All standard antiplatelet agents are available as generics. Cangrelor is available in select tertiary centers but rarely used due to cost. Prasugrel and ticagrelor generics are affordable (INR 5-15/tablet). Drug-eluting stents from Indian manufacturers (Meril, SMT) offer quality at significantly lower prices (INR 15,000-25,000 vs. INR 40,000-80,000 for imported DES).
- **Cost considerations**: IVUS/OCT adds INR 15,000-30,000 per case, which limits routine use despite guideline recommendations. FFR wires cost INR 25,000-35,000 per case. Pressure wire reuse (after resterilization) is practiced in many centers despite regulatory concerns. CABG remains cost-effective for multivessel disease in India given relatively lower surgical costs.
- **Cath lab practicality**: Radial access is standard in India with high operator proficiency. CTO programs are established in major centers (AIIMS, Medanta, Fortis, Apollo) with dedicated operators. Heart Team discussions happen informally in many centers; formal documentation is improving but not universal. The STEMI India registry shows improving door-to-balloon times in network hospitals.
- **Follow-up adherence**: Post-PCI DAPT compliance is a major concern, especially after 6 months when patients feel asymptomatic. Generic fixed-dose combinations help. Post-CABG patients often default on cardiac rehabilitation. Telemedicine follow-up is emerging as a tool to improve adherence in remote areas.
