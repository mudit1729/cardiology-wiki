# Interventional Cardiology

Interventional cardiology is the subspecialty of cardiology focused on catheter-based treatment of cardiovascular diseases. It encompasses percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), structural heart interventions, peripheral vascular interventions, and mechanical circulatory support. The field has transformed from simple balloon angioplasty in 1977 to a sophisticated discipline employing intravascular imaging, physiology-guided decision-making, complex stenting techniques, transcatheter valve therapies, and advanced hemodynamic support.

## Key Principles

- **Evidence-based revascularization**: Decisions guided by trial data, not angiographic appearance alone. Physiology-guided PCI ([[ffr-ifr-procedure]]) reduces unnecessary stenting and improves outcomes. The ISCHEMIA trial reinforced that stable CAD management should prioritize medical therapy with revascularization reserved for refractory symptoms or high-risk anatomy
- **Imaging-guided optimization**: Intravascular imaging ([[ivus-procedure]], [[oct-procedure]]) improves stent outcomes through better sizing, expansion assessment, and complication detection. RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI confirmed imaging guidance reduces major adverse events in complex PCI
- **Appropriate use criteria**: Heart Team discussions for complex decisions (left main, multivessel disease, structural heart). SYNTAX score and clinical risk assessment guide PCI vs. CABG decisions. Appropriate Use Criteria documents help standardize indications
- **Radial-first approach**: Transradial access ([[radial-access]]) is the default access route, reducing bleeding and vascular complications with equivalent procedural success. Distal radial access is the next evolution
- **Complete revascularization**: Treating all hemodynamically significant lesions improves outcomes in STEMI and multivessel disease (with nuanced timing and staging strategies). COMPLETE trial supports staged complete revascularization in STEMI
- **Calcium modification**: Severely calcified lesions ([[calcified-lesions]]) require dedicated modification strategies. IVL ([[intravascular-lithotripsy]]), rotablation ([[rotablation]]), and scoring balloons are essential tools for contemporary practice
- **Minimal residual disease**: Post-PCI optimization using imaging and physiology ensures best possible acute result, reducing target vessel failure
- **Complication readiness**: Every interventional cardiologist must be proficient in managing [[cath-lab-complications]], [[no-reflow]], and coronary perforation scenarios. Simulation-based training is increasingly emphasized
- **Mechanical circulatory support**: Understanding cardiogenic shock ([[cardiogenic-shock]]) management including IABP ([[iabp]]), Impella ([[impella]]), and ECMO is integral to modern interventional practice
- **Shared decision-making**: Patient preferences, comorbidities, and individual risk profile are central to treatment planning. Informed consent requires clear communication of risks and alternatives

## Key Trials

- [[FAME]] and [[FAME-2]] - Physiology-guided PCI in stable CAD and multivessel disease
- [[SYNTAX]] - PCI vs. CABG in three-vessel and left main disease
- [[EXCEL]] and [[NOBLE]] - PCI vs. CABG for left main disease
- [[ISCHEMIA]] - Invasive vs. conservative strategy in stable ischemic heart disease
- [[PARTNER]] series and [[Evolut-Low-Risk]] - TAVR across surgical risk categories
- [[COAPT]] - TEER for secondary mitral regurgitation in heart failure
- [[CULPRIT-SHOCK]] - Culprit-only PCI in cardiogenic shock
- [[DanGer-Shock]] - Impella in AMI cardiogenic shock
- [[ULTIMATE]] and [[RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI]] - Imaging-guided PCI for improved outcomes
- [[DISRUPT-CAD-III]] - Intravascular lithotripsy for calcified coronary lesions

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC/EACTS 2024 Chronic Coronary Syndromes**: Physiology or imaging-guided PCI recommended for intermediate stenosis (Class I). Heart Team decision for left main and multivessel disease. GDMT optimization before elective revascularization. CTO PCI for symptomatic patients with documented ischemia and viability
- **ESC 2023 ACS Guidelines**: Primary PCI within 120 minutes for STEMI (Class I). Radial access preferred (Class I). Early invasive strategy for high-risk NSTEMI (<24 hours). Complete revascularization before discharge for STEMI. Potent P2Y12 inhibitors (ticagrelor/prasugrel) over clopidogrel
- **ACC/AHA 2021 Revascularization Guidelines**: SYNTAX score integration for PCI vs. CABG decision. Intravascular imaging for left main PCI (Class IIa). Mechanical support for high-risk PCI in select patients
- **ESC 2024 Valvular Heart Disease**: TAVR standard for severe AS in patients >= 75 years. Heart Team assessment for ages 65-75. SAVR preferred under 65. TEER for selected patients with secondary MR meeting COAPT criteria (Class IIa)
- **Competency Standards**: SCAI recommends minimum case volumes for interventional fellowship training. Maintenance of competency requires ongoing procedural volume and continuing education

## India Practice Lens

Interventional cardiology in India has grown rapidly, with over 3,500 cath labs performing an estimated 7-8 lakh PCI procedures annually. India occupies a unique position globally due to several factors:

- **Volume and complexity**: Indian interventionalists perform high-volume, complex PCI including CTO, bifurcation, and calcified lesion management at major centers. Operator skill levels are world-class at high-volume centers
- **Cost innovation**: Indian-manufactured stents (Supraflex, Biomime, Yukon) provide quality DES at a fraction of international costs. NPPA price capping has further improved affordability. This model is increasingly studied globally for cost-effective cardiovascular care
- **Primary PCI networks**: STEMI treatment networks are improving in metropolitan areas but significant gaps remain in rural India. Door-to-balloon time targets are met inconsistently outside major cities
- **Structural heart growth**: TAVR and TEER programs are expanding at select centers but remain limited by device costs. Indigenous TAVR valve development could be transformative
- **Technology adoption**: IVUS, OCT, and FFR/iFR adoption is growing but remains below optimal utilization. Cost is the primary barrier; demonstrated cost-effectiveness data may drive adoption
- **Training ecosystem**: CSI (Cardiological Society of India), ISCAI, NIC (National Interventional Council), and dedicated fellowship programs produce well-trained interventionalists. International proctorship and workshop programs supplement formal training
- **Unique disease patterns**: Younger onset CAD, smaller coronary arteries, higher diabetes prevalence, and rheumatic heart disease create distinct challenges for Indian interventionalists compared to Western populations
- **Government insurance**: Ayushman Bharat and state-level insurance schemes are expanding coverage for cardiovascular procedures, though TAVR and advanced MCS remain largely uncovered

The Indian interventional cardiology community continues to innovate in cost-effective care delivery, frugal device development, and high-volume complex PCI, contributing significantly to global practice.

**Key Indian Conferences and Training Platforms:**
- NIC (National Interventional Council) - CSI annual meeting with live case demonstrations
- TCTAP Asia-Pacific - with significant Indian participation
- India Live - premier live case course in interventional cardiology
- ISHACON - Indian Society of Hypertension conference (preventive cardiology focus)
- Regional and state-level interventional workshops with hands-on simulation training

**Emerging Frontiers in Indian Interventional Cardiology:**
- Robotic-assisted PCI programs at select Indian centers
- AI-assisted angiographic interpretation and procedure planning
- 3D-printed cardiac models for procedural planning (structural heart)
- Biodegradable stent technology development by Indian manufacturers
- Telemedicine-guided PCI proctorship for tier-2/3 city operators
- Physiology-guided PCI advocacy campaigns to reduce unnecessary stenting
- Indigenous mechanical circulatory support device development
- Quality registries and outcome benchmarking across Indian cath labs
- Mobile cath lab deployment for underserved regions
- Integration of AI-based risk scoring into clinical workflows
