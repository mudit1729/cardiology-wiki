# Coronary Physiology

Coronary physiology encompasses the hemodynamic principles governing blood flow through the coronary circulation and the invasive and non-invasive tools used to assess the functional significance of coronary stenoses. Physiology-guided PCI -- using fractional flow reserve (FFR) or instantaneous wave-free ratio (iFR) -- has fundamentally changed interventional practice by demonstrating that angiographic appearance alone is insufficient to determine the hemodynamic impact of coronary lesions. Deferring intervention on physiologically non-significant stenoses improves outcomes and reduces costs.

## Key Principles

- **Angiography overestimates and underestimates stenosis severity**: Visual assessment of coronary stenoses is inaccurate in 30-40% of intermediate lesions. Even experienced operators show poor interobserver agreement for stenosis severity estimation. FFR/iFR provides objective hemodynamic assessment ([[ffr-ifr-procedure]])
- **FFR measures pressure ratio during maximal hyperemia**: FFR = Pd/Pa during adenosine-induced hyperemia. FFR <= 0.80 indicates hemodynamic significance; FFR > 0.80 can be safely deferred with medical therapy. The DEFER trial demonstrated 15-year safety of deferring FFR-negative lesions
- **iFR measures resting pressure ratio**: iFR is measured during the wave-free period of diastole without hyperemia. iFR <= 0.89 indicates significance; iFR > 0.89 can be deferred. Non-inferior to FFR for guiding revascularization (DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART). Eliminates adenosine cost and side effects
- **Grey zone management**: FFR values between 0.75-0.80 and iFR values between 0.86-0.93 represent a grey zone where clinical context, symptoms, and adjunctive data (imaging, non-invasive testing) should inform decision-making
- **Microvascular dysfunction**: Beyond epicardial stenosis, coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) contributes to ischemia. CFR (coronary flow reserve) and IMR (index of microcirculatory resistance) assess microvascular function. CMD is an independent predictor of adverse outcomes
- **Diffuse disease alters interpretation**: FFR/iFR may underestimate the significance of serial stenoses in diffusely diseased vessels. Pullback assessment helps identify the most significant focal drop for targeted PCI
- **Post-PCI physiology**: Residual ischemia after PCI (post-PCI FFR < 0.90) predicts worse outcomes. Physiology can confirm adequacy of intervention and identify residual disease
- **Non-hyperemic pressure ratios (NHPR)**: iFR, RFR (resting full-cycle ratio), and DFR (diastolic pressure ratio) are all non-hyperemic indices with equivalent diagnostic accuracy, eliminating the need for adenosine
- **Coronary angiography-derived physiology**: CT-derived FFR (FFRCT) and angiography-derived FFR (QFR, vFFR) provide non-invasive or wire-free physiological assessment, expanding the applicability of physiology-guided decisions

## Physiological Indices Comparison

| Index | Type | Hyperemia | Threshold | Key Advantage |
|-------|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| FFR | Pressure | Required | <= 0.80 | Gold standard; strongest evidence base |
| iFR | Pressure | Not required | <= 0.89 | No adenosine; faster; equivalent outcomes |
| RFR | Pressure | Not required | <= 0.89 | Full-cycle resting index; vendor-neutral |
| CFR | Flow | Required | <2.0 | Assesses epicardial + microvascular |
| IMR | Resistance | Required | >= 25 | Specific for microvascular dysfunction |
| QFR | Angiography-derived | Not required | <= 0.80 | Wire-free; software-based |
| FFRCT | CT-derived | Not required | <= 0.80 | Non-invasive; pre-cath planning |

## Key Trials

- [[FAME]] - FFR-guided PCI reduced MACE vs. angiography-guided PCI in multivessel disease
- [[FAME-2]] - FFR-guided PCI + OMT reduced urgent revascularization vs. OMT alone in stable CAD with positive FFR
- [[DEFINE-FLAIR]] - iFR non-inferior to FFR for guiding revascularization decisions at 1 year
- [[iFR-SWEDEHEART]] - iFR non-inferior to FFR; no difference in death, MI, or revascularization
- [[DEFER]] - Deferring PCI for FFR >0.75 is safe at 15-year follow-up
- [[RIPCORD-2]] - Routine FFR changes management plan in 25% of patients with stable angina
- [[COMBINE-OCT-FFR]] - Combining physiology and imaging for risk stratification
- [[TARGET-FFR]] - Physiology-guided optimization of PCI results
- [[FAVOR-III-China]] - QFR-guided PCI vs. angiography-guided PCI: QFR superior
- [[CORMICA]] - Interventional diagnostic procedure for CMD using CFR, IMR, and acetylcholine testing

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC 2024 CCS Guidelines**: FFR or iFR recommended to assess hemodynamic significance of intermediate coronary stenoses (50-90%) when non-invasive testing is inconclusive or unavailable (Class I). Physiology-guided PCI improves outcomes compared to angiography-guided PCI. FFRCT recommended for non-invasive assessment when CCTA shows intermediate stenosis (Class IIa)
- **ACC/AHA 2021 Revascularization Guidelines**: FFR or iFR for intermediate stenosis assessment (Class I). Deferral of revascularization for FFR > 0.80 or iFR > 0.89 is safe and appropriate
- **ESC 2023 ACS Guidelines**: Physiology-guided assessment of non-culprit lesions is recommended during staged procedures in NSTEMI and after primary PCI in STEMI. FFR/iFR should not be used on the culprit lesion in ACS (unreliable due to microvascular dysfunction)
- **EuroPCR/EAPCI Consensus on Microvascular Disease**: CFR <2.0 and IMR >= 25 define microvascular dysfunction. Comprehensive physiological assessment (FFR + CFR + IMR) identifies four endotypes: normal, epicardial disease, microvascular disease, and combined

## India Practice Lens

Coronary physiology adoption in India is growing but remains significantly underutilized relative to guideline recommendations:

- **Low adoption rate**: Fewer than 5% of PCI procedures in India are guided by FFR/iFR, compared to 15-30% in Europe and the US. The primary barriers are cost and awareness
- **Wire cost as the main barrier**: Pressure wire cost (INR 30,000-50,000) is a significant deterrent in a cost-sensitive environment. Each wire adds substantially to procedural cost. Some centers reuse pressure wires, though this is not manufacturer-recommended
- **IC adenosine preferred**: Intracoronary adenosine bolus is preferred over IV infusion in India due to lower cost (no infusion pump needed) and faster procedure completion. IC adenosine provides adequate hyperemia for single-vessel FFR assessment
- **iFR adoption growing**: The elimination of adenosine with iFR is attractive in Indian practice, reducing both cost and procedure time. iFR adoption is increasing among physiology-aware operators
- **Cost-effectiveness argument**: FFR/iFR prevents unnecessary stenting, potentially saving INR 50,000-1,00,000 per deferred stent. This cost-offset argument needs to be emphasized in Indian practice to drive adoption
- **Wire-free physiology**: Angiography-derived FFR (QFR, vFFR) is an appealing concept for India, eliminating pressure wire costs entirely. Software-based solutions could dramatically increase physiology-guided decision-making in Indian cath labs
- **FFRCT for non-invasive assessment**: CT-derived FFR (HeartFlow) is available at select centers in India. Could reduce unnecessary diagnostic angiography and improve triage
- **Training gap**: Physiology-guided PCI training needs to be incorporated more systematically into Indian interventional cardiology fellowships. Workshops at CSI and NIC have increased awareness but hands-on training opportunities remain limited
- **Microvascular disease assessment**: CMD assessment using CFR and IMR is performed at very few centers in India. Given the high prevalence of diabetes and microvascular disease in Indian patients, this represents an important diagnostic gap
- **Research opportunities**: Indian population data on FFR/iFR outcomes, cost-effectiveness analysis in Indian healthcare economics, and validation of wire-free physiology tools in South Asian coronary anatomy represent important research opportunities
- **Small vessel disease**: Indian patients frequently have small-caliber coronary arteries. Physiology assessment is particularly valuable in small vessels where angiographic assessment is even more unreliable and stenting carries higher risk
- **Diabetes and microvascular dysfunction**: Given the extremely high diabetes prevalence in India, coronary microvascular disease is likely underdiagnosed. Comprehensive physiological assessment (FFR + CFR + IMR) could identify patients with CMD who would benefit from targeted medical therapy rather than PCI
- **Registry data needed**: An Indian physiology-guided PCI registry documenting outcomes, cost-effectiveness, and practice patterns would provide valuable data to support guideline adoption and insurance reimbursement advocacy
- **Integration with CCTA**: Combining anatomical assessment with CCTA and functional assessment with FFRCT or invasive physiology creates a comprehensive diagnostic pathway well-suited to Indian practice. This tiered approach could optimize resource utilization across different levels of cardiac care facilities
- **Education and advocacy**: Professional society endorsement and structured physiology training modules within Indian DM and fellowship curricula are needed to move physiology-guided PCI from niche practice to standard of care across Indian cath labs
- **Operator volume and learning curve**: FFR/iFR measurement is technically straightforward but interpretation in complex scenarios (serial lesions, diffuse disease, post-PCI assessment) requires experience. Dedicated physiology training courses are essential
