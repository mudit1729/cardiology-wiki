# Coronary Artery Disease

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is the progressive atherosclerotic narrowing of the coronary arteries, resulting in myocardial ischemia when oxygen supply fails to meet demand. It exists on a spectrum from subclinical atherosclerosis to stable angina to acute coronary syndromes (ACS), and remains the leading cause of cardiovascular mortality worldwide. Modern management integrates risk factor modification, guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), and selective revascularization.

## Key Principles

- **Atherosclerosis is a systemic disease**: CAD rarely exists in isolation. Patients should be assessed for concurrent peripheral arterial disease, cerebrovascular disease, and renal artery disease
- **Plaque vulnerability matters more than stenosis severity**: Most acute coronary syndromes arise from rupture or erosion of non-obstructive plaques. Identifying vulnerable plaques remains a major research focus ([[oct-procedure]] can identify thin-cap fibroatheroma)
- **Medical therapy is foundational**: High-intensity statins, aspirin, ACE inhibitors/ARBs (where indicated), and risk factor control form the cornerstone of management regardless of revascularization decisions
- **Revascularization for ischemia**: PCI ([[pci-basics]]) or CABG should be offered for hemodynamically significant stenoses causing symptoms or large ischemic burden, not based on angiographic appearance alone
- **Physiology-guided decisions**: FFR/iFR ([[ffr-ifr-procedure]]) objectify ischemic significance and reduce unnecessary stenting. FAME trial data demonstrates improved outcomes with physiology guidance
- **Risk stratification drives management**: SYNTAX score for anatomical complexity, clinical risk scores (EuroSCORE, STS) for surgical risk, and ischemic burden guide Heart Team decisions on PCI vs. CABG vs. medical therapy
- **Secondary prevention is lifelong**: Post-revascularization management requires ongoing antiplatelet therapy ([[antiplatelet-therapy]]), statin therapy, diabetes management, and lifestyle modification
- **Lipid management is aggressive**: LDL-C targets of <55 mg/dL for very high-risk patients and <70 mg/dL for high-risk patients. High-intensity statin plus ezetimibe as needed. PCSK9 inhibitors for refractory cases
- **Coronary calcium scoring**: CAC score provides powerful risk stratification in asymptomatic individuals. CAC = 0 carries excellent prognosis. Rising CAC scores may trigger more aggressive risk factor management
- **Cardiac rehabilitation**: Structured exercise-based rehabilitation improves outcomes after MI and revascularization but remains underutilized globally

## Risk Factor Management Targets

| Risk Factor | Target | Key Intervention |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|
| LDL-C | <55 mg/dL (very high risk) | High-intensity statin + ezetimibe +/- PCSK9i |
| Blood Pressure | <130/80 mmHg | ACEi/ARB + CCB/thiazide |
| HbA1c | <7% (individualized) | Metformin + SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA |
| Smoking | Complete cessation | Counseling + pharmacotherapy |
| Physical Activity | 150 min/week moderate | Cardiac rehabilitation |
| BMI | <25 kg/m2 | Diet + exercise + GLP-1 RA |
| Antiplatelet | Lifelong aspirin or clopidogrel | See [[antiplatelet-therapy]] |

## Key Trials

- [[ISCHEMIA]] - Invasive strategy did not reduce death or MI vs. conservative strategy in stable CAD with moderate-severe ischemia (but improved angina)
- [[FAME]] and [[FAME-2]] - FFR-guided PCI improves outcomes compared to angiography-guided PCI; FFR-guided PCI reduces urgent revascularization vs. medical therapy
- [[SYNTAX]] - PCI vs. CABG for three-vessel disease; CABG superior for high SYNTAX scores
- [[COURAGE]] - PCI + OMT vs. OMT alone in stable CAD; no mortality benefit from initial PCI
- [[ORBITA]] - PCI vs. sham procedure for stable single-vessel CAD; no improvement in exercise time
- [[ORBITA-2]] - PCI vs. placebo in stable angina (off medications); PCI improved symptom burden
- [[REVIVED-BCIS2]] - PCI did not reduce death or HF hospitalization in ischemic cardiomyopathy
- [[SCOT-HEART]] - CCTA-guided management reduced coronary heart disease death and MI
- [[FOURIER]] and [[ODYSSEY Outcomes]] - PCSK9 inhibitors reduce MACE in high-risk CAD

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC 2024 Chronic Coronary Syndromes**: Lifestyle modification and GDMT for all patients. Non-invasive functional testing or CCTA for initial diagnostic evaluation. Invasive angiography for high-risk patients or those with refractory symptoms. Revascularization guided by symptoms, ischemia, and anatomy
- **ACC/AHA 2023 Stable Ischemic Heart Disease**: High-intensity statin for all with established CAD. Antiplatelet therapy (aspirin or clopidogrel). SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 RA for CAD patients with diabetes. BP target <130/80 mmHg. HbA1c target individualized
- **ESC 2023 ACS Guidelines**: See [[acute-coronary-syndromes]] for detailed ACS-specific recommendations
- **ESC 2019 Diabetes Guidelines**: SGLT2 inhibitors recommended for CAD patients with diabetes. Metformin remains first-line. Comprehensive cardiovascular risk management in diabetes

## India Practice Lens

CAD is the leading cause of mortality in India, with epidemiological patterns distinct from Western populations:

- **Younger onset**: Indians develop CAD 10-15 years earlier than Western populations. Premature CAD (age <45 years) is disproportionately common, attributed to genetic predisposition (Lp(a) levels, familial hypercholesterolemia), diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and lifestyle factors
- **Higher diabetes burden**: Over 100 million Indians have diabetes, and diabetic CAD involves diffuse, small-vessel disease with higher rates of multivessel disease, complicating revascularization decisions
- **Smaller coronary arteries**: Indian patients have smaller reference vessel diameters on average, affecting stent sizing and outcomes. Small-vessel PCI is more commonly required
- **Risk factor management gaps**: Despite awareness, targets for LDL-C, HbA1c, and blood pressure remain poorly achieved in community practice. High-intensity statin utilization is suboptimal
- **Tobacco use**: Bidis and smokeless tobacco (gutka, pan masala) are unique contributors to CAD in India beyond cigarette smoking
- **Vegetarian population and lipids**: Despite vegetarian diets in a significant proportion, carbohydrate-heavy diets contribute to metabolic syndrome and atherogenic dyslipidemia
- **CCTA adoption**: Coronary CT angiography is increasingly available and affordable in India, offering an excellent non-invasive diagnostic pathway before committing to invasive angiography
- **Revascularization access**: PCI access has improved dramatically with cath lab expansion across India. CABG access is more limited but growing. Heart Team decisions for complex disease need standardization
- **Generic medication availability**: High-quality generic statins, antiplatelets, and antihypertensives make GDMT affordable in India. PCSK9 inhibitor access is limited by cost (INR 10,000-40,000/month)
- **Cardiac rehabilitation**: Structured cardiac rehab programs are scarce in India; community-based and digital models are emerging as alternatives
- **Air pollution**: Ambient and household air pollution in India is a significant and underappreciated risk factor for CAD, contributing to endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and accelerated atherosclerosis
- **Lipoprotein(a)**: South Asians have among the highest Lp(a) levels globally. Elevated Lp(a) is an independent risk factor for premature CAD and is not modifiable with statins. Novel Lp(a)-lowering therapies (antisense oligonucleotides) are in clinical trials
- **Familial hypercholesterolemia**: Underdiagnosed in India; cascade screening programs are needed. Prevalence estimated at 1 in 250, meaning approximately 5 million Indians may be affected
- **Preventive cardiology gaps**: Population-level screening for cardiovascular risk factors at primary health centers (under NPCDCS) is expanding but implementation quality varies significantly
- **Research contributions**: Indian registries (CREATE, DEFI, Kerala ACS) provide important epidemiological and outcome data specific to the South Asian population
- **Cost-effective diagnostic pathways**: Risk-factor-based screening followed by CCTA (where available) and selective invasive angiography represents a cost-effective diagnostic algorithm suited to Indian practice
- **Polypill strategy**: Fixed-dose combination pills containing statin, aspirin, and antihypertensive agents have been studied in Indian populations (TIPS-3) and may improve adherence for primary and secondary prevention
- **Yoga and traditional practices**: While lifestyle modification is essential, evidence for yoga-based cardiac rehabilitation is growing with Indian-led clinical trials demonstrating benefits on exercise tolerance, quality of life, and risk factor control
