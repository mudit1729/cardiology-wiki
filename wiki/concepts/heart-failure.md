# Heart Failure

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome characterized by symptoms and signs caused by structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities, resulting in reduced cardiac output and/or elevated intracardiac pressures. Modern HF management has been revolutionized by the "four pillars" of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF and the emergence of SGLT2 inhibitors as therapy across the HF spectrum. Interventional cardiologists play critical roles through revascularization ([[pci-basics]]), device therapy ([[crt]], [[icd-basics]]), and mechanical circulatory support ([[cardiogenic-shock]]).

## Key Principles

- **HF classification matters**: HFrEF (LVEF <= 40%), HFmrEF (LVEF 41-49%), and HFpEF (LVEF >= 50%) have distinct pathophysiology and evidence bases. All benefit from SGLT2 inhibitors
- **Four pillars of HFrEF therapy**: ACEi/ARB/ARNI + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2 inhibitor should be initiated early (ideally before discharge) and uptitrated to target doses. Simultaneous initiation is safe and preferred over sequential addition
- **SGLT2 inhibitors across the spectrum**: Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin reduce HF hospitalization and CV death in HFrEF (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced) and HF hospitalization in HFpEF (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER). They are now recommended regardless of diabetes status
- **Neurohormonal modulation**: The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and sympathetic nervous system drive HF progression. ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan) is superior to ACEi in HFrEF (PARADIGM-HF)
- **Device therapy improves survival**: ICD for primary prevention of SCD in LVEF <= 35%. CRT for LVEF <= 35% with LBBB and QRS >= 130 ms. Device eligibility should be reassessed after 3 months of optimized GDMT
- **Revascularization for ischemic HF**: Consider revascularization in ischemic cardiomyopathy with viable myocardium and significant coronary disease, though REVIVED-BCIS2 and STICH trials show nuanced benefit
- **Volume management**: Diuretics relieve congestion but are not disease-modifying. Loop diuretics dosed to euvolemia. Combination diuretic therapy for refractory congestion (sequential nephron blockade)
- **Advanced HF therapies**: LVAD and heart transplant for end-stage HF. Identifying the appropriate timing for referral to advanced HF centers is critical
- **Comorbidity management**: Iron deficiency (IV iron if ferritin <100 or ferritin 100-300 with TSAT <20%), AF (rate/rhythm control), sleep-disordered breathing, renal dysfunction, and depression all require attention
- **Biomarker-guided management**: NT-proBNP and BNP for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment monitoring. Rising natriuretic peptides despite therapy indicate need for intensification or reassessment
- **Multidisciplinary HF clinics**: Nurse-led, protocol-driven HF clinics improve medication uptitration, reduce readmissions, and improve quality of life. The team approach includes cardiologist, HF nurse, pharmacist, dietitian, and social worker

## Four Pillars of HFrEF Therapy

| Pillar | Drug Class | Target Dose Example | Mortality Benefit |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | ACEi/ARB/ARNI | Sacubitril/valsartan 97/103 mg BID | Yes (PARADIGM-HF) |
| 2 | Beta-blocker | Carvedilol 25 mg BID, Bisoprolol 10 mg OD | Yes (MERIT-HF, CIBIS-II) |
| 3 | MRA | Spironolactone 50 mg OD, Eplerenone 50 mg OD | Yes (RALES, EMPHASIS-HF) |
| 4 | SGLT2 inhibitor | Dapagliflozin 10 mg OD, Empagliflozin 10 mg OD | Yes (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced) |

## Key Trials

- [[DAPA-HF]] - Dapagliflozin in HFrEF: reduced CV death and HF hospitalization regardless of diabetes
- [[EMPEROR-Reduced]] - Empagliflozin in HFrEF: reduced CV death and HF hospitalization
- [[EMPEROR-Preserved]] - Empagliflozin in HFpEF: reduced HF hospitalization
- [[DELIVER]] - Dapagliflozin in HFpEF: reduced CV death and HF hospitalization
- [[PARADIGM-HF]] - Sacubitril/valsartan vs. enalapril in HFrEF: superior mortality reduction
- [[STRONG-HF]] - Rapid uptitration of HF medications after acute HF: improved outcomes
- [[MADIT-II]] and [[SCD-HeFT]] - ICD for primary prevention in HFrEF
- [[COMPANION]] and [[CARE-HF]] - CRT in HFrEF with dyssynchrony
- [[DanGer-Shock]] - Impella in AMI cardiogenic shock
- [[STICH]] - CABG + OMT vs. OMT alone in ischemic cardiomyopathy
- [[REVIVED-BCIS2]] - PCI vs. OMT in ischemic cardiomyopathy (no benefit from PCI)
- [[IRONMAN]] and [[AFFIRM-AHF]] - IV iron in HF with iron deficiency
- [[COAPT]] - TEER for secondary MR in HFrEF: mortality benefit

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC 2023 Heart Failure Guidelines (Focused Update)**: All four pillars of GDMT should be initiated early and simultaneously in HFrEF. SGLT2 inhibitors recommended for HFmrEF and HFpEF. ARNI preferred over ACEi in HFrEF. Rapid uptitration of GDMT (STRONG-HF model). CRT for LVEF <= 35%, LBBB, QRS >= 130 ms. ICD for primary prevention in LVEF <= 35% after >= 3 months GDMT
- **ACC/AHA 2022 Heart Failure Guidelines**: Four-pillar GDMT endorsed. SGLT2 inhibitors Class I for HFrEF and Class IIa for HFpEF. Hydralazine-nitrate combination for Black patients with HFrEF. Ivabradine for sinus rate >70 bpm on maximally tolerated beta-blocker
- **ESC 2024 CCS Guidelines**: Revascularization for ischemic cardiomyopathy guided by viability and symptoms. TEER for functional MR meeting COAPT criteria in HF patients

## India Practice Lens

Heart failure is a growing epidemic in India with unique challenges related to etiology, access to therapy, and healthcare infrastructure:

- **Etiological differences**: Ischemic cardiomyopathy is the most common cause, but rheumatic heart disease, dilated cardiomyopathy (idiopathic, peripartum), and hypertensive heart disease are significant contributors. Endomyocardial fibrosis is seen in specific geographic regions
- **Younger HF population**: Indian HF patients are 10-15 years younger than Western counterparts, making long-term management strategies particularly important
- **GDMT utilization gap**: Despite availability of affordable generic medications, GDMT prescription and uptitration remain suboptimal in India. A significant proportion of eligible HFrEF patients are not on all four pillars at target doses
- **SGLT2 inhibitor adoption**: Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are available in India at competitive prices (INR 15-30/day). Adoption is growing rapidly, driven by strong trial evidence and cardiologist awareness
- **Sacubitril/valsartan access**: Available as generic formulations in India (INR 20-60/day), making ARNI therapy more accessible than in many countries. Uptake is growing but remains below potential
- **Device therapy barriers**: ICD and CRT implantation rates in India are far below guideline-recommended levels due to cost (ICD INR 3-5 lakh, CRT INR 6-10 lakh). This represents a major unmet need. Government pricing regulations have helped but not fully addressed the gap
- **Advanced HF and transplant**: Heart transplant programs exist at select centers (approximately 200-300 transplants/year nationally). LVAD programs are in early stages. Donor organ shortage is the primary limitation
- **Telemedicine for HF**: Remote monitoring and telemedicine-based HF management programs are emerging and well-suited to India's geography and smartphone penetration
- **Polypill strategies**: Fixed-dose combination pills for HF (combining ACEi/ARB + beta-blocker + MRA) could improve adherence and are an area of active interest for Indian pharmaceutical companies
- **Research contributions**: Indian HF registries (Trivandrum Heart Failure Registry, THFR) provide important epidemiological data. Indian sites contribute to major global HF trials
- **Chagas disease analogy**: While Chagas cardiomyopathy is not prevalent in India, other infectious etiologies (viral myocarditis, tuberculosis-related pericardial constriction) contribute to the HF burden and require region-specific diagnostic algorithms
- **Peripartum cardiomyopathy**: PPCM is an important cause of HF in young Indian women. Early recognition, GDMT initiation, and bromocriptine therapy (BEACON trial data) can improve recovery rates
- **Iron deficiency management**: Iron deficiency is highly prevalent in Indian HF patients due to nutritional factors. IV ferric carboxymaltose is available in India at reasonable cost and should be administered per guideline criteria
- **Cardiac amyloidosis**: ATTR cardiac amyloidosis is increasingly recognized in India as a cause of HFpEF in the elderly. Tafamidis is available but expensive (INR 1-2 lakh/month). Bone scintigraphy (Tc-99m PYP) for non-invasive diagnosis is widely available
- **Palliative care in HF**: End-stage HF palliative care services are underdeveloped in India. Integrating palliative care into HF management for patients not eligible for transplant or LVAD is an important unmet need
- **Community health worker models**: Training community health workers (ASHAs) in basic HF symptom recognition, medication adherence monitoring, and early decompensation detection could extend HF care to rural India
