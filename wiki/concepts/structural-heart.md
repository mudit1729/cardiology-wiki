# Structural Heart Disease

Structural heart disease encompasses pathology of the cardiac valves, septae, myocardium, and great vessels that can be treated with catheter-based interventions. The field has experienced explosive growth, evolving from balloon valvuloplasty and ASD closure to sophisticated transcatheter valve replacement, edge-to-edge repair, paravalvular leak closure, and left atrial appendage occlusion. Structural heart interventions increasingly offer less-invasive alternatives to open-heart surgery for appropriately selected patients.

## Key Principles

- **Multimodality imaging is essential**: Cardiac CT, 3D transesophageal echocardiography, and cardiac MRI are indispensable for patient selection, procedural planning, and intraprocedural guidance in structural heart interventions
- **Heart Team approach**: Collaborative decision-making between interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging specialists, and heart failure physicians is mandatory for structural heart procedures
- **Patient selection determines outcomes**: Careful assessment of anatomy, comorbidities, frailty, and life expectancy drives appropriate patient selection. Not every anatomically feasible intervention is clinically appropriate
- **TAVR has transformed aortic stenosis management**: TAVR ([[tavr-procedure]]) is now the dominant treatment for severe symptomatic aortic stenosis across all surgical risk categories in patients over 65-70 years. Long-term durability data beyond 10 years are still maturing
- **Mitral interventions are evolving**: TEER ([[mitral-interventions]]) is established for secondary MR in heart failure (COAPT criteria) and high-risk primary MR. Transcatheter mitral valve replacement is investigational
- **Tricuspid interventions are emerging**: Transcatheter tricuspid repair and replacement are in early clinical adoption for severe tricuspid regurgitation in surgical high-risk patients
- **LAA occlusion reduces stroke risk**: Percutaneous LAA closure (Watchman, Amulet) is an alternative to long-term anticoagulation for stroke prevention in AF patients who cannot tolerate OAC
- **Procedural volumes matter**: Structural heart outcomes are strongly volume-dependent. Centers and operators performing high volumes achieve better results

## Key Trials

- [[PARTNER-1]] through [[PARTNER-3]] - TAVR across surgical risk categories (balloon-expandable)
- [[Evolut-Low-Risk]] - Self-expanding TAVR in low surgical risk patients
- [[COAPT]] - TEER for secondary MR in heart failure: significant mortality and HF hospitalization reduction
- [[MITRA-FR]] - TEER for secondary MR: no benefit (different patient selection than COAPT)
- [[EVEREST-II]] - MitraClip vs. surgery for primary MR
- [[CLASP-IID/IIF]] - PASCAL system for MR
- [[TRILUMINATE]] - Transcatheter tricuspid repair
- [[PROTECT-AF]] and [[PREVAIL]] - Watchman LAA closure vs. warfarin
- [[AMULET-IDE]] - Amulet vs. Watchman for LAA closure
- [[RESHAPE-HF2]] - TEER for secondary MR in heart failure (supports COAPT findings)
- [[NOTION]] - TAVR vs. SAVR in low-risk patients (small RCT)

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC 2024 Valvular Heart Disease**: TAVR recommended for severe AS in patients >= 75 years (Class I). Heart Team assessment for patients 65-75 years considering anatomy, comorbidities, and patient preference. SAVR preferred for patients <65 years (durability concerns with bioprosthetic valves). TEER for secondary MR in HF patients meeting COAPT criteria (Class IIa). Surgical repair remains gold standard for primary MR in suitable candidates
- **ACC/AHA 2020 Valvular Heart Disease**: Similar TAVR recommendations with emphasis on shared decision-making for all valve interventions. LAA occlusion for patients with non-valvular AF at increased stroke risk who have contraindications to long-term OAC (Class IIb). Comprehensive echocardiographic evaluation recommended before all valve interventions
- **ESC 2021 Heart Failure**: TEER recommended for selected patients with severe secondary MR and HF symptoms despite optimized GDMT, meeting anatomical criteria (Class IIa). Patient must have LVEF 20-50%, LVESD <= 70 mm, and EROA >= 30 mm2 or RVol >= 45 mL
- **Anticoagulation after valve intervention**: TAVR patients receive DAPT for 3-6 months or OAC if AF is present. Mechanical valve patients require lifelong warfarin. Bioprosthetic valve patients may receive short-term anticoagulation followed by aspirin
- **Endocarditis prophylaxis**: Recommended for all patients with prosthetic valves (surgical or transcatheter) undergoing dental procedures with gingival manipulation or perforation of the oral mucosa

## India Practice Lens

Structural heart disease in India presents a unique landscape shaped by rheumatic heart disease, younger patient demographics, and evolving access to transcatheter therapies:

- **Rheumatic heart disease (RHD)**: RHD remains the dominant valvular pathology in India, particularly affecting younger patients. Mitral stenosis from RHD is treated with PMBV (percutaneous mitral balloon valvuloplasty), which is well-established and widely performed across India. RHD-related valve disease in younger patients generally requires surgical repair or replacement rather than transcatheter approaches
- **Degenerative valve disease is growing**: With an aging population, degenerative aortic stenosis and mitral regurgitation are increasingly recognized. This population represents the primary candidates for TAVR and TEER
- **TAVR growth**: TAVR volumes in India have increased significantly, with approximately 2,000-3,000 procedures annually across 50+ centers. Cost remains the primary barrier (INR 15-25 lakh vs. INR 3-6 lakh for SAVR). Indigenous valve development could be transformative for accessibility
- **Bicuspid aortic valve challenge**: Bicuspid aortic valve is common in younger Indian AS patients and adds complexity to TAVR due to asymmetric calcification, larger annuli, and paravalvular leak risk. Newer-generation devices are addressing this
- **TEER is nascent**: MitraClip and PASCAL availability is limited to approximately 15-20 centers. High device costs (INR 15-25 lakh) and need for specialized imaging expertise limit adoption
- **PMBV expertise**: India has world-class expertise in PMBV, performing among the highest volumes globally. Inoue balloon technique is standard. PMBV provides excellent outcomes for suitable rheumatic MS patients
- **ASD/VSD closure**: Percutaneous device closure of atrial septal defects and select ventricular septal defects is well-established in India with Indian-made devices (Lifetech, Starway) offering cost advantages
- **Training pathways**: Structural heart fellowship programs are emerging at select Indian centers (AIIMS, Medanta, Fortis Escorts, Apollo). International training and proctorship programs supplement domestic training. SAI (Structural Heart Association of India) organizes dedicated workshops
- **Cost innovation needed**: The primary barrier to structural heart intervention adoption in India is device cost. Government insurance schemes do not typically cover TAVR or TEER. Indigenously developed devices and competitive pricing from multiple manufacturers could drive adoption
- **Paravalvular leak closure**: PVL closure after surgical valve replacement is performed at specialized centers. Indian patients with rheumatic valve disease and prior surgery represent an important population for this intervention
- **LAA occlusion in India**: Percutaneous LAA closure (Watchman, Amulet) is available at select centers but limited by cost (INR 4-6 lakh for device) and relatively low AF ablation volumes
- **Congenital heart disease in adults**: Transcatheter ASD closure, VSD closure, and PDA closure are well-established in India with high procedural volumes. Indian-made devices offer significant cost advantages
- **Future directions in India**: As device costs decrease and operator experience grows, India has the potential to become a high-volume structural heart hub. The combination of large patient populations, skilled operators, and cost-effective care delivery models positions India uniquely in this field
- **Simulation and 3D printing**: Procedural planning using 3D-printed cardiac models and simulation platforms is being adopted at leading Indian structural heart centers for complex cases including TAVR sizing, TEER planning, and paravalvular leak closure
