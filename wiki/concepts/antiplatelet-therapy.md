# Antiplatelet Therapy

Antiplatelet therapy is the cornerstone of pharmacological management after percutaneous coronary intervention and in acute coronary syndromes. Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) -- aspirin plus a P2Y12 receptor inhibitor -- prevents stent thrombosis and recurrent atherothrombotic events. The optimal duration and intensity of antiplatelet therapy require balancing ischemic protection against bleeding risk, individualized for each patient.

## Key Principles

- **DAPT is mandatory after PCI**: Aspirin (75-100 mg daily) plus a P2Y12 inhibitor for a defined duration based on clinical context, stent type, and patient risk profile. DAPT prevents stent thrombosis ([[stent-thrombosis]]) and recurrent atherothrombotic events
- **P2Y12 inhibitor selection matters**: Ticagrelor and prasugrel provide more potent and predictable platelet inhibition than clopidogrel. Preferred in ACS ([[acute-coronary-syndromes]]); clopidogrel acceptable in stable CAD. Each agent has distinct pharmacokinetic profiles, contraindications, and side effect profiles
- **Duration is risk-based, not fixed**: Default DAPT duration is 6-12 months after DES implantation, but can be shortened (1-3 months) in high bleeding risk or extended (>12 months) in high ischemic risk patients. Modern thin-strut DES allow shorter DAPT than older-generation stents
- **De-escalation strategy**: Stepping down from potent P2Y12 inhibitor to clopidogrel after 1-3 months in ACS patients reduces bleeding while maintaining ischemic protection (TWILIGHT, HOST-EXAM, TOPIC trials)
- **Aspirin dropping strategy**: Emerging evidence supports dropping aspirin after 1-3 months and continuing P2Y12 monotherapy (TWILIGHT, TICO, STOPDAPT-2, STOPDAPT-3). Reduces bleeding without increasing ischemic events
- **Bleeding risk assessment**: PRECISE-DAPT and ARC-HBR criteria identify high bleeding risk patients who benefit from shorter DAPT duration
- **Ischemic risk assessment**: DAPT score helps identify patients who may benefit from extended DAPT (>12 months)
- **Triple therapy minimization**: In patients requiring oral anticoagulation (AF, mechanical valve), minimize triple therapy duration (OAC + DAPT) to 1 week to 1 month, then OAC + single antiplatelet (clopidogrel preferred)
- **Platelet function testing**: Not recommended for routine practice but may be considered in specific clinical scenarios (stent thrombosis, suspected clopidogrel resistance)

## Drug Profiles

**Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid):**
- Mechanism: irreversible COX-1 inhibition; blocks thromboxane A2 synthesis
- Dose: 75-100 mg daily (maintenance); 300 mg loading dose
- Onset: 30-60 minutes; effect lasts for platelet lifespan (7-10 days)

**Clopidogrel:**
- Mechanism: irreversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist (prodrug requiring hepatic CYP2C19 activation)
- Dose: 300-600 mg loading; 75 mg daily maintenance
- Limitation: variable response due to CYP2C19 polymorphisms; slower onset than ticagrelor/prasugrel

**Ticagrelor:**
- Mechanism: reversible, direct-acting P2Y12 inhibitor (non-prodrug)
- Dose: 180 mg loading; 90 mg BID maintenance (60 mg BID for extended therapy beyond 1 year)
- Side effects: dyspnea (14%), ventricular pauses; requires twice-daily dosing

**Prasugrel:**
- Mechanism: irreversible P2Y12 antagonist (prodrug, but more efficient activation than clopidogrel)
- Dose: 60 mg loading; 10 mg daily (5 mg if weight <60 kg or age >75 years)
- Contraindicated: prior stroke/TIA; caution in age >75 and weight <60 kg

## Key Trials

- [[PLATO]] - Ticagrelor vs. clopidogrel in ACS: ticagrelor reduced CV death, MI, stroke with increased non-CABG bleeding
- [[TRITON-TIMI-38]] - Prasugrel vs. clopidogrel in ACS PCI: prasugrel reduced ischemic events but increased major bleeding (avoid in age >75, weight <60 kg, prior stroke/TIA)
- [[TWILIGHT]] - Ticagrelor monotherapy after 3 months DAPT in high-risk PCI: reduced bleeding without increased ischemic events
- [[HOST-EXAM]] - Clopidogrel monotherapy vs. aspirin monotherapy after DAPT: clopidogrel superior
- [[TICO]] - Ticagrelor monotherapy after 3 months vs. 12 months DAPT in ACS
- [[STOPDAPT-2]] - 1-month DAPT then clopidogrel monotherapy: non-inferior to 12-month DAPT
- [[STOPDAPT-3]] - Aspirin-free strategy with prasugrel after PCI
- [[MASTER-DAPT]] - Abbreviated DAPT (1 month) in high bleeding risk PCI patients
- [[DAPT]] - 30 months vs. 12 months DAPT: extended DAPT reduced MI and stent thrombosis but increased bleeding
- [[AUGUSTUS]] - Antithrombotic strategy in AF patients after ACS/PCI: apixaban + P2Y12 without aspirin optimal
- [[AFIRE]] - Rivaroxaban monotherapy vs. rivaroxaban + antiplatelet in AF with stable CAD

## Guideline Recommendations

- **ESC 2023 ACS Guidelines**: Ticagrelor or prasugrel preferred over clopidogrel in ACS patients undergoing PCI. Default DAPT 12 months. De-escalation to clopidogrel after 1-3 months is a Class IIa recommendation. P2Y12 monotherapy (dropping aspirin) after 1-3 months is a Class IIa recommendation
- **ESC 2024 CCS Guidelines**: DAPT 6 months after elective PCI with DES (default). Shortened DAPT (1-3 months) for high bleeding risk. Extended DAPT (>6 months) if high ischemic risk and tolerable bleeding risk. Clopidogrel is the standard P2Y12 inhibitor for stable CAD
- **ACC/AHA 2021 Guidelines**: DAPT 6-12 months after DES. Ticagrelor preferred over clopidogrel in ACS. Shortened DAPT acceptable in high bleeding risk patients
- **ESC 2020 AF + ACS/PCI**: Triple therapy for up to 1 month then OAC + clopidogrel. Avoid ticagrelor or prasugrel with OAC. Direct oral anticoagulant preferred over warfarin

## India Practice Lens

Antiplatelet therapy in India is shaped by cost considerations, medication adherence challenges, and population-specific pharmacogenomics:

- **Clopidogrel dominance**: Despite guideline recommendations for ticagrelor/prasugrel in ACS, clopidogrel remains the most commonly prescribed P2Y12 inhibitor in India due to cost (generic clopidogrel INR 2-5/day vs. ticagrelor INR 15-30/day vs. prasugrel INR 10-20/day)
- **CYP2C19 polymorphisms**: South Asian populations have a high prevalence of CYP2C19 loss-of-function alleles (15-25%), resulting in poor clopidogrel metabolism and reduced antiplatelet effect. This favors ticagrelor or prasugrel use when feasible
- **Generic medication quality**: India is a major generic pharmaceutical manufacturer. Generic clopidogrel, ticagrelor, and prasugrel are widely available. Quality and bioequivalence of generics are generally reliable from reputable manufacturers
- **Adherence challenges**: DAPT non-compliance is a major driver of stent thrombosis ([[stent-thrombosis]]) in India. Cost, polypharmacy, health literacy, and cultural factors contribute. Structured patient education at discharge and follow-up are essential
- **De-escalation adoption**: De-escalation from ticagrelor to clopidogrel after 1-3 months is an attractive strategy in India, reducing cost burden while maintaining ischemic protection during the highest-risk period
- **Aspirin monotherapy vs. clopidogrel monotherapy**: HOST-EXAM data favoring clopidogrel monotherapy is relevant for Indian practice given the CYP2C19 concern with clopidogrel in Asian populations (paradox requiring nuance)
- **Triple therapy challenges**: Managing patients with AF + ACS/PCI is complex given cost of DOACs (INR 50-150/day) and bleeding risk. Warfarin with INR monitoring is commonly used as a cost-effective alternative
- **Prasugrel advantages in India**: Fixed dosing (no CYP2C19 dependence), potent antiplatelet effect, and increasingly competitive generic pricing make prasugrel an attractive option for Indian ACS patients
- **Government price control**: NPPA has not specifically capped antiplatelet drug prices, but generic competition keeps costs manageable for most formulations
- **Point-of-care platelet function testing**: Available at select centers; may help guide therapy in patients with suspected clopidogrel resistance or recurrent ischemic events on therapy
- **Cangrelor availability**: IV P2Y12 inhibitor cangrelor is not widely available in India. Its role as a bridge in patients who cannot take oral P2Y12 inhibitors is limited to very few centers
- **Aspirin dose**: Low-dose aspirin (75 mg) is standard in India. Enteric-coated formulations are widely used, though evidence for superior GI safety over plain aspirin is limited
- **Herbal and traditional medicine interactions**: Patients may use traditional Ayurvedic or herbal preparations that can interact with antiplatelet agents. Clinicians should specifically ask about such use during medication reconciliation
- **Surgery and DAPT management**: Coordinating antiplatelet management around non-cardiac surgery is a common clinical challenge. Bridging strategies and timing of P2Y12 inhibitor discontinuation require individualized assessment
- **Elderly patients**: Indian elderly patients on DAPT require careful bleeding risk assessment. Shortened DAPT strategies (1-3 months) followed by P2Y12 monotherapy are particularly attractive in this population given the high bleeding risk and polypharmacy burden
- **Patient education materials**: Developing vernacular-language DAPT education materials and medication reminder tools can improve compliance, particularly in patients with limited health literacy
