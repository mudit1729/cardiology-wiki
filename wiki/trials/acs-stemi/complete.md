# COMPLETE

## Clinical Question

In patients with STEMI and multivessel coronary artery disease who have undergone successful primary PCI of the culprit lesion, does routine complete revascularization of angiographically significant non-culprit lesions reduce cardiovascular death or MI compared to culprit-only PCI?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 4,041 patients with STEMI and multivessel CAD (>=70% stenosis or 50-69% with FFR <=0.80 in at least one non-culprit vessel) who underwent successful culprit-vessel PCI |
| **Intervention** | Complete revascularization of all non-culprit lesions (staged or during index hospitalization) |
| **Comparator** | Culprit-lesion-only PCI (no further intervention on non-culprit lesions) |
| **Outcome** | Co-primary: (1) CV death or MI; (2) CV death, MI, or ischemia-driven revascularization |

## Result

Complete revascularization significantly reduced the co-primary endpoint of CV death or MI compared to culprit-only PCI (7.8% vs 10.5%, HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.60-0.91, p=0.004) at a median follow-up of 3 years. The broader co-primary endpoint of CV death, MI, or ischemia-driven revascularization was also reduced (8.9% vs 16.7%, HR 0.51, p<0.001). The benefit was consistent regardless of whether staged PCI was performed during index hospitalization or at a later procedure. The benefit was driven by reduction in both MI and ischemia-driven revascularization.

## What Changed?

COMPLETE established routine complete revascularization as the standard of care in STEMI patients with multivessel disease, overturning a long-standing practice of treating only the culprit lesion. It was the first trial in this setting to show a reduction in "hard" endpoints (CV death or MI), not just revascularization-driven benefits. Guidelines were updated to recommend complete revascularization (Class I, Level of Evidence A) in hemodynamically stable STEMI patients with multivessel disease.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial did NOT show a significant reduction in CV death alone (this component was individually non-significant).
- It did NOT address complete revascularization in cardiogenic shock (these patients were excluded).
- Lesion selection was angiographic (>=70%); the role of routine FFR for non-culprit lesions remained uncertain.
- Timing of staged PCI (during index hospitalization vs later admission) was left to operator discretion and not formally compared.
- It did NOT address CTO lesions as non-culprit vessels.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"When you came in with your heart attack, we opened the blocked artery that caused it. But your other arteries also have significant blockages. A large study showed that going back and opening those other blockages with stents -- rather than leaving them alone -- reduces your chances of having another heart attack in the future. We recommend treating those other blockages either during this hospital stay or in a planned follow-up procedure."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Multivessel disease prevalence**: Indian STEMI patients frequently have multivessel disease (estimated 40-60%), making COMPLETE highly relevant.
- **Staged vs index procedure**: In India, performing complete revascularization during the index hospitalization may be preferred to avoid patient loss to follow-up, which is common in underserved populations.
- **Cost implications**: Complete revascularization requires additional stents and procedures, increasing costs. However, the reduction in future MI and ischemia-driven revascularization may offset these costs long-term.
- **CABG consideration**: Some patients with complex multivessel disease may be better served by CABG rather than multivessel PCI. Heart Team decisions are important.
- **Infrastructure**: Performing complete revascularization requires cath lab availability for staged procedures, which may be limited in smaller centers.
