# DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI

## Clinical Question

In STEMI patients with multivessel disease, does FFR-guided complete revascularization reduce a composite of death, non-fatal MI, or ischemia-driven revascularization compared to culprit-only treatment?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 627 STEMI patients with multivessel disease (>=50% stenosis in >=1 non-culprit vessel) treated with primary PCI of the culprit vessel |
| **Intervention** | FFR-guided complete revascularization of non-culprit lesions (PCI if FFR <=0.80), performed during index hospitalization |
| **Comparator** | No further invasive treatment (culprit-only PCI) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, non-fatal MI, or ischemia-driven revascularization of non-culprit lesions at 27 months |

## Result

FFR-guided complete revascularization significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint compared to culprit-only PCI (13% vs 22%, HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.38-0.83, p=0.004). The benefit was driven primarily by a reduction in ischemia-driven revascularization (5% vs 17%, p<0.001). There was no significant difference in all-cause death or MI individually. Among non-culprit lesions assessed with FFR, approximately 31% had FFR >0.80 and were deferred.

## What Changed?

DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI was one of the earliest randomized trials to demonstrate the benefit of complete revascularization in STEMI, paving the way for COMPLETE. It established that FFR could be used in the acute STEMI setting to guide non-culprit treatment and that deferring hemodynamically insignificant lesions was safe. However, the benefit was primarily in reducing future ischemia-driven revascularization rather than hard endpoints.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial was relatively small (627 patients) and underpowered for mortality or MI endpoints.
- The benefit was driven by ischemia-driven revascularization, a "softer" endpoint.
- It did NOT definitively prove that FFR guidance was superior to angiographic guidance (FLOWER-MI later showed no added benefit of FFR).
- It did NOT address the optimal timing of staged procedures.
- The trial did NOT include patients with cardiogenic shock.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After treating the artery that caused your heart attack, we checked your other arteries with a special pressure measurement. This study showed that treating the other significantly blocked arteries -- rather than leaving them alone -- reduces your need for future procedures. We use the pressure measurement to decide which blockages truly need treatment and which can be safely left alone."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Historical importance**: DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI was influential in shifting Indian practice toward complete revascularization in STEMI, prior to COMPLETE.
- **FFR in STEMI**: While FLOWER-MI later showed FFR may not be necessary in STEMI, FFR remains useful for borderline lesions where the operator is uncertain.
- **Timing**: The protocol of in-hospital staged PCI aligns with Indian practice patterns where patients may not reliably return for outpatient staged procedures.
- **Soft endpoint caveat**: Indian practitioners should note that the benefit was primarily in reducing revascularization, not in reducing death or MI.
- **Generalizability**: The relatively homogeneous Danish population may differ from the Indian STEMI population in comorbidities and disease complexity.
