# FLOWER-MI

## Clinical Question

In STEMI patients with multivessel disease undergoing complete revascularization of non-culprit lesions, does FFR-guided PCI reduce death, MI, or urgent revascularization compared to angiography-guided PCI?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,171 STEMI patients with multivessel disease who had undergone successful primary PCI of the culprit vessel and were scheduled for non-culprit lesion PCI |
| **Intervention** | FFR-guided PCI of non-culprit lesions (PCI only if FFR <=0.80) |
| **Comparator** | Angiography-guided PCI of non-culprit lesions (PCI for all lesions with >=50% stenosis) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, MI, or urgent revascularization at 1 year |

## Result

FFR-guided PCI did NOT reduce the primary endpoint compared to angiography-guided PCI (5.5% vs 4.2%, HR 1.32, 95% CI 0.78-2.23, p=0.31). There were no significant differences in individual components. In the FFR group, approximately 32% of angiographically significant non-culprit lesions were deferred based on FFR >0.80. The trial was stopped early for futility. The results were surprising given the strong evidence for FFR guidance in stable disease.

## What Changed?

FLOWER-MI suggested that in the specific context of STEMI with complete revascularization, adding FFR to guide non-culprit PCI does not improve outcomes over angiographic guidance alone. This tempered enthusiasm for routine FFR use in the acute MI setting and supported a simpler, angiography-based approach to non-culprit lesions during complete revascularization. It implied that in the post-MI prothrombotic environment, angiographically significant lesions may warrant treatment regardless of FFR values.

## What Did Not Change?

- FFR guidance remains well-supported in stable CAD (FAME, FAME 2).
- The trial was stopped early and may have been underpowered for definitive conclusions.
- It did NOT prove that FFR is harmful in this setting -- just that it did not add value.
- Microvascular dysfunction post-STEMI may affect FFR accuracy in non-culprit territories.
- It did NOT address the use of iFR or other resting indices in this context.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After your heart attack, we plan to treat the other blockages in your arteries. We tested whether using a special pressure wire to measure each blockage would help us decide which ones to treat. It turns out that in this situation, simply treating all the blockages that look significant on the angiogram works just as well. So we will proceed with treating the blockages we can see rather than doing additional measurements."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Cost savings**: Not requiring FFR wires for non-culprit lesion assessment in STEMI saves INR 15,000-25,000 per wire, a significant consideration in Indian practice.
- **Simplified workflow**: Angiographic guidance for non-culprit PCI in STEMI is simpler and faster, suitable for high-volume Indian cath labs.
- **COMPLETE alignment**: The COMPLETE trial used angiographic guidance, and FLOWER-MI confirms this approach is adequate.
- **FFR availability**: Many Indian centers lack FFR capability. FLOWER-MI reassures that in STEMI patients, this is not a disadvantage.
- **Selective FFR use**: FFR should still be considered for borderline lesions in the stable disease setting.
