# EXCEL

## Clinical Question

In patients with left main coronary artery disease of low-to-intermediate anatomic complexity, is PCI with everolimus-eluting stents non-inferior to CABG for the composite of death, stroke, or MI?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,905 patients with left main CAD (>=70% visual or 50-70% with FFR <=0.80) and SYNTAX score <=32 |
| **Intervention** | PCI with everolimus-eluting stents (XIENCE) |
| **Comparator** | CABG |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, stroke, or MI at 3 years |

## Result

At 3 years, PCI was non-inferior to CABG for the primary endpoint (15.4% vs 14.7%, difference 0.7%, upper bound of 97.5% CI 4.0%, non-inferiority margin 4.2%, p=0.02 for non-inferiority). However, at 5-year follow-up, the primary endpoint was numerically higher with PCI (22.0% vs 19.2%, p=0.13), driven by higher MI and repeat revascularization rates. The trial became controversial due to differing interpretations of outcomes -- particularly around the definition of periprocedural MI and the inclusion of all-cause death versus cardiac death.

## What Changed?

EXCEL initially supported PCI as a viable alternative to CABG for left main disease with low-to-intermediate SYNTAX scores. It contributed to guideline recommendations giving PCI a Class IIa indication for left main disease with low SYNTAX scores. However, the 5-year results and subsequent controversy tempered enthusiasm and reignited debate about which endpoint definitions should drive practice.

## What Did Not Change?

- The controversy over MI definitions (universal definition vs protocol definition) was never fully resolved.
- For high SYNTAX scores (>32), CABG remains clearly superior and EXCEL did not challenge this.
- It did NOT prove PCI is superior to CABG in any subgroup.
- The all-cause death vs cardiac death debate highlighted that endpoint selection matters enormously.
- Long-term CABG benefits (particularly graft to LAD) were not fully captured at 3 years.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You have a blockage in the main artery of your heart. A large study compared stents versus bypass surgery for this. In the first three years, both treatments worked similarly. But by five years, there were some signals that bypass surgery may have an edge, with fewer heart attacks and fewer needs for repeat procedures. Both options are reasonable for you, but we need to discuss the trade-offs: stents mean a shorter recovery but potentially more procedures later, while bypass surgery is a bigger operation upfront but may be more durable."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Left main PCI growth**: Left main PCI has grown substantially in India, partly driven by EXCEL's early results. The 5-year data urge more cautious patient selection.
- **SYNTAX score application**: Careful SYNTAX score assessment is essential before offering left main PCI; many Indian centers may underutilize this scoring.
- **Heart Team discussions**: EXCEL reinforces the need for Heart Team involvement in left main decisions, which is inconsistently practiced in India.
- **Economic factors**: PCI for left main may require 2-3 stents, making it comparable in cost to CABG in India. The long-term need for repeat revascularization after PCI adds to cumulative costs.
- **Surgical expertise**: CABG outcomes are highly dependent on surgical volume and expertise. In high-volume Indian cardiac surgery centers, CABG outcomes are excellent and favor surgical approaches.
