# NOBLE

## Clinical Question

In patients with left main coronary artery disease, is PCI with biolimus-eluting stents non-inferior to CABG for major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events at 5 years?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,201 patients with significant left main stenosis (>50% by visual assessment or FFR <=0.80) |
| **Intervention** | PCI with biolimus-eluting stents (BioMatrix Flex) |
| **Comparator** | CABG |
| **Outcome** | Primary: MACCE (all-cause death, non-procedural MI, repeat revascularization, or stroke) at 5 years |

## Result

At 5 years, PCI was NOT non-inferior to CABG for MACCE (29% vs 19%, HR 1.48, 95% CI 1.11-1.96, p=0.007). The difference was driven by higher rates of non-procedural MI and repeat revascularization with PCI. All-cause mortality was similar between groups. Stroke was numerically lower with PCI but not significantly different. At 10-year follow-up, CABG maintained its advantage with lower MACCE rates.

## What Changed?

NOBLE, reported at the same time as EXCEL, reached a more definitive conclusion favoring CABG for left main disease. The contrasting results between NOBLE and EXCEL highlighted how trial design, stent type, MI definition, and follow-up duration can dramatically influence conclusions. NOBLE strengthened the position that CABG should remain the default strategy for left main disease, with PCI reserved for patients who are poor surgical candidates or have very low anatomic complexity.

## What Did Not Change?

- NOBLE used biolimus-eluting stents, which are less studied than everolimus-eluting stents, limiting direct comparison with EXCEL.
- It did NOT show a mortality difference between PCI and CABG.
- The trial did NOT include patients with high SYNTAX scores (most had low-to-intermediate complexity).
- It did NOT evaluate fractional flow reserve-guided PCI strategies.
- Technical PCI advances (better stent platforms, imaging guidance) since the enrollment period may improve modern PCI outcomes.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Another study comparing stents and bypass surgery for blockages in the main heart artery found that bypass surgery led to fewer heart attacks and fewer needs for additional procedures over five years. Both treatments had similar survival rates. This study, together with others, suggests that bypass surgery is generally the better choice for this type of blockage, though stents may be appropriate in certain situations depending on your specific anatomy and overall health."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Surgical preference reinforced**: NOBLE supports the preference for CABG in left main disease, aligning with the strength of cardiac surgical programs in India.
- **Stent type considerations**: Biolimus-eluting stents are less commonly used in India compared to everolimus-eluting stents, which may limit direct applicability.
- **Combined interpretation**: Indian cardiologists should interpret NOBLE alongside EXCEL and SYNTAX when counseling patients, recognizing the overall signal favoring CABG.
- **Patient preference**: Indian patients sometimes prefer PCI due to shorter hospital stay and faster return to work. NOBLE data can help inform these discussions with objective evidence.
- **Long-term follow-up**: The 10-year data favoring CABG are particularly relevant in younger Indian patients with left main disease who have a long life expectancy.
