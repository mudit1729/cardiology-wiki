# ORBITA-CTO

## Clinical Question

Does PCI of a chronic total occlusion (CTO) improve angina and exercise capacity compared to a sham procedure in patients with stable symptoms?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 152 patients with a chronic total occlusion of a native coronary artery, stable angina or ischemia, and viable myocardium in the CTO territory |
| **Intervention** | CTO PCI (attempted recanalization using modern CTO techniques) |
| **Comparator** | Sham procedure (diagnostic catheterization without intervention, patient blinded) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: co-primary endpoints of angina symptom score and peak VO2 on cardiopulmonary exercise testing at 12 weeks |

## Result

CTO PCI significantly improved angina symptom score compared to sham (difference 3.4 points, p=0.002). There was also a trend toward improved peak VO2, though this did not reach statistical significance in the primary analysis. CTO PCI improved wall motion on dobutamine stress echo and reduced ischemic burden. The procedural success rate for CTO PCI was approximately 85%, reflecting modern CTO techniques and experienced operators.

## What Changed?

ORBITA-CTO provided the first sham-controlled evidence that CTO PCI genuinely improves angina beyond placebo, adding to the nuanced story from the ORBITA program. This is particularly important because CTO PCI is technically demanding, carries higher complication rates, and has long been questioned for its value. The trial supports CTO PCI as a symptom-driven procedure in selected patients with refractory angina and viable myocardium in the CTO territory.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial was small and underpowered for hard clinical endpoints.
- It did NOT demonstrate a mortality or MI benefit from CTO PCI.
- Peak VO2 improvement did not reach significance, leaving exercise capacity benefit uncertain.
- It did NOT address CTO PCI in patients with well-controlled symptoms on medical therapy.
- Procedural complications of CTO PCI (perforation, radiation exposure, contrast use) remain a concern that must be weighed against symptom benefit.
- The trial used expert CTO operators; results may not generalize to lower-volume centers.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You have a completely blocked artery in your heart. A new study tested whether opening this kind of blockage with a stent actually helps with chest pain, or if the improvement people feel is just a placebo effect. They found that the procedure does genuinely reduce chest pain symptoms. However, it does not appear to prevent heart attacks or improve survival. So if your symptoms are well controlled with medications, there is no urgent need for this procedure. But if chest pain is limiting your daily life despite medications, this procedure is a reasonable option."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Operator expertise**: CTO PCI requires specialized training and high-volume experience. In India, this is concentrated at tertiary centers, and the trial's results should not be extrapolated to low-volume operators.
- **Cost considerations**: CTO PCI is among the most expensive PCI procedures due to multiple guidewires, microcatheters, and sometimes multiple stents. The symptom-only benefit must be weighed against costs of INR 3-6 lakh or more.
- **Radiation and contrast**: CTO procedures involve prolonged fluoroscopy and large contrast volumes, which is relevant in Indian patients who frequently have chronic kidney disease and diabetes.
- **Patient selection**: The trial enrolled patients with viable myocardium; viability assessment (CMR, dobutamine echo) should be performed before CTO PCI, which requires imaging infrastructure.
- **Alternative strategies**: In India, CABG may be a more cost-effective option for CTO with significant ischemic territory, especially if other vessels also need treatment.
