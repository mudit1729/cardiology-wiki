# SYNTAX

## Clinical Question

In patients with three-vessel or left main coronary artery disease, is PCI with drug-eluting stents non-inferior to CABG for major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,800 patients with de novo three-vessel or left main CAD deemed suitable for either PCI or CABG by a heart team |
| **Intervention** | PCI with TAXUS paclitaxel-eluting stents |
| **Comparator** | CABG |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, stroke, MI, or repeat revascularization (MACCE) at 12 months |

## Result

At 12 months, PCI had a significantly higher rate of MACCE compared to CABG (17.8% vs 12.4%, p=0.002), driven primarily by higher repeat revascularization in the PCI group. At 5-year follow-up, CABG remained superior overall (26.9% vs 37.3%, p<0.001). However, outcomes were highly dependent on anatomical complexity measured by the SYNTAX score: patients with low SYNTAX scores (<=22) had similar outcomes with PCI and CABG, while those with intermediate (23-32) or high (>=33) scores had significantly worse outcomes with PCI. For left main disease specifically, PCI was comparable to CABG at low-to-intermediate SYNTAX scores.

## What Changed?

SYNTAX created the foundational framework for deciding between PCI and CABG in complex coronary disease. The SYNTAX score became a standard tool for quantifying anatomic complexity and guiding revascularization strategy. It established that CABG is the default strategy for complex three-vessel disease, while PCI can be considered an alternative in anatomically simpler cases. The "Heart Team" concept of joint surgical-interventional decision-making was popularized by this trial.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial used first-generation paclitaxel-eluting stents, which have worse outcomes than modern DES.
- It did NOT use physiology-guided PCI (FFR), which could have changed lesion selection.
- The SYNTAX score does not account for clinical variables, leading to development of the SYNTAX II score.
- It did NOT include patients deemed suitable for only one strategy (the registry arm was separate).
- Long-term outcomes beyond 10 years continue to favor CABG for complex disease.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"When you have multiple blocked arteries or a blockage in the main artery of the heart, there are two options: bypass surgery or stents. This study found that for simpler patterns of blockages, both options work about equally well. But for more complex patterns, bypass surgery leads to fewer future problems like needing another procedure. Your cardiologist and surgeon will look at your specific blockage pattern using a scoring system to recommend the best option for you."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **CABG preference in complex disease**: SYNTAX strongly supports CABG for high SYNTAX-score patients, which is relevant given the high prevalence of diffuse, complex CAD in Indian patients.
- **SYNTAX score adoption**: The SYNTAX score is widely used in Indian cath labs, though its calculation requires training and standardization.
- **Heart Team approach**: Multidisciplinary Heart Team discussions are mandated by guidelines but variably implemented across Indian hospitals.
- **Cost considerations**: CABG may be more cost-effective than multivessel PCI with multiple DES in the Indian context, especially at government hospitals where surgical costs are subsidized.
- **Surgeon availability**: In some Indian regions, access to experienced cardiac surgeons is limited, which may influence the choice toward PCI despite guideline recommendations.
