# ORBITA-2

## Clinical Question

In patients with stable angina and anatomically significant coronary stenosis who are NOT on regular antianginal medications, does PCI improve angina symptom score compared to a sham procedure?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 301 patients with stable angina and at least one anatomically significant coronary stenosis (>=70% visual or FFR <=0.80), with antianginal medications withdrawn 2 weeks before randomization |
| **Intervention** | PCI with drug-eluting stent |
| **Comparator** | Sham procedure (diagnostic catheterization only, patient blinded) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: angina symptom score (Ordinal Scale of Angina) at 12 weeks |

## Result

PCI produced a statistically significant improvement in the angina symptom score compared to sham (difference 2.9 points, 95% CI 0.2-5.7, p=0.003). Freedom from angina was more common in the PCI group. However, the absolute magnitude of benefit was modest, and there was substantial overlap between groups. Importantly, this trial withdrew antianginal medications, creating a scenario where PCI's true anti-ischemic effect could be isolated from the confounding effect of background medical therapy.

## What Changed?

ORBITA-2 partially rehabilitated PCI's reputation for symptom relief by showing that when background antianginal medications are removed, PCI does provide a real (though small) benefit beyond placebo. This nuanced the message from the original ORBITA trial. The clinical implication is that PCI genuinely relieves angina but that the effect size is modest and must be weighed against procedural risks, particularly when medications can achieve similar relief.

## What Did Not Change?

- The effect size was small -- PCI is not a dramatic cure for angina symptoms.
- The trial did NOT show any benefit for hard clinical endpoints (death, MI).
- It did NOT address whether PCI adds benefit ON TOP of optimized medical therapy (that was ORBITA-1's design).
- It did NOT study multivessel disease or complex anatomy.
- The 12-week follow-up was short; durability of symptom benefit is unknown.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"The first ORBITA study suggested that stents might not help with chest pain symptoms beyond a placebo effect. So the researchers did another study, this time taking people off their heart medications first. They found that stents do provide real relief from chest pain, but the benefit is fairly small. For most patients, this means medications remain a good first option, but if your symptoms are not well controlled, a stent can provide additional, genuine relief."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Nuanced message**: Indian cardiologists can use ORBITA-2 to support PCI in patients with refractory angina while reinforcing that OMT should be tried first.
- **Medication withdrawal design**: The design of withdrawing antianginals before randomization is informative but does not reflect real clinical practice, where patients typically receive both medications and PCI.
- **Patient expectations**: Managing expectations remains critical -- PCI provides modest, not dramatic, symptom improvement.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: Given the modest effect size, the cost of PCI must be justified by the severity of symptoms in the Indian healthcare context.
- **Generic antianginals**: Low-cost generic beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers remain the most cost-effective first-line therapy for angina in India.
