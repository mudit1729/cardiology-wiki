# COURAGE

## Clinical Question

In patients with stable coronary artery disease and objective evidence of ischemia, does PCI added to optimal medical therapy (OMT) reduce the risk of death or myocardial infarction compared to OMT alone?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 2,287 patients with stable CAD with at least one coronary stenosis >=70% and objective evidence of ischemia |
| **Intervention** | PCI (predominantly bare-metal stents) plus OMT |
| **Comparator** | Optimal medical therapy alone |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death or non-fatal MI |

## Result

Over a median follow-up of 4.6 years, PCI plus OMT did not reduce the primary composite of death or MI compared to OMT alone (19.0% vs 18.5%, HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.87-1.27, p=0.62). There was no difference in individual components of death, MI, stroke, or hospitalization for ACS. PCI did provide greater freedom from angina at 1 and 3 years, but this difference narrowed by 5 years as medical therapy optimization continued.

## What Changed?

COURAGE was a watershed moment in interventional cardiology. It challenged the deeply held belief that "fixing" a blockage would prevent heart attacks. It led to a more thoughtful approach to stable CAD, emphasizing that OMT should be the foundation of treatment and that PCI should be reserved for symptom relief when medications are insufficient. It also catalyzed the concept of "appropriateness criteria" for coronary revascularization.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial did NOT use drug-eluting stents (which were emerging at the time), raising questions about generalizability to modern PCI.
- It did NOT include patients with left main disease or severely reduced LV function.
- It did NOT prove that PCI is never beneficial -- symptom relief was real.
- It did NOT address the role of complete revascularization strategies.
- The high crossover rate (~32% of OMT patients eventually underwent PCI) complicates interpretation.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"This study looked at whether opening a blocked artery with a stent helps prevent heart attacks or death in people whose heart condition is stable. It found that as long as you take the right medications -- cholesterol-lowering drugs, blood pressure medicines, blood thinners -- adding a stent does not reduce your risk of a heart attack or dying. Stents can help if you have chest pain that medications alone cannot control, but they are not a life-saving procedure for stable blockages."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Historical impact**: COURAGE remains one of the most cited trials in discussions about overuse of PCI in India, where catheterization rates have grown rapidly.
- **Stent era caveat**: The trial used mostly bare-metal stents. Modern drug-eluting stents have lower restenosis rates, but subsequent trials (ISCHEMIA) confirmed the overall message.
- **OMT adherence**: Aggressive OMT requires high-dose statins, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and lifestyle changes. Ensuring this level of therapy in Indian practice requires robust follow-up systems.
- **Cost relevance**: PCI in India costs INR 1.5-4 lakh depending on stent type and hospital. OMT is a fraction of this cost, making the trial highly relevant for resource allocation.
- **Insurance considerations**: Many Indian insurance plans cover PCI but have limited outpatient medication coverage, creating perverse incentives to intervene rather than medicate.
