# ODYSSEY OUTCOMES

## Clinical Question

In patients who have had a recent acute coronary syndrome and have LDL-C >=70 mg/dL despite intensive statin therapy, does the addition of alirocumab reduce recurrent major cardiovascular events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 18,924 patients with ACS (STEMI, NSTEMI, or unstable angina) within the preceding 1-12 months, on atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg with LDL-C >=70 mg/dL |
| **Intervention** | Alirocumab 75 mg SC every 2 weeks (titrated to 150 mg to achieve LDL-C 25-50 mg/dL) |
| **Comparator** | Placebo injection plus statin |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of coronary heart disease death, non-fatal MI, ischemic stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina |

## Result

Alirocumab significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint (9.5% vs 11.1%, HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78-0.93, p=0.0003) at a median follow-up of 2.8 years. Unlike FOURIER, ODYSSEY OUTCOMES showed a significant reduction in all-cause mortality in a pre-specified analysis of patients with baseline LDL-C >=100 mg/dL (mortality HR 0.71, p=0.01, overall mortality HR 0.85, p=0.026 nominal). LDL-C was lowered from 87 mg/dL to 38 mg/dL at 12 months. The dose-titration strategy successfully maintained LDL-C in the 25-50 mg/dL target range.

## What Changed?

ODYSSEY OUTCOMES provided the strongest evidence for PCSK9 inhibitor efficacy in the highest-risk population (recent ACS) and was the first PCSK9i trial to show a mortality signal, particularly in patients with the highest baseline LDL-C. The dose-titration approach was practical and demonstrated that targeting a specific LDL-C range (25-50 mg/dL) is achievable. It reinforced the concept that the sooner and lower LDL-C is reduced after ACS, the greater the benefit.

## What Did Not Change?

- The overall mortality result was nominally significant but not part of the hierarchical testing procedure, so must be interpreted cautiously.
- Cost remained a barrier to widespread adoption.
- The trial did NOT compare alirocumab to evolocumab head-to-head.
- Longer follow-up would be needed to confirm the mortality benefit.
- It did NOT address patients with stable ASCVD (FOURIER addressed that population).
- The manufacturer (Sanofi/Regeneron) subsequently reduced commercial availability of alirocumab in some markets.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After your heart attack, your cholesterol needs to be as low as possible to prevent another one. Despite your statin, your bad cholesterol is still above target. Adding an injectable medication called alirocumab, given every two weeks, can cut your cholesterol further and reduce your risk of another heart attack or stroke by about 15%. In patients with the highest cholesterol levels, there was even a suggestion of improved survival. This medication is especially important in the first few years after a heart attack when your risk is highest."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Post-ACS population**: India has a very high burden of ACS with young patients. Aggressive LDL-C lowering post-ACS is critically important.
- **Alirocumab availability**: Alirocumab has had variable availability in India. Evolocumab may be more consistently accessible.
- **Dose titration model**: The titration strategy used in ODYSSEY (starting at 75 mg, titrating to 150 mg based on LDL-C response) is practical and resource-efficient.
- **Baseline LDL-C targeting**: Patients with LDL-C >=100 mg/dL despite statins benefit the most, which is common in Indian ACS patients who are often on suboptimal statin doses.
- **Alternative approaches**: For cost-constrained patients, maximizing statin dose and adding ezetimibe should be prioritized before PCSK9 inhibitor consideration.
- **Inclisiran potential**: The emerging availability of inclisiran in India (twice-yearly injection) may provide a more practical alternative for Indian patients.
