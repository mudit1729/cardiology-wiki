# FOURIER

## Clinical Question

In patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease already on statin therapy, does the addition of evolocumab (a PCSK9 inhibitor) reduce major cardiovascular events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 27,564 patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (prior MI, prior stroke, or symptomatic PAD) on optimized statin therapy with LDL-C >=70 mg/dL or non-HDL-C >=100 mg/dL |
| **Intervention** | Evolocumab 140 mg SC every 2 weeks or 420 mg SC monthly (patient choice) plus statin |
| **Comparator** | Placebo injection plus statin |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of CV death, MI, stroke, hospitalization for unstable angina, or coronary revascularization |

## Result

Evolocumab reduced the primary composite endpoint by 15% (9.8% vs 11.3%, HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.79-0.92, p<0.001) at a median follow-up of 2.2 years. The key secondary endpoint (CV death, MI, or stroke) was reduced by 20% (5.9% vs 7.4%, HR 0.80, p<0.001). Median LDL-C was lowered from 92 mg/dL to 30 mg/dL (a 59% reduction). There was no increase in adverse events including neurocognitive effects, diabetes, or cancer. The benefit increased with time -- by year 3, there was a 25% reduction in MI and 21% reduction in stroke.

## What Changed?

FOURIER established PCSK9 inhibitors as a new class of CV risk-reducing therapies and validated the "lower is better" hypothesis for LDL-C to levels previously considered unachievable. It demonstrated that LDL-C levels of 20-30 mg/dL are safe and beneficial. PCSK9 inhibitors received guideline endorsement for patients with ASCVD not at goal on maximally tolerated statin therapy.

## What Did Not Change?

- There was NO reduction in cardiovascular death, which was puzzling and possibly related to short follow-up duration.
- The relatively short median follow-up (2.2 years) limited the ability to detect mortality benefits, which lipid-lowering trials typically show only after 3-5 years.
- Cost-effectiveness was a major concern at the original list price (~$14,000/year USD). Subsequent price reductions have improved this.
- The trial did NOT address primary prevention or patients without established ASCVD.
- It did NOT compare PCSK9 inhibitors to ezetimibe as add-on therapy.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You already take a statin to lower your cholesterol, but your levels are still not where they need to be. A new injectable medication called evolocumab, given as a shot under the skin every two weeks, can dramatically lower your bad cholesterol to very low levels. A study of nearly 28,000 patients showed this reduces your risk of heart attacks and strokes by about 20%. The very low cholesterol levels achieved are safe. The main barrier has been cost, but this is improving with time."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **PCSK9i access**: Evolocumab is available in India at approximately INR 10,000-15,000 per injection (bi-weekly), making it expensive but accessible for affluent patients.
- **ASCVD burden**: India has an enormous burden of premature ASCVD. FOURIER supports aggressive LDL lowering in high-risk patients, which is particularly relevant.
- **Statin optimization first**: Many Indian patients are on suboptimal statin doses. Maximizing statin therapy and adding ezetimibe should precede PCSK9i consideration.
- **Inclisiran alternative**: Inclisiran (siRNA-based PCSK9 lowering, twice-yearly injection) is emerging as a more affordable alternative in India.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: At current Indian prices, PCSK9 inhibitors may be cost-effective for very high-risk patients (recurrent events on maximal therapy) but not for broad use.
- **Familial hypercholesterolemia**: PCSK9 inhibitors are particularly valuable for Indian patients with FH, where statins alone are often insufficient.
