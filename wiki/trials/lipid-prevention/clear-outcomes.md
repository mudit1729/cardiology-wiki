# CLEAR Outcomes

## Clinical Question

In patients with or at high risk for cardiovascular disease who are unable or unwilling to take statins, does bempedoic acid reduce major adverse cardiovascular events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 13,970 patients with established CVD or at high CV risk who were statin-intolerant (unable to take statins due to adverse effects) with LDL-C 100-190 mg/dL |
| **Intervention** | Bempedoic acid 180 mg daily (oral) |
| **Comparator** | Placebo |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of CV death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke, or coronary revascularization (4-point MACE) |

## Result

Bempedoic acid significantly reduced the primary 4-point MACE endpoint (11.7% vs 13.3%, HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.79-0.96, p=0.004) at a median follow-up of 40.6 months. LDL-C was reduced by 21% from a median baseline of 139 mg/dL. The benefit was driven primarily by reductions in MI (HR 0.77) and coronary revascularization (HR 0.81). There was no significant reduction in CV death or stroke individually. Adverse effects included higher rates of gout, cholelithiasis, and elevated uric acid/hepatic enzymes.

## What Changed?

CLEAR Outcomes provided the first evidence that an oral, non-statin LDL-lowering therapy reduces cardiovascular events when used as the primary lipid-lowering agent. This was critically important for the statin-intolerant population, who previously had limited oral options. Bempedoic acid works as a prodrug activated only in the liver (not in skeletal muscle), explaining the absence of myalgias. Guidelines now recommend bempedoic acid as an option for patients who cannot tolerate statins.

## What Did Not Change?

- The LDL-C reduction with bempedoic acid (21%) is modest compared to statins (30-50%) or PCSK9 inhibitors (50-60%).
- It did NOT prove a mortality benefit.
- The statin-intolerant population may be partly driven by nocebo effect -- true pharmacological statin intolerance is less common than reported.
- It did NOT compare bempedoic acid to ezetimibe head-to-head.
- The combination of bempedoic acid + ezetimibe (available as a fixed-dose combination) was not specifically tested against PCSK9 inhibitors.
- Gout and gallstone risk need to be considered in patient selection.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"If you have not been able to take statins because of muscle pain or other side effects, there is now an oral pill called bempedoic acid that lowers your cholesterol in a different way. It works in the liver but not in muscles, so it does not cause the muscle problems that statins can. A large study showed it reduces your risk of heart attacks and the need for heart procedures. The cholesterol-lowering effect is more modest than statins, but for someone who cannot take statins, it provides meaningful protection."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Statin intolerance management**: Statin intolerance is commonly reported in India, though true pharmacological intolerance should be distinguished from nocebo effects through rechallenge protocols.
- **Bempedoic acid availability**: Bempedoic acid is becoming available in India, with the fixed-dose combination (bempedoic acid + ezetimibe) offering a convenient non-statin option.
- **Cost**: Bempedoic acid costs INR 30-60/day in India, making it more expensive than generic statins but more affordable than PCSK9 inhibitors.
- **Gout risk**: Hyperuricemia and gout are common in Indian patients. Bempedoic acid's uric acid-raising effect is clinically relevant and requires monitoring.
- **Sequential approach**: For Indian patients with true statin intolerance, a practical approach is: ezetimibe first, then add bempedoic acid, then consider PCSK9 inhibitor if LDL-C remains above goal.
