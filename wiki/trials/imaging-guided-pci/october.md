# OCTOBER

## Clinical Question

Does OCT-guided PCI of coronary bifurcation lesions reduce major adverse cardiac events compared to angiography-guided PCI?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,201 patients undergoing PCI of coronary bifurcation lesions involving a side branch >=2.5 mm |
| **Intervention** | OCT-guided PCI with systematic assessment of stent apposition, expansion, edge dissection, and side branch compromise |
| **Comparator** | Angiography-guided PCI |
| **Outcome** | Primary: MACE (cardiac death, target lesion MI, or ischemia-driven target lesion revascularization) at 2 years |

## Result

OCT-guided PCI significantly reduced MACE at 2 years compared to angiography-guided PCI (10.1% vs 14.1%, HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.50-0.98, p=0.035). The benefit was driven primarily by fewer target-lesion-related MIs and target lesion revascularizations. OCT guidance led to more frequent post-dilation, additional stenting, and side-branch interventions based on OCT findings not apparent on angiography. This was the first trial to demonstrate a clinical benefit of OCT guidance in a specific lesion subset.

## What Changed?

OCTOBER was the first positive randomized trial for OCT-guided PCI, establishing that in bifurcation lesions specifically, OCT guidance translates into improved clinical outcomes. It shifted the paradigm from "OCT provides better procedural results but unclear clinical benefit" to "OCT improves outcomes in the right lesion context." Guidelines now more strongly recommend intravascular imaging for bifurcation PCI.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial was specific to bifurcation lesions; generalizability to all PCI is uncertain.
- It did NOT compare OCT to IVUS for bifurcation PCI.
- The absolute event reduction was modest (4% over 2 years), and the number needed to treat is relatively high.
- It did NOT address the learning curve -- experienced OCT operators may have contributed to the benefit.
- Cost-effectiveness of routine OCT for all bifurcations was not established.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Your blockage is at a point where two arteries branch apart, which makes stent placement more complex. A study showed that using a special camera inside the artery during this type of procedure helps the doctor see problems that X-rays might miss, leading to fewer heart problems afterward. For this reason, we recommend using this camera during your procedure to ensure the best possible result."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Bifurcation PCI frequency**: Bifurcation lesions are common in Indian practice. OCTOBER supports OCT use specifically for these cases.
- **Targeted use**: Rather than routine OCT for all PCI, Indian centers can adopt a targeted strategy using OCT primarily for bifurcation lesions, maximizing benefit while controlling costs.
- **Training priority**: Bifurcation-specific OCT interpretation should be a training priority for Indian interventional fellows.
- **Cost justification**: The clinical benefit in bifurcations may justify OCT cost to insurance providers and hospital administrators.
- **Single-stent vs two-stent**: OCT is particularly helpful in deciding whether a side branch needs stenting, which has significant cost implications.
