# ULTIMATE

## Clinical Question

Does IVUS-guided DES implantation reduce target vessel failure (TVF) compared to angiography-guided DES implantation in patients undergoing PCI?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,448 patients undergoing PCI with second-generation DES for all-comer coronary lesions |
| **Intervention** | IVUS-guided PCI with predefined IVUS optimization criteria (minimum stent area, apposition, edge dissection assessment) |
| **Comparator** | Angiography-guided PCI (standard fluoroscopic and angiographic optimization) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: target vessel failure (TVF) -- composite of cardiac death, target vessel MI, or clinically driven TVR at 12 months |

## Result

IVUS-guided PCI significantly reduced TVF at 12 months compared to angiography-guided PCI (2.9% vs 5.4%, HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31-0.90, p=0.019). The benefit was maintained at 3-year follow-up (TVF 6.6% vs 10.7%, p=0.01). IVUS guidance led to larger post-PCI minimum stent area, fewer edge dissections, and more appropriate stent sizing. The benefit was particularly pronounced in complex lesions (long lesions, bifurcations, and small vessels).

## What Changed?

ULTIMATE provided strong evidence that IVUS-guided PCI produces superior outcomes compared to angiography alone. It supported guideline upgrades for intravascular imaging use during PCI, particularly for complex lesions. The trial helped define specific IVUS optimization endpoints (minimum stent area >5.0 mm2 or >80% of distal reference lumen area) that operators should target.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial did NOT compare IVUS to OCT (head-to-head imaging comparison was not performed).
- It did NOT address whether IVUS guidance is necessary for simple, straightforward lesions.
- The benefit was driven by a composite endpoint; individual hard endpoints (cardiac death) were not significantly different.
- Cost-effectiveness of routine IVUS use was not formally assessed.
- Operator experience with IVUS interpretation varied and could influence outcomes.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"When we put in a stent, we usually guide the procedure using X-ray images. But there is a tiny camera we can put inside your artery -- called IVUS -- that gives us a much more detailed view. A study showed that using this camera during stent placement leads to better results, with fewer problems related to the stent over time. We especially recommend it for complex blockages where precise stent placement is critical."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **IVUS availability**: IVUS is available in most major Indian cardiac centers but is underutilized due to cost (INR 15,000-25,000 per catheter) and time constraints.
- **Complex lesions**: Indian patients often present with complex, calcified, diffuse disease where IVUS guidance is most beneficial.
- **Training needs**: Widespread IVUS adoption requires structured training programs for fellows and practicing interventional cardiologists.
- **Cost-benefit**: The reduction in TVF and need for repeat procedures could offset the upfront IVUS cost, especially in complex lesions.
- **Reimbursement**: Insurance coverage for IVUS is inconsistent in India, creating a barrier to adoption.
