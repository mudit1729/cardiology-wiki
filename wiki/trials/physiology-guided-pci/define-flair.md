# DEFINE-FLAIR

## Clinical Question

Is iFR non-inferior to FFR for guiding coronary revascularization decisions in terms of major adverse cardiac events at 1 year?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 2,492 patients with coronary stenosis in whom the interventional cardiologist was uncertain about functional significance and deemed physiological assessment appropriate |
| **Intervention** | iFR-guided revascularization (PCI/CABG if iFR <=0.89, defer if iFR >0.89) |
| **Comparator** | FFR-guided revascularization (PCI/CABG if FFR <=0.80, defer if FFR >0.80) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, non-fatal MI, or unplanned revascularization at 1 year |

## Result

iFR-guided revascularization was non-inferior to FFR-guided revascularization for the primary endpoint at 1 year (6.8% vs 7.0%, difference -0.2%, 95% CI -2.3% to 1.8%, p=0.007 for non-inferiority). Individual components including death, MI, and unplanned revascularization were similar between groups. iFR-guided procedures were shorter (reduced procedure time by ~5 minutes) because iFR does not require adenosine administration. Patient discomfort was also reduced with iFR due to avoidance of adenosine-related symptoms.

## What Changed?

DEFINE-FLAIR, along with the simultaneously published iFR-SWEDEHEART trial, established iFR as a validated alternative to FFR for guiding coronary revascularization. This was significant because iFR does not require adenosine (saving cost, time, and avoiding adenosine-related symptoms) and can be measured during the same catheterization setup. Guidelines were updated to recommend both FFR and iFR as equivalent options for physiology-guided PCI (Class I, Level of Evidence A).

## What Did Not Change?

- One year of follow-up may not capture late differences between iFR and FFR strategies.
- The trial did NOT establish superiority of iFR -- only non-inferiority.
- Agreement between iFR and FFR is approximately 80%; for the 20% of discordant cases, it remains unclear which index is more accurate.
- The trial did NOT specifically address ACS patients or specific lesion subsets (tandem lesions, left main, diffuse disease).
- It did NOT compare either index to intravascular imaging guidance.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"To measure whether your blockage is limiting blood flow, we have two options. One uses a drug called adenosine to stress your heart during the measurement (FFR), and the other measures blood flow at rest without any drug (iFR). A large study showed both methods give equally reliable results for deciding whether you need a stent. The resting measurement is quicker and avoids the uncomfortable sensations some people feel with adenosine."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Adenosine logistics**: Adenosine availability and cost are challenges in some Indian centers. iFR eliminates this requirement.
- **Procedure efficiency**: In high-volume Indian cath labs, the time saved by avoiding adenosine administration is clinically meaningful.
- **Cost comparison**: iFR and FFR use the same pressure wire; the cost difference is primarily in adenosine. In India, IV adenosine costs INR 500-1500 per dose.
- **Adoption barrier**: Both iFR and FFR require pressure wire expertise. The fundamental barrier in India is physiology adoption overall, not the choice between indices.
- **Hybrid approach**: Some Indian centers use iFR as the first-line resting index and FFR only for borderline iFR values (0.86-0.93), a practical hybrid approach.
