# iFR-SWEDEHEART

## Clinical Question

Is iFR-guided revascularization non-inferior to FFR-guided revascularization for major adverse cardiac events at 1 year in patients with stable angina or acute coronary syndromes?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 2,037 patients with stable angina or non-ST elevation ACS in whom the operator judged physiological assessment of a coronary stenosis to be indicated (registry-based randomization from SWEDEHEART) |
| **Intervention** | iFR-guided revascularization (PCI if iFR <=0.89, defer if iFR >0.89) |
| **Comparator** | FFR-guided revascularization (PCI if FFR <=0.80, defer if FFR >0.80) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, non-fatal MI, or unplanned revascularization at 1 year |

## Result

iFR-guided revascularization was non-inferior to FFR-guided revascularization for the primary endpoint (6.7% vs 6.1%, difference 0.7%, 95% CI -1.5% to 2.8%, p=0.007 for non-inferiority). There were no significant differences in any individual component. The revascularization rate was similar in both groups (approximately 50%). Unlike DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART included a substantial proportion of ACS patients (approximately 25%), extending the non-inferiority finding to this population. The registry-based randomized design leveraged the SWEDEHEART infrastructure for efficient enrollment and complete follow-up.

## What Changed?

Together with DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART provided a robust evidence base for iFR as a guideline-recommended alternative to FFR. The inclusion of ACS patients was a unique contribution, as FFR validation in ACS has been questioned due to potential effects of microvascular dysfunction on hyperemic measurements. The registry-randomized design showcased an innovative trial methodology later adopted by other Scandinavian trials.

## What Did Not Change?

- The 1-year follow-up was relatively short for establishing long-term equivalence.
- It did NOT resolve the question of discordant iFR/FFR cases -- which index is correct when they disagree.
- The trial did NOT include STEMI patients during the acute phase.
- It did NOT compare physiology-guided PCI to imaging-guided PCI (IVUS/OCT).
- The SWEDEHEART population may not fully represent global practice patterns in terms of lesion complexity and patient demographics.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Two separate studies, including this one from Sweden, confirmed that measuring blood flow in your arteries at rest is just as reliable as using a drug to stress the measurement. Both methods help us make the same quality of decisions about whether to place a stent. The resting measurement is simpler and faster, which is an advantage for both you and the medical team."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **ACS inclusion**: The inclusion of ACS patients is relevant for Indian practice, where a large proportion of catheterizations are performed in the acute setting.
- **Registry-based trials**: The innovative design could inspire similar efforts using Indian registries (e.g., Kerala ACS Registry, CREATE) for efficient randomized trials.
- **Practical adoption**: iFR's adenosine-free protocol is particularly suited to Indian cath labs where procedure throughput is high and adenosine logistics can be cumbersome.
- **Combined evidence**: The simultaneous publication with DEFINE-FLAIR provided a pooled sample of >4,500 patients, strengthening the evidence base beyond what either trial alone could provide.
- **Guideline alignment**: Indian guidelines (CSI, ICMR) can confidently recommend either iFR or FFR based on this combined evidence.
