# EMPEROR-Preserved

## Clinical Question

In patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction (LVEF >40%), does empagliflozin reduce the composite of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 5,988 patients with NYHA class II-IV HF, LVEF >40%, elevated NT-proBNP, with or without diabetes |
| **Intervention** | Empagliflozin 10 mg daily |
| **Comparator** | Placebo |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of CV death or hospitalization for HF |

## Result

Empagliflozin significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint (13.8% vs 17.1%, HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69-0.90, p<0.001) at a median follow-up of 26.2 months. The benefit was driven by a 29% reduction in HF hospitalization (HR 0.71, p<0.001). CV death was not significantly reduced (HR 0.91, p=0.43). Benefits were seen across the LVEF spectrum (>40% to >60%), though the effect appeared attenuated at the highest LVEF values. Renal function decline was slower with empagliflozin.

## What Changed?

EMPEROR-Preserved was the first large randomized trial to demonstrate a significant clinical benefit of any therapy in HFpEF -- a condition that has been called the "graveyard of clinical trials" because every prior therapy had failed. This was a historic moment in heart failure medicine. It established SGLT2 inhibitors as the first evidence-based therapy for HFpEF and changed the clinical nihilism that had surrounded this condition for decades.

## What Did Not Change?

- CV death was NOT reduced, limiting the benefit to HF hospitalization reduction.
- The mechanism of benefit in HFpEF is even less clear than in HFrEF (volume effects, metabolic effects, anti-inflammatory effects).
- HFpEF remains a heterogeneous syndrome -- which specific phenotypes benefit most from SGLT2 inhibitors is not fully resolved.
- Other HFpEF-targeted therapies (ARAs, ARNIs) have shown inconsistent results; SGLT2 inhibitors are the only clearly positive class.
- It did NOT compare empagliflozin to dapagliflozin in HFpEF (DELIVER later provided dapagliflozin data).
- Patients with HFpEF and specific etiologies (amyloidosis, HCM, constrictive pericarditis) were underrepresented.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Your heart pumps normally but is stiff and does not fill properly, a condition called heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. For years, we did not have any proven medication for this condition. A breakthrough study showed that empagliflozin -- a medication that was originally for diabetes -- reduces your risk of being hospitalized for heart failure by about 29%. This is the first time any medication has been shown to clearly help this type of heart failure. We recommend adding it to your current medications."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **HFpEF prevalence**: HFpEF is increasingly recognized in India, particularly in elderly, hypertensive, and diabetic patients. It may account for 40-50% of HF hospitalizations.
- **Diagnostic challenges**: HFpEF diagnosis requires echocardiography and NT-proBNP, which are not universally available in India. Improving diagnostic capacity is essential to apply EMPEROR-Preserved findings.
- **Therapeutic gap**: Prior to SGLT2 inhibitors, Indian HFpEF management was limited to diuretics, hypertension control, and fluid restriction. Now there is a disease-modifying therapy.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: At INR 8-20/day for generic empagliflozin, the cost per HF hospitalization prevented is likely favorable.
- **Comorbidity management**: SGLT2 inhibitors simultaneously address diabetes, CKD, and HFpEF -- a common triad in Indian patients.
- **Obesity phenotype**: Indian HFpEF patients often have metabolic syndrome. Combining SGLT2 inhibitors with GLP-1RAs (if affordable) could be synergistic.
