# STRONG-HF

## Clinical Question

In patients recently hospitalized for acute heart failure, does rapid up-titration of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) to full doses within 2 weeks of discharge, guided by close monitoring, reduce death or HF readmission compared to usual care?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,078 patients hospitalized for acute HF (any LVEF), not on full doses of GDMT, with NT-proBNP >1,500 pg/mL that decreased by >10% before discharge |
| **Intervention** | High-intensity care: rapid up-titration of ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, and MRA to full recommended doses within 2 weeks of discharge, with close follow-up visits at 1, 2, 3, and 6 weeks |
| **Comparator** | Usual care: discharge with existing medications, follow-up per local practice |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death or HF readmission at 180 days |

## Result

The trial was stopped early for efficacy. Rapid up-titration significantly reduced the primary endpoint (15.2% vs 23.3%, adjusted RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.50-0.86, p=0.0021). Both components were reduced: HF readmission (11.4% vs 17.2%) and all-cause death (5.7% vs 8.2%). In the high-intensity group, 55% of patients reached full GDMT doses by 2 weeks. NT-proBNP decreased significantly more with rapid up-titration. Quality of life improved substantially more in the high-intensity arm. Safety signals (hypotension, hyperkalemia, worsening renal function) were manageable with close monitoring.

## What Changed?

STRONG-HF addressed one of the most critical gaps in HF care: the implementation gap. Despite multiple trials proving the benefit of GDMT, real-world data consistently show that patients are discharged on suboptimal doses and rarely up-titrated. STRONG-HF proved that rapid, aggressive up-titration to full doses is both safe and markedly beneficial when paired with close monitoring. It shifted the focus from "which drug" to "how fast and how fully" HF medications should be prescribed.

## What Did Not Change?

- The high-intensity protocol required 4-6 follow-up visits within 6 weeks, which is resource-intensive and may be difficult to implement in all settings.
- It did NOT include SGLT2 inhibitors in the GDMT protocol (the trial was designed before SGLT2i were guideline-recommended for HF).
- The trial was stopped early, which may have inflated the effect size.
- It did NOT address patients with very low blood pressure or advanced CKD who may not tolerate rapid up-titration.
- The usual care arm may have been particularly undertreated, magnifying the difference.
- Long-term outcomes beyond 6 months are unknown.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"When you leave the hospital after a heart failure episode, the doses of your heart medications are often lower than what has been proven to work best. This study showed that if we increase your medications to their full recommended doses quickly -- within two weeks of discharge -- and see you frequently to make sure you are tolerating them well, you are much less likely to be readmitted to the hospital or die in the next six months. It requires more frequent visits initially, but the payoff is significant."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Implementation gap**: Indian HF patients are frequently discharged on suboptimal GDMT doses, with limited outpatient follow-up for titration. STRONG-HF highlights the urgency of addressing this gap.
- **Follow-up infrastructure**: The 4-6 visit schedule within 6 weeks is challenging in Indian settings. Telemedicine, nurse-led titration clinics, and pharmacist-managed protocols could help implement this approach.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: All GDMT medications used in STRONG-HF are available as affordable generics in India. The intervention is primarily about care delivery, not medication cost.
- **HF clinics**: STRONG-HF supports the development of dedicated HF clinics in Indian hospitals, with protocolized up-titration and close monitoring.
- **NTproBNP-guided therapy**: NT-proBNP monitoring (used for enrollment and response assessment) requires point-of-care assay availability, which is growing in India.
- **SGLT2 inhibitor integration**: Modern implementation of STRONG-HF principles in India should include SGLT2 inhibitors as part of the rapid up-titration protocol.
