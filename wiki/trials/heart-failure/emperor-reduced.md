# EMPEROR-Reduced

## Clinical Question

In patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction (LVEF <=40%), does empagliflozin reduce the composite of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure, regardless of diabetes status?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 3,730 patients with NYHA class II-IV HF, LVEF <=40%, elevated NT-proBNP (thresholds varied by LVEF and AF status), on background GDMT -- approximately 50% without diabetes |
| **Intervention** | Empagliflozin 10 mg daily |
| **Comparator** | Placebo |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of CV death or hospitalization for HF |

## Result

Empagliflozin significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint (19.4% vs 24.7%, HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.65-0.86, p<0.001) at a median follow-up of 16 months. This was driven primarily by a 30% reduction in HF hospitalization (HR 0.70, p<0.001). CV death was not significantly reduced individually (HR 0.92, p=0.23). The eGFR decline was slower with empagliflozin (-0.55 vs -2.28 mL/min/1.73m2/year, p<0.001), demonstrating renal protection. Benefits were consistent regardless of diabetes status, baseline LVEF, and use of sacubitril/valsartan.

## What Changed?

EMPEROR-Reduced confirmed the class effect of SGLT2 inhibitors in HFrEF, complementing DAPA-HF. Together, these trials established SGLT2 inhibitors as a foundational HFrEF therapy. EMPEROR-Reduced enrolled a sicker population (lower median LVEF of 27%, more patients on sacubitril/valsartan) than DAPA-HF, extending the evidence to patients with more advanced disease. The renal protection data were particularly notable and added another dimension to the benefit profile.

## What Did Not Change?

- Unlike DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced did NOT show a significant reduction in CV death alone, though the trial was not specifically powered for this endpoint.
- It did NOT determine whether empagliflozin and dapagliflozin are interchangeable (no head-to-head data).
- The mechanism of benefit remains under investigation.
- Patients with eGFR <20 mL/min/1.73m2 were excluded.
- The trial did NOT compare SGLT2 inhibitors to other HF therapies as add-on options.
- Very severe HF (INTERMACS profiles 1-3, inotrope-dependent) was not well represented.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"We have a second medication in the same class as dapagliflozin -- called empagliflozin -- that has also been proven to help weak hearts. In a study of nearly 4,000 patients, it reduced the chance of being hospitalized for heart failure by 30%. It also helped protect your kidneys, which is important because heart failure and kidney disease often go together. Like dapagliflozin, it works whether or not you have diabetes."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Class effect confirmation**: Indian practitioners can choose either dapagliflozin or empagliflozin based on availability and cost, confident that both have robust HFrEF evidence.
- **Empagliflozin cost**: Generic empagliflozin costs INR 8-20/day in India, comparable to dapagliflozin.
- **Sicker patients**: EMPEROR-Reduced enrolled sicker patients than DAPA-HF, which is relevant for Indian HF patients who often present late with advanced disease.
- **Renal protection**: The eGFR preservation is particularly valuable in India where diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy are common comorbidities of HF.
- **ARNI combination**: The trial showed consistent benefit in patients on sacubitril/valsartan, supporting the use of all four pillars simultaneously.
- **Treatment inertia**: Despite strong evidence, SGLT2 inhibitor prescription in Indian HF patients remains suboptimal, reflecting treatment inertia.
