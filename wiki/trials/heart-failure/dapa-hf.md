# DAPA-HF

## Clinical Question

In patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), does dapagliflozin reduce the composite of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death, regardless of the presence of diabetes?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 4,744 patients with NYHA class II-IV HF, LVEF <=40%, elevated NT-proBNP, on optimized GDMT (ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA) -- approximately 55% without diabetes |
| **Intervention** | Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily |
| **Comparator** | Placebo |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of worsening HF (hospitalization or urgent visit requiring IV therapy) or CV death |

## Result

Dapagliflozin significantly reduced the primary endpoint (16.3% vs 21.2%, HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.65-0.85, p<0.001) at a median follow-up of 18.2 months. Both components were reduced: worsening HF (HR 0.70) and CV death (HR 0.82). All-cause mortality was also reduced (HR 0.83, p=0.022). Critically, the benefit was consistent regardless of diabetes status, establishing SGLT2 inhibitors as a heart failure therapy rather than merely a diabetes drug. Quality of life (KCCQ) improved significantly with dapagliflozin.

## What Changed?

DAPA-HF was a paradigm-shifting trial that added SGLT2 inhibitors as the fourth pillar of HFrEF therapy (alongside ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blockers, and MRAs). The finding that benefit was independent of diabetes status was revolutionary and transformed SGLT2 inhibitors from diabetes drugs into cardiovascular drugs. Guidelines rapidly incorporated dapagliflozin as a Class I recommendation for HFrEF. The number needed to treat (NNT) of approximately 21 at 18 months was compelling.

## What Did Not Change?

- The mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitor benefit in HF remains incompletely understood (likely multifactorial: natriuresis, hemodynamics, metabolic substrate shifting, anti-inflammatory effects).
- The trial required background GDMT; SGLT2i should be added to, not substituted for, foundational HF therapies.
- It did NOT address HFpEF (EMPEROR-Preserved and DELIVER addressed this later).
- It did NOT compare dapagliflozin to empagliflozin head-to-head.
- The trial did not include patients with very severe HF (NYHA class IV was underrepresented).
- eGFR down to 30 mL/min/1.73m2 was permitted; benefit in more severe CKD is less clear.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Your heart muscle is weak and is not pumping blood as well as it should. We have found a medication called dapagliflozin -- originally developed for diabetes -- that significantly helps the heart even in people without diabetes. A large study showed it reduces the chance of heart failure getting worse or of dying from heart problems by about 26%. It works alongside the other heart failure medications you are already taking. It is a pill taken once a day, and the main things to watch for are urinary tract infections and, rarely, low blood sugar if you also have diabetes."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **HF burden**: India has an estimated 8-10 million patients with heart failure. HFrEF is the predominant phenotype, making DAPA-HF directly relevant to millions.
- **Dapagliflozin cost**: Generic dapagliflozin costs INR 5-15/day in India, making it one of the most cost-effective HF therapies globally.
- **GDMT optimization gap**: Many Indian HF patients are on suboptimal GDMT. Adding SGLT2i should complement, not replace, efforts to optimize ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blockers, and MRAs.
- **Diabetes prevalence**: India has a high prevalence of diabetes-associated HF. While DAPA-HF showed benefit regardless of diabetes, the overlap between diabetes and HF makes SGLT2 inhibitors particularly efficient in Indian practice (treating both conditions).
- **Renal protection**: The renal protective effects of SGLT2 inhibitors are a bonus in Indian HF patients, many of whom have concomitant CKD.
- **Formulary adoption**: Major Indian hospitals and government health programs are increasingly including SGLT2 inhibitors in their HF formularies.
