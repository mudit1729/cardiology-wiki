# PARTNER 3

## Clinical Question

In patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis at low surgical risk, is TAVR with a balloon-expandable valve (SAPIEN 3) non-inferior or superior to surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) for death, stroke, or rehospitalization at 1 year?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,000 patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis and low surgical risk (STS-PROM <4%, mean 1.9%) |
| **Intervention** | Transfemoral TAVR with SAPIEN 3 balloon-expandable valve |
| **Comparator** | SAVR (surgical aortic valve replacement via sternotomy or minimally invasive approach) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, stroke, or rehospitalization at 1 year |

## Result

TAVR was superior to SAVR for the primary endpoint at 1 year (8.5% vs 15.1%, HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.37-0.79, p=0.001). TAVR had lower rates of stroke (0.6% vs 2.4%), death (1.0% vs 2.5%), and rehospitalization (7.3% vs 11.0%), though individual components were not all individually significant. TAVR patients had shorter hospital stays, faster recovery, and better quality of life at 30 days. At 5-year follow-up, outcomes were broadly similar between groups, with no significant difference in the composite endpoint, though new pacemaker and moderate-or-greater aortic regurgitation rates were higher in the TAVR group.

## What Changed?

PARTNER 3 was transformative in extending TAVR indications from high/intermediate surgical risk patients to low-risk patients, who represent the majority of AS patients. The 1-year results were widely interpreted as making TAVR the preferred therapy for most patients with severe AS, regardless of surgical risk. However, the 5-year data tempered this enthusiasm, showing convergence of outcomes and raising questions about TAVR valve durability and the importance of long-term follow-up.

## What Did Not Change?

- Long-term valve durability (beyond 10 years) remains unknown for TAVR, while SAVR bioprosthetic valves have 15-20 year track records.
- Pacemaker rates remained higher with TAVR, especially with self-expanding valves.
- Paravalvular regurgitation, while improved with SAPIEN 3, is still more common after TAVR than SAVR.
- TAVR-in-TAVR (valve-in-valve) for structural valve degeneration has uncertain long-term outcomes.
- The trial excluded bicuspid aortic valve patients (up to 50% of AS in younger patients), limiting applicability.
- Patients under 65 were underrepresented; TAVR durability concerns are greatest in younger patients.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You have a narrowed heart valve that needs to be replaced. In the past, this required open-heart surgery. Now we have a less invasive option -- putting in a new valve through an artery in your groin. A large study in patients like you (at low surgical risk) showed that the catheter-based approach had fewer complications in the first year and a faster recovery. However, we are still learning about how long these catheter-based valves last compared to surgical valves, which is important if you are younger. We will discuss which option is best for your specific situation."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **TAVR growth in India**: TAVR programs are expanding rapidly across major Indian cardiac centers, with both SAPIEN and self-expanding valve platforms available.
- **Cost barrier**: TAVR in India costs INR 15-30 lakh (device + procedure), compared to INR 3-6 lakh for SAVR. This limits TAVR primarily to patients who are poor surgical candidates or choose to self-pay.
- **Young patients**: Indian AS patients may present younger than Western cohorts. Valve durability concerns are paramount, and SAVR may remain preferred for patients under 65.
- **Bicuspid valve disease**: Bicuspid aortic valve is common in India and was excluded from PARTNER 3. Off-label TAVR in bicuspid anatomy requires careful case selection.
- **Operator experience**: TAVR outcomes are highly volume-dependent. Concentration of TAVR in high-volume centers is important for Indian programs.
- **Rheumatic AS**: Rheumatic AS (with mitral involvement) is a distinct Indian challenge; TAVR may not be ideal for combined rheumatic valve disease.
