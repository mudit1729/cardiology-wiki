# DEDICATE

## Clinical Question

In patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis at intermediate surgical risk, is TAVR with a self-expanding valve non-inferior to SAVR for death or disabling stroke at 1 year?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 1,414 patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis and intermediate surgical risk (mean STS-PROM 2.9%) |
| **Intervention** | Transfemoral TAVR with self-expanding valve (predominantly Evolut R/PRO/PRO+) |
| **Comparator** | SAVR (surgical aortic valve replacement) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death or disabling stroke at 1 year |

## Result

TAVR was non-inferior to SAVR for the primary endpoint of death or disabling stroke at 1 year (3.3% vs 4.4%, difference -1.1%, upper 95% CI 0.8%, non-inferiority margin 4.0%, p<0.001 for non-inferiority). The trial also tested for superiority, which was not met (p=0.16). New pacemaker implantation was significantly higher with TAVR (19.0% vs 6.3%), and moderate-or-greater paravalvular regurgitation was more common with TAVR. SAVR patients had more atrial fibrillation, bleeding, and acute kidney injury.

## What Changed?

DEDICATE provided key self-expanding valve data in the intermediate-risk population, complementing the PARTNER series (balloon-expandable) and SURTAVI (self-expanding, intermediate risk). It confirmed that self-expanding TAVR is non-inferior to SAVR for the most critical endpoints. However, the high pacemaker rate remained a concern specific to self-expanding valves and highlighted the importance of valve platform selection.

## What Did Not Change?

- Pacemaker rates with self-expanding valves remain significantly higher than SAVR, which is a real clinical disadvantage.
- Long-term valve durability data are pending.
- The trial did NOT achieve superiority of TAVR over SAVR.
- Paravalvular regurgitation rates, while improved with newer iterations, remain higher than SAVR.
- The choice between balloon-expandable and self-expanding platforms was not directly addressed (no head-to-head comparison).
- Bicuspid aortic valve patients were excluded.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You need your aortic valve replaced, and your surgical risk is moderate. This study compared a catheter-based valve replacement (using a self-expanding valve) with traditional open-heart surgery. At one year, both approaches had similar rates of death and disabling stroke. The catheter approach means a shorter recovery, but it carries a higher chance of needing a permanent pacemaker -- about 1 in 5 patients, compared to 1 in 16 with surgery. We will discuss which approach is best for you, considering your anatomy and preferences."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Self-expanding valve availability**: Both self-expanding (Medtronic Evolut) and balloon-expandable (Edwards SAPIEN) platforms are available in India.
- **Pacemaker considerations**: The high pacemaker rate is particularly relevant in India where pacemaker access, follow-up, and battery replacement costs are significant long-term concerns.
- **Cost comparison**: Self-expanding and balloon-expandable TAVR valves have similar costs in India (INR 12-20 lakh for the device alone). SAVR remains substantially cheaper.
- **Anatomic selection**: Valve selection should account for annulus size, calcification pattern, and risk of conduction disturbance. Larger Indian centers should offer both platforms.
- **Intermediate risk population**: This is the most common risk stratum for Indian AS patients presenting for intervention, making DEDICATE directly relevant.
