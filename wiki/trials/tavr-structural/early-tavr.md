# EARLY TAVR

## Clinical Question

In patients with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis, does early TAVR reduce death or stroke compared to clinical surveillance (watchful waiting with intervention only when symptoms or guideline-based indications develop)?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 901 patients with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (aortic valve area <1.0 cm2 and mean gradient >=40 mmHg or peak velocity >=4.0 m/s) |
| **Intervention** | Early TAVR (within 60 days of randomization) |
| **Comparator** | Clinical surveillance with guideline-directed management (intervention only if symptoms develop or other indications emerge) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, stroke, or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization at 2-4 years |

## Result

Early TAVR significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint compared to clinical surveillance (26.8% vs 45.3%, HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.40-0.63, p<0.001). The benefit was driven by reductions in all components: death (8.4% vs 9.2%), stroke (4.2% vs 6.7%), and unplanned CV hospitalization (20.9% vs 41.7%). The most dramatic difference was in unplanned hospitalizations, reflecting the unpredictable nature of symptom onset in asymptomatic AS. In the surveillance arm, approximately 70% of patients eventually required AVR during follow-up.

## What Changed?

EARLY TAVR fundamentally challenged the long-standing paradigm of watchful waiting in asymptomatic severe AS. For decades, guidelines recommended intervention only after symptom onset, based on SAVR-era risk-benefit calculations. With the low procedural risk of modern TAVR, early intervention became favorable. This trial supports a shift toward proactive treatment of asymptomatic severe AS with TAVR, recognizing that waiting for symptoms exposes patients to risk of sudden deterioration, heart failure hospitalization, and AF development.

## What Did Not Change?

- Valve durability concerns remain -- treating asymptomatic patients earlier means they need their valve to last longer.
- The trial follow-up was 2-4 years; longer-term data are needed to confirm sustained benefit.
- It did NOT address whether early SAVR has similar benefits (the trial was TAVR-specific).
- Patients with very early severe AS (newly diagnosed, young age) may still warrant initial surveillance.
- The trial did NOT resolve the role of biomarkers (BNP), exercise testing, or imaging markers in risk-stratifying asymptomatic patients.
- Economic analyses of early TAVR vs surveillance are still pending.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Your heart valve is very narrow, but you feel fine right now. Traditionally, we would wait until you develop symptoms before replacing the valve. However, a new study showed that replacing the valve now -- using a minimally invasive catheter-based approach -- leads to fewer hospitalizations, strokes, and potentially better survival compared to waiting. The main risk of waiting is that symptoms can come on suddenly and unpredictably. We recommend discussing early valve replacement, understanding that the long-term durability of these valves is still being studied."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Late presentation**: Many Indian patients present with symptomatic severe AS and would not have been eligible for this trial. EARLY TAVR is most relevant for patients detected early through screening or incidental echocardiography.
- **Screening opportunity**: As echocardiography becomes more accessible in India, more asymptomatic severe AS will be detected. EARLY TAVR informs management of these patients.
- **Cost and access**: Early TAVR implies treating more patients overall (those who would have been monitored). The cost implications are substantial in the Indian context where TAVR access is limited.
- **SAVR alternative**: For asymptomatic patients who are good surgical candidates, early SAVR may achieve similar benefits at lower cost. EARLY TAVR data may be partially extrapolated to early intervention in general.
- **Exercise testing**: Many Indian centers use exercise stress echocardiography to unmask symptoms in "asymptomatic" patients. EARLY TAVR suggests this step may be bypassed in favor of early intervention.
