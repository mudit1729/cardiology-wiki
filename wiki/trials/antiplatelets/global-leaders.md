# GLOBAL LEADERS

## Clinical Question

Is an experimental antiplatelet strategy of 1 month of DAPT (ticagrelor + aspirin) followed by 23 months of ticagrelor monotherapy superior to the standard strategy of 12 months of DAPT (aspirin + ticagrelor/clopidogrel) followed by 12 months of aspirin monotherapy?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 15,991 all-comer PCI patients (47% ACS, 53% stable CAD) across 130 sites |
| **Intervention** | Experimental: ticagrelor + aspirin for 1 month, then ticagrelor monotherapy for 23 months |
| **Comparator** | Standard: aspirin + ticagrelor (ACS) or aspirin + clopidogrel (stable CAD) for 12 months, then aspirin monotherapy for 12 months |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death or new Q-wave MI at 2 years |

## Result

The experimental strategy did NOT achieve superiority over the standard strategy for the primary endpoint (3.81% vs 4.37%, RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.75-1.01, p=0.073). While the point estimate favored the experimental arm, the result was not statistically significant. Major or minor bleeding (BARC 3 or 5) was numerically lower in the experimental arm but also not significant. Subgroup analyses suggested a possible benefit in ACS patients and in those with higher ischemic risk.

## What Changed?

GLOBAL LEADERS was the largest trial testing ticagrelor monotherapy but technically failed to meet its primary endpoint. However, it generated important hypothesis-generating data and contributed to the growing body of evidence supporting P2Y12 monotherapy strategies. The trial paved the way for TWILIGHT (which used a different design and succeeded). It also demonstrated the feasibility and safety of early aspirin discontinuation after PCI in a very large, all-comer population.

## What Did Not Change?

- The primary endpoint of all-cause death or Q-wave MI was arguably too stringent -- a less conservative endpoint might have been positive.
- The differential P2Y12 inhibitor use in the control arm (ticagrelor for ACS, clopidogrel for stable CAD) complicated interpretation.
- It did NOT prove superiority of ticagrelor monotherapy -- the trial was formally negative.
- The 2-year follow-up combined with the complex treatment regimen led to significant non-adherence.
- Cost of prolonged ticagrelor use was not addressed.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"A very large study tried to test a new approach: using just one strong blood thinner (ticagrelor) after the first month, instead of two blood thinners for a full year. While the study showed a trend toward fewer deaths and heart attacks, the difference was not large enough to be certain. Other studies since then have refined this approach and shown more clearly that dropping aspirin after a short period is safe and reduces bleeding."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Ticagrelor cost barrier**: Prolonged ticagrelor monotherapy (23 months in GLOBAL LEADERS) is prohibitively expensive for many Indian patients compared to clopidogrel or aspirin.
- **All-comer design**: The inclusion of both ACS and stable CAD patients mirrors Indian practice patterns and makes the data broadly applicable in concept.
- **Negative trial lessons**: GLOBAL LEADERS demonstrates how endpoint selection and trial design can influence outcomes, relevant for Indian clinical trial design.
- **Transition to TWILIGHT**: The failure of GLOBAL LEADERS led to the more focused TWILIGHT trial, which succeeded with a refined design.
- **Generic ticagrelor**: As generic ticagrelor becomes more affordable in India, the concept of ticagrelor monotherapy may become more practical.
