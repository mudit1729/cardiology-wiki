# HOST-EXAM

## Clinical Question

After completion of 6-18 months of DAPT following PCI with DES, is clopidogrel monotherapy superior to aspirin monotherapy for net adverse clinical events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 5,438 patients in South Korea who had completed 6-18 months of event-free DAPT after PCI with DES |
| **Intervention** | Clopidogrel 75 mg daily monotherapy |
| **Comparator** | Aspirin 100 mg daily monotherapy |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of all-cause death, non-fatal MI, stroke, readmission for ACS, or BARC 3-5 bleeding at 24 months |

## Result

Clopidogrel monotherapy was superior to aspirin monotherapy for the primary net adverse clinical event endpoint (5.7% vs 7.7%, HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.59-0.90, p=0.003). The benefit was driven by reductions in both ischemic events (MI, stroke, ACS readmission) and bleeding (BARC 3-5). Individual components all favored clopidogrel numerically, though not all reached significance individually. At 5-year follow-up, clopidogrel maintained its superiority over aspirin.

## What Changed?

HOST-EXAM was the first large randomized trial to directly compare clopidogrel vs aspirin as long-term monotherapy after DAPT completion. It challenged the century-old default of aspirin as the maintenance antiplatelet agent after PCI and provided evidence that clopidogrel is the better single agent. This aligned with the emerging understanding from TWILIGHT and other trials that P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy is preferable to aspirin monotherapy.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial was conducted exclusively in Korean patients; applicability to other ethnic groups requires validation.
- It did NOT compare clopidogrel to ticagrelor or prasugrel as monotherapy.
- CYP2C19 genotyping was not part of the randomization or analysis, despite being a critical determinant of clopidogrel efficacy.
- It did NOT address patients still within the DAPT period -- this was a post-DAPT maintenance question.
- The generalizability to non-East Asian populations, where the bleeding/thrombosis balance differs, is uncertain.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After your stent procedure, you took two blood thinners for several months. Now that you are transitioning to just one blood thinner for the long term, a study found that clopidogrel is better than aspirin for this purpose. Patients who continued on clopidogrel had fewer heart attacks, strokes, and bleeding events compared to those who switched to aspirin. So we recommend continuing clopidogrel as your long-term blood thinner."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Clopidogrel cost advantage**: Clopidogrel is extremely affordable in India (INR 3-5/day) and widely available as a generic, making this strategy highly practical.
- **CYP2C19 concern**: Up to 30-40% of South Asians carry CYP2C19 loss-of-function alleles, which may reduce clopidogrel efficacy. Genotype-guided therapy or alternative agents should be considered in suspected poor metabolizers.
- **Aspirin displacement**: HOST-EXAM supports the shift away from aspirin as the default post-PCI maintenance agent in India, though this represents a significant cultural change in prescribing habits.
- **Korean to Indian extrapolation**: While East Asian bleeding profiles differ from South Asian, the direction of benefit (clopidogrel over aspirin) is biologically plausible across populations.
- **Generic quality**: The efficacy of clopidogrel monotherapy depends on bioequivalent generic formulations, which should be sourced from reputable manufacturers.
