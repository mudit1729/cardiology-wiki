# TWILIGHT

## Clinical Question

In high-risk patients who have undergone PCI and completed 3 months of DAPT with aspirin plus ticagrelor without events, does ticagrelor monotherapy (dropping aspirin) reduce clinically relevant bleeding compared to continued DAPT?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 7,119 high-risk patients who underwent successful PCI and completed 3 months of event-free DAPT (aspirin + ticagrelor) -- high risk defined by clinical features (ACS, diabetes, CKD, age >=65) or procedural complexity (multivessel PCI, bifurcation, long stent, etc.) |
| **Intervention** | Ticagrelor 90 mg BID monotherapy (aspirin discontinued) after 3 months of DAPT |
| **Comparator** | Ticagrelor 90 mg BID plus aspirin 81-100 mg (continued DAPT) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: BARC type 2, 3, or 5 bleeding from 3 to 15 months |

## Result

Ticagrelor monotherapy significantly reduced clinically relevant bleeding compared to continued DAPT (4.0% vs 7.1%, HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.45-0.68, p<0.001). There was no increase in ischemic events -- the composite of all-cause death, MI, or stroke was similar (3.9% vs 3.9%, HR 0.99). Definite or probable stent thrombosis was not increased. The bleeding reduction was consistent across all subgroups, including ACS patients, diabetic patients, and those with complex PCI.

## What Changed?

TWILIGHT established the concept of P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy as a viable de-escalation strategy after a brief period of DAPT. Dropping aspirin (rather than the P2Y12 inhibitor) reduced bleeding without sacrificing ischemic protection. This challenged the decades-long primacy of aspirin in post-PCI care and launched the "aspirin-free" antiplatelet era. Guidelines incorporated shortened DAPT with P2Y12 monotherapy as a recommended option for bleeding reduction.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial enrolled only patients who were event-free at 3 months -- this is a selected, lower-risk-within-high-risk population.
- It did NOT address the initial 3-month DAPT period (aspirin was maintained during this phase).
- It did NOT compare ticagrelor monotherapy to clopidogrel monotherapy.
- Patients on oral anticoagulation (AF patients) were excluded.
- The cost of ticagrelor relative to clopidogrel limits generalizability in cost-sensitive settings.
- It did NOT test aspirin monotherapy vs ticagrelor monotherapy.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After your stent was placed, you have been taking two blood-thinning medications for three months -- aspirin and ticagrelor. Now that three months have passed without problems, we can stop the aspirin and continue with just ticagrelor. A large study showed that this reduces your risk of significant bleeding by nearly half without increasing your risk of heart attack or stroke. The ticagrelor alone provides sufficient protection for your stent."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Bleeding reduction priority**: Indian patients, who may have undiagnosed peptic ulcer disease or use NSAIDs, stand to benefit significantly from reduced bleeding risk.
- **Ticagrelor cost**: Ticagrelor is more expensive than clopidogrel in India (INR 15-25/day vs INR 3-5/day). This limits the applicability of the TWILIGHT strategy to patients who can afford ticagrelor.
- **Clopidogrel extrapolation**: Many Indian cardiologists extrapolate the concept to clopidogrel monotherapy (dropping aspirin, continuing clopidogrel), supported by HOST-EXAM data.
- **Generic ticagrelor**: Generic ticagrelor availability in India has improved affordability, making the TWILIGHT strategy more accessible.
- **Compliance considerations**: Simplifying to a single antiplatelet may improve medication adherence in the Indian context.
