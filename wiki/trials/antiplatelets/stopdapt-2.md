# STOPDAPT-2

## Clinical Question

In patients undergoing PCI with cobalt-chromium everolimus-eluting stents, is 1 month of DAPT followed by clopidogrel monotherapy superior to 12 months of standard DAPT for net adverse clinical events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 3,045 patients undergoing PCI with cobalt-chromium everolimus-eluting stents (XIENCE) in Japan, including both stable CAD and ACS presentations |
| **Intervention** | 1 month of DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) followed by clopidogrel monotherapy |
| **Comparator** | 12 months of standard DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) followed by aspirin monotherapy |
| **Outcome** | Primary: composite of CV death, MI, definite stent thrombosis, stroke, or TIMI major/minor bleeding at 12 months |

## Result

One-month DAPT followed by clopidogrel monotherapy was superior to 12-month standard DAPT for the primary net clinical benefit endpoint (2.4% vs 3.7%, HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.42-0.98, p=0.04 for superiority). This was driven primarily by a reduction in bleeding events. Ischemic endpoints were numerically similar. However, in a subsequent subgroup analysis of ACS patients specifically (STOPDAPT-2 ACS), the abbreviated strategy showed numerically higher ischemic events, raising caution.

## What Changed?

STOPDAPT-2 was one of the first trials to demonstrate superiority of abbreviated DAPT with clopidogrel monotherapy over standard DAPT. It suggested that with modern, thin-strut everolimus-eluting stents, ultra-short DAPT could be both safe and beneficial by reducing bleeding. The trial was particularly influential in East Asian practice, where bleeding risk is a greater concern. It supported the emerging paradigm of clopidogrel monotherapy (rather than aspirin) as the preferred long-term single antiplatelet agent.

## What Did Not Change?

- The STOPDAPT-2 ACS substudy raised concerns about applying ultra-short DAPT to ACS patients, leading to more cautious recommendations.
- The trial was conducted exclusively in Japanese patients, who may have different bleeding/thrombotic risk profiles than other populations.
- It did NOT compare clopidogrel monotherapy to ticagrelor monotherapy.
- Cobalt-chromium EES were used exclusively; applicability to other stent platforms requires extrapolation.
- It did NOT address patients with complex PCI (long stent length, bifurcation, etc.) as a specific subgroup.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"After your stent procedure, the standard practice has been to take two blood thinners for a year. A study from Japan showed that with the modern stents we use today, switching to just one blood thinner -- clopidogrel -- after just one month is actually better overall because it significantly reduces your risk of bleeding while keeping your stent protected. However, if you came in with a heart attack, we might keep the two medicines on a bit longer."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Clopidogrel access**: Clopidogrel is the most commonly used P2Y12 inhibitor in India due to cost (INR 3-5/day). STOPDAPT-2 supports clopidogrel monotherapy as a first-line post-DAPT strategy.
- **Genetic variation**: CYP2C19 loss-of-function polymorphisms (affecting clopidogrel metabolism) are common in South Asian populations. Clopidogrel monotherapy may be less effective in poor metabolizers.
- **ACS caution**: The ACS substudy data mandate caution in applying ultra-short DAPT to Indian ACS patients, who often present late with higher thrombus burden.
- **Bleeding risk**: GI bleeding from NSAIDs, H. pylori, and iron deficiency anemia is common in India, making bleeding reduction strategies particularly valuable.
- **East Asian data**: Bleeding risk profiles in Japanese patients differ from South Asian patients; direct applicability requires consideration of local risk factors.
