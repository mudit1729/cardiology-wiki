# MASTER DAPT

## Clinical Question

In high-bleeding-risk patients who have undergone PCI with a biodegradable polymer-coated DES, is abbreviated DAPT (1 month) followed by single antiplatelet therapy non-inferior to standard/prolonged DAPT for net adverse clinical events?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 4,579 patients at high bleeding risk (defined by ARC-HBR criteria) who underwent PCI with Ultimaster biodegradable polymer sirolimus-eluting stent and completed 1 month of event-free DAPT |
| **Intervention** | Abbreviated DAPT: discontinuation of one antiplatelet agent at 1 month (operator's choice of which agent to stop) |
| **Comparator** | Standard or prolonged DAPT (at least 2 additional months for stable patients, at least 5 additional months for ACS patients) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: net adverse clinical events (NACE) -- composite of all-cause death, MI, stroke, or BARC 3 or 5 bleeding at 335 days |

## Result

Abbreviated DAPT was non-inferior to standard/prolonged DAPT for NACE (7.5% vs 7.7%, HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.78-1.20, p<0.001 for non-inferiority). Abbreviated DAPT significantly reduced BARC 3 or 5 major bleeding (1.1% vs 1.8%, HR 0.58, p=0.03). There was no significant increase in ischemic events (death, MI, stroke, or definite/probable stent thrombosis) with abbreviated DAPT. Results were consistent in both ACS and stable CAD subgroups.

## What Changed?

MASTER DAPT provided high-quality evidence that in HBR patients, 1 month of DAPT followed by single antiplatelet therapy is safe and reduces major bleeding. It validated the use of ARC-HBR criteria for identifying patients who benefit from abbreviated DAPT. Guidelines incorporated 1-month DAPT as a recommended strategy for HBR patients, particularly when used with modern polymer-coated DES.

## What Did Not Change?

- The trial used a specific biodegradable polymer stent; applicability to all DES platforms is assumed but not proven.
- The choice of which antiplatelet to continue (aspirin or P2Y12 inhibitor) was left to the operator, limiting insight into the optimal single agent.
- It did NOT test DAPT durations shorter than 1 month.
- HBR patients are heterogeneous; some bleeding risk factors may warrant even shorter DAPT while others may tolerate standard duration.
- It did NOT address patients on concomitant oral anticoagulation.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"Because you have a higher risk of bleeding -- due to your age, kidney function, or other factors -- we want to minimize the time you are on two blood thinners. A large study showed that just one month of dual blood thinners after your stent, followed by a single blood thinner, is safe and reduces your bleeding risk compared to staying on two blood thinners for longer. The stent we used is designed for this shorter treatment period."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **HBR prevalence**: Many Indian PCI patients qualify as HBR due to advanced age, CKD, anemia, or concomitant NSAID use, making MASTER DAPT widely applicable.
- **Stent availability**: Biodegradable polymer DES are available in India. The results may reasonably extend to other modern DES platforms.
- **Cost savings**: Shorter DAPT duration reduces medication costs and bleeding-related hospitalizations.
- **Anemia considerations**: Iron deficiency anemia is highly prevalent in India. Abbreviated DAPT reduces the risk of GI bleeding that could worsen anemia.
- **Operator choice flexibility**: The trial allowed operator choice of single agent, which aligns with the practical, individualized approach common in Indian practice.
