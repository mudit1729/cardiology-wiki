# ARISTOTLE

## Clinical Question

In patients with atrial fibrillation and at least one risk factor for stroke, is apixaban superior to warfarin for the prevention of stroke or systemic embolism while reducing major bleeding?

## PICO

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Population** | 18,201 patients with atrial fibrillation (or flutter) and at least one additional risk factor for stroke (prior stroke/TIA, age >=75, heart failure, diabetes, or hypertension) |
| **Intervention** | Apixaban 5 mg BID (or 2.5 mg BID if >=2 of: age >=80, weight <=60 kg, creatinine >=1.5 mg/dL) |
| **Comparator** | Dose-adjusted warfarin (target INR 2.0-3.0) |
| **Outcome** | Primary: stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic) or systemic embolism |

## Result

Apixaban was superior to warfarin for the primary endpoint of stroke or systemic embolism (1.27% vs 1.60% per year, HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66-0.95, p=0.01 for superiority). Apixaban also significantly reduced major bleeding (2.13% vs 3.09% per year, HR 0.69, p<0.001) and all-cause mortality (3.52% vs 3.94% per year, HR 0.89, p=0.047). It achieved the rare trifecta of better efficacy, less bleeding, and improved survival compared to warfarin. Intracranial hemorrhage was reduced by 58% with apixaban.

## What Changed?

ARISTOTLE established apixaban as the preferred oral anticoagulant for atrial fibrillation, based on its unique combination of superior stroke prevention, reduced major bleeding, and lower mortality compared to warfarin. It was the only DOAC trial to demonstrate superiority on all three of these critical endpoints. ARISTOTLE fundamentally shifted global AF management from warfarin to DOACs and positioned apixaban at the top of the DOAC hierarchy for many clinicians and guideline writers.

## What Did Not Change?

- Patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <25 mL/min) were excluded; safety and efficacy in advanced CKD remain uncertain.
- Patients with mechanical heart valves were excluded; DOACs remain contraindicated in this population.
- The trial did NOT address AF patients undergoing PCI (this was later addressed by AUGUSTUS).
- TTR (time in therapeutic range) for the warfarin arm was 62.2%, which is reasonable but below the best achievable. In centers with excellent TTR, the advantage of apixaban may be smaller.
- The reduced dose criteria (2.5 mg BID) have been applied variably in practice, with concerns about both over- and under-dosing.

## How I Would Explain This to a Patient

"You have atrial fibrillation, which puts you at risk for stroke. The traditional treatment was warfarin, which works but requires regular blood tests and has many interactions with food and medications. A large study of over 18,000 patients showed that apixaban is better at preventing strokes, causes less bleeding, and is associated with a lower risk of death compared to warfarin. It also does not require regular blood monitoring. For these reasons, we recommend apixaban for you."

## Relevance for Indian Practice

- **Warfarin dominance**: Warfarin remains the most commonly used anticoagulant in India due to cost (INR 2-5/day vs INR 30-50/day for apixaban). ARISTOTLE provides the strongest evidence for transitioning when affordable.
- **INR monitoring challenges**: INR monitoring is inconsistent in rural India due to limited lab access and follow-up. Apixaban eliminates this requirement.
- **Dose reduction in India**: Many Indian AF patients meet dose-reduction criteria (low body weight, older age, renal impairment). The 2.5 mg BID dose must be applied correctly using ARISTOTLE criteria, not arbitrarily.
- **Generic apixaban**: Generic apixaban availability in India has significantly reduced costs, making it more accessible.
- **Rheumatic heart disease**: India has a high burden of rheumatic valvular AF, for which warfarin remains the standard. ARISTOTLE applies only to non-valvular AF.
- **Hemorrhagic stroke reduction**: The 58% reduction in intracranial hemorrhage is particularly important in India where neurosurgical access for hemorrhagic stroke is limited.
